# Catholic Church Reference

## Status and authority

- **Scope:** the Catholic Church's internal state, diplomacy, papal succession, councils, missions, political relationships, and institutional development from 1941 through 1985.
- **Canonical source:** the Catholic Church in 1941 branch archived in [transcript.md](./transcript.md), chiefly Turns 460–551, together with the accepted Latin-bloc chronology.
- **Related references:** [Setting Bible](./settingBible%20-%20AltWWII.md), [Setting Timeline](./settingTimeline%20-%20AltWWII.md), and [SS Order Faith Reference](./ssReligionReference%20-%20AltWWII.md).
- **Authority rule:** direct user corrections control over earlier summaries. In particular, the papal sequence and council described here supersede the older John XXIII–historical Paul VI treatment.
- **Fiction notice:** the four clandestine “infiltration” currents below are elements of this alternate setting. They are not asserted as factual descriptions of the real Catholic Church.

## Governing summary

The alternate Church does not escape crisis because communism collapses and several Catholic confessional states survive. The pressure changes.

The central twentieth-century problem becomes the preservation of a universal, supernatural, and apostolic Church in a world dominated by triumphant national states, racial empires, Catholic authoritarian governments, colonial missions, mass communications, and rival political religions.

The Church is neither a liberal opposition movement nor the chaplaincy of the Latin bloc. It possesses genuine friends in Italy, Spain, Portugal, Occitania, America, and the Eastern churches, but repeatedly has to distinguish cooperation from capture.

Its decisive alternate developments are:

- Pius XII's preparation for an Axis-dominated postwar Europe;
- the **1946 consecration of Russia** and the unexplained Orthodox revival that follows;
- the 1958 election of Armenian cardinal **Gregorio Pietro Agagianian as Gregory XVII**;
- the **Council of Christ the King**, 1962–1965;
- the 1971 election of Croatian cardinal **Franjo Šeper as Paul VI**;
- the rise of Fulton Sheen as cardinal, missionary interpreter, and American bridge;
- and the January 1982 election of **Giuseppe Siri as Pius XIII**.

## The Church at the divergence

### Roman government

Pius XII governs a highly centralized Church whose doctrinal, diplomatic, and administrative offices remain strong but whose information is uneven. The Holy See is experienced in concordats, minority churches, authoritarian states, and diplomacy without reliable military backing.

The Roman theological establishment remains doctrinally conservative. New biblical, patristic, liturgical, and social scholarship exists, but it is contained inside inherited dogma and discipline rather than forming a broad project of accommodation to liberal modernity.

### Liturgy and devotion

The Roman Rite remains Latin and structurally traditional. The liturgical movement seeks better preaching, chant, participation, Holy Week observance, and knowledge of the rites. It does not yet imply construction of a new order of Mass.

Marian devotion, the Sacred Heart, Christ the King, Eucharistic devotion, Catholic Action, schools, hospitals, youth organizations, religious orders, and missionary societies give the Church a thick public and social presence.

### External position

- **Germany:** the concordat survives as a legal defense while the regime narrows Catholic public life, controls education, and treats Rome as a rival transnational authority.
- **Italy:** the Lateran settlement protects Vatican sovereignty while Fascist Italy surrounds and pressures it.
- **Poland and the East:** war, displacement, occupation, and martyrdom threaten clergy and diocesan continuity.
- **Spain and Portugal:** the Church enjoys political privilege but risks national subordination.
- **France:** northern and southern Catholic cultures are about to diverge radically.
- **Britain and America:** the Church is a growing minority with strong institutions and persistent suspicion toward papal political influence.
- **Missions:** European imperial structures still carry many missions, creating both access and danger of identification with colonial government.

## Fatima and the supernatural frame

Fatima is authorially real in the setting. The Third Secret retains its historical wording because the later memoir records the 1917 revelation rather than a new post-divergence message.

Pius XII performs a specific **consecration of Russia in 1946**. The act is not broadly communicated inside the Reichskommissariats. Nevertheless, Orthodox Christianity begins an unexpected revival across Russian lands and parts of Caucasia.

The authorial meaning is not that Orthodoxy becomes Latin or that the schism has ceased to matter. Catholicism and Orthodoxy are two wounded branches sharing apostolic roots, sacraments, and a history that Providence has not abandoned.

In-world actors cannot prove the causal relationship. The Vatican interprets the revival as a sign requiring serious Eastern attention, and the 1958 conclave treats it as one reason an Eastern cardinal may be providentially suited to the papacy. Catholic theology continues to teach that the Holy Spirit guides a conclave without selecting the only possible or perfect candidate.

## The four clandestine currents

The setting contains four decentralized currents that use a “Vendita” method of long-term personnel placement and indirect institutional influence. None possesses formal state sponsorship after 1947. They are related by method more than by a unified command.

### Communist-derived current

The collapse of the Soviet Union removes funding, direction, and confidence. Survivors migrate toward Britain, the United States, northern France, and parts of Latin America. They emphasize class politics, secularized social action, and a weak proto-liberation theology, but never reproduce the scale or discipline that Soviet support could have provided.

### German nationalizing current

This current survives best locally. It seeks a Catholicism interpreted through nation, race, obedience, and German historical vocation. It is careful not to praise Hitler or the SS so openly that Rome or German anti-Masonic institutions can identify it.

By 1980 it has captured or influenced many German diocesan bureaucracies and bishops without controlling the universal Church. Its success lies in appointments, seminary culture, translation, reception of Roman documents, and the claim that Rome habitually misunderstands Germany.

### Italian imperial-sacral current

The Italian current seeks access to Roman administration, finance, appointments, concordats, and the political symbolism of a Catholic empire. Mussolini provides proximity but not safe sponsorship. Under Umberto II its political shelter weakens because the monarch is personally devout and does not need a clandestine national church to legitimate himself.

The current remains dangerous through bureaucratic access rather than a coherent cardinal bloc.

### Spanish current and the orthodoxy trap

The Spanish current begins by seeking a nationalized, hyper-orthodox Catholic system. Scrutiny after *Humani Generis*, Franco's anti-Masonry, and the danger of exposure force it to defend itself through increasingly exact Roman orthodoxy.

Its pupils eventually believe what their compromised teachers used as camouflage. The current loops back into Catholicism, strengthens traditionalist and confessional-state thought, and ceases to function as a hostile infiltration bloc. By the 1960s and 1970s it is better described as the **Spanish Roman school**.

### Switzerland

Neutral financial, educational, publishing, and travel networks provide continuity among otherwise isolated currents. Switzerland is a conduit, refuge, and archive rather than the secret headquarters of one global operation.

## Pius XII, 1941–1958

### 1941–1947: governing toward an Axis peace

Pius XII does not bless National Socialism or racial empire. He does recognize by 1943–1945 that Germany and Italy may win and that a restored liberal Europe is unlikely.

His strategy is bounded resistance:

- preserve bishops, dioceses, seminaries, records, and sacramental life;
- condemn racial and total-state principles in universal moral language;
- avoid gestures that achieve martyrdom without institutional benefit;
- keep canonical borders distinct from imposed political borders;
- and prepare the Church to live inside authoritarian national orders without becoming their department.

The General Government remains Catholic despite German domination and forced population movement. Polish dioceses around Kraków, Lwów, Stanisławów, Kielce, Radom, and Warsaw absorb enormous displacement. Piasecki is a problem of political capture rather than simple anti-Catholic persecution.

Northern France retains a pressured and increasingly secularized Catholic remnant. Southern France becomes the field in which an Occitan Catholic order can form.

### 1946: consistory and consecration

The international consistory broadens the College beyond an exclusively Italian administrative horizon. The consecration of Russia gives the Eastern question a supernatural and diplomatic urgency that survives the war settlement.

### 1947–1950: reconstruction and doctrinal discipline

Rome manages displaced dioceses, clergy migration, new borders, imperial missions, and Catholic life under German, Italian, Spanish, British, and Japanese systems.

*Mediator Dei* closes the war period by permitting serious liturgical renewal while opposing improvisation and antiquarian reconstruction. *Humani Generis* in 1950 disciplines speculative theology and alerts governments—especially Franco's Spain—to the danger of nationalized pseudo-orthodoxy.

The Assumption is defined in 1950. In this setting it also demonstrates that doctrinal development remains possible without political liberalization.

### 1950–1958: the Occitan and Eastern openings

Pius XII treats Occitania as the best opportunity to build a Catholic public order not wholly subordinated to one ruler. Rome supports:

- provincial and diocesan strength;
- independent schools and associations;
- noblesse oblige rather than ornamental aristocracy;
- Lourdes as a spiritual center;
- migration of clergy and institutions from northern France;
- corporative social teaching bounded by subsidiarity;
- and explicit Church freedom from Italian or Spanish appointment control.

Occitania does not become sinless or clerically governed. It becomes the most visible laboratory of a Catholic state whose political decentralization protects ecclesiastical and social pluralism.

Pius XII also rejects dispensationalism during the Israeli–Jordanian wars. Christians may provide humanitarian aid, but no parallel salvific covenant or prophetic territorial title authorizes war, and Christians may not attempt to manufacture the Apocalypse. This does not license anti-Jewish violence or deny ordinary civil protection.

## Gregory XVII, 1958–1971

### Election

The 1958 conclave is divided among Angelo Roncalli, Gregorio Pietro Agagianian, Giuseppe Siri, and several Roman compromises. The Orthodox revival in Russia, the Italian empire's Orthodox populations, the Armenian survival question, and the need for a universal rather than merely Italian papacy give Agagianian a narrow final majority.

He takes the name **Gregory XVII**, evoking Gregory the Illuminator, the Eastern missions, and continuity with the Roman see. His first address emphasizes the Church's apostolic universality, the suffering and resurrection of Eastern Christianity, and the duty to bring every people into Christ without erasing its legitimate inheritance.

The election is not an Armenian purge of the Curia. Roman doctrinal officials remain influential; Eastern, missionary, and Oriental offices gain stature; and Domenico Tardini remains a plausible Secretary of State. Italy celebrates the election but cannot claim the pope as an imperial ornament.

### Friends and political posture

Gregory possesses genuine friends:

- Franco's Spain has already moved from nationalizing Catholicism toward Catholicizing nationalism.
- Occitania is the most enthusiastic social reception of his program.
- Portugal offers cautious, institutionally useful cooperation.
- Umberto II becomes a principal ally through his protection of Eastern Christians and restoration of Greek and Yugoslav crowns.

Gregory's warning to these states is prudential rather than accusatory: success is not an eschatological timetable and no political order is guaranteed permanent divine favor.

### Germany

Gregory distinguishes Berlin, German dioceses, Wolfgang's household, and the SS Reichskommissariats. He hopes that Wolfgang's Lutheranism and generational distance from Hitler may permit better relations.

His statement that a ruler inherits responsibility for institutions exercising power in his name is received by Wolfgang as a veiled geopolitical threat. Wolfgang responds by demanding tighter control of scandal and information rather than inviting moral inspection. The result is a tragic, transactional relationship: Rome believes conditions may be improving because fewer reports arrive; Wolfgang believes Rome is waiting to weaponize every failure.

## The Council of Christ the King, 1962–1965

### Purpose and participants

The Second Vatican Ecumenical Council is popularly called the **Council of Christ the King**. Gregory calls it to:

- complete Vatican I's unfinished ecclesiology;
- define papal primacy together with episcopal and patriarchal authority;
- defend Church freedom against friendly and hostile national states;
- give a positive Catholic answer to nationalism and racialism;
- regularize relations with the Eastern apostolic churches;
- clarify religious immunity without indifferentism;
- and settle liturgical and social questions without constructing a new faith.

Catholic bishops vote. Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and other apostolic Eastern delegations attend as honored observers and theological interlocutors. Protestant delegations do not receive an observer seat at the council.

### Ecclesial patrimony

The council's central constructive idea is **ecclesial patrimony**.

Every person receives a real historical, cultural, familial, linguistic, and ritual inheritance. That inheritance is good but not total. It cannot abolish conversion, free will, natural law, sacramental communion, or the unity of humanity in Christ.

The normal or patrimonial rite of Greeks, Russians, Copts, Armenians, Syriacs, Latins, and other apostolic peoples deserves protection. A person should ordinarily live within that inherited church and rite, but Catholic and Orthodox sacraments are mutually recognized as valid, and limited sacramental sharing becomes possible where access or grave pastoral need requires it.

This doctrine answers racialism without demanding cultural deracination:

> Inheritance is received as a vocation and gift; it is not the whole definition or final end of the person.

### Papacy, bishops, and patriarchs

The council teaches that papal infallibility applies only under defined conditions of definitive universal teaching on faith or morals. It does not attach automatically to interviews, diplomatic tactics, appointments, scientific claims, economic proposals, or every disciplinary decision.

Diocesan bishops are true apostolic pastors, not papal branch managers. Eastern patriarchs and synods recover meaningful authority over liturgy, clergy discipline, canon law, and internal government. The pope remains the visible center and final guardian of universal communion.

### Near-reunion with the East

The council:

- recognizes the validity of Orthodox sacraments and apostolic succession;
- permits the original Greek Creed without the *Filioque* while retaining the legitimacy of the Latin formula;
- rejects compulsory Latinization;
- strengthens Eastern Catholic patriarchal government;
- and creates standing Catholic–Orthodox theological and canonical commissions.

Full reunion still fails over universal papal jurisdiction, post-schism definitions, overlapping dioceses, and the Eastern Catholic question. The schism is nevertheless reduced from mutual practical estrangement to an incomplete apostolic communion one settlement short of reunion.

### Other religions and religious immunity

The council does not treat Judaism, Islam, Protestantism, Shinto, or any other non-apostolic religion as a permanent parallel dispensation. Mission and conversion remain duties.

It distinguishes:

- the objective claim of religious truth;
- the freedom required for genuine faith;
- civil immunity from forced profession or sacramental participation;
- and the state's authority to restrain fraud, violence, sedition, exploitation, and attacks on the common good.

Non-Catholics possess civil protection as persons. A Catholic confessional state may privilege the Church but may not compel baptism, create a national church, or make ordinary justice conditional on sacramental conformity.

### Political and social order

Patriotism, monarchy, republic, federalism, corporative institutions, and confessional states may all be legitimate. The council condemns racial supremacy, collective punishment, coerced ethnic transformation, national idolatry, leader worship, and absorption of Church, family, profession, or conscience into the state.

Catholic social teaching supports just wages, distributed property, family responsibility, workers' associations, occupational bodies, cooperatives, and subsidiarity. It rejects both atomized economic liberalism and party-state corporatism.

### Liturgy

No historical Novus Ordo is created.

- Latin remains normative in the Roman Rite.
- The Roman Canon and inherited structure survive.
- Gregorian chant remains the normative solemn music.
- Readings, sermons, catechetical instruction, and selected sacramental texts may use the vernacular.
- Holy Week, rubrics, the calendar, and selected readings receive modest revision.
- Eastern rites are restored and protected rather than Latinized.

The postconciliar crisis is therefore political and administrative, not a universal parish-level rupture over a replaced Mass.

### Principal conciliar texts

The council's working corpus includes:

1. Dogmatic Constitution on the Church of Christ the King.
2. Decree on Ecclesial Patrimonies.
3. Decree on Apostolic Communion.
4. Constitution on Christ's Kingship and Civil Authority.
5. Decree on the Mission of the Nations.
6. Constitution on Sacred Worship.
7. Decree on the Christian Social Order.

## Fulton Sheen

Gregory makes Sheen one of the council's principal American interpreters rather than a prophet predicting a future conciliar apostasy.

Sheen contributes to:

- mission understood as evangelization rather than colonial geography;
- the distinction between patrimony and racial destiny;
- public communication and modern media;
- Catholic–Orthodox rapprochement;
- and the claim that national achievement remains subordinate to moral order.

Sheen becomes a cardinal in Gregory's College. His support is important in the 1971 conclave. He does not publicly endorse Kennedy, Buchanan, or National Renewal, but he treats Catholic statesmen as adults rather than perpetual suspects.

He condemns Rockwell's assassination and retaliation, counsels Buchanan privately, and interprets American space achievements as human workmanship within creation rather than secular emancipation from God.

Sheen dies in December 1979. His funeral becomes a major international Catholic and American event.

## Paul VI, 1971–1981

### Election

Gregory XVII dies on **16 May 1971**.

The conclave initially divides among Giovanni Battista Montini, Giuseppe Siri, and other candidates. Cardinal Sheen helps make Franjo Šeper intelligible as the man who can combine doctrinal reliability, Gregory's Eastern settlement, missionary seriousness, and administrative competence. Siri ultimately backs Šeper rather than Montini.

Šeper takes the name **Paul VI**. It signals mission, doctrinal travel, and continuation of Gregory's council without simply becoming Gregory XVIII.

### Character of the pontificate

Paul VI is an administrative and doctrinal consolidator rather than a prolific personal visionary. He refuses to treat truth and charity as rival parties and presents his Croatian and South Slav experience as evidence that inherited peoples can coexist within a larger apostolic communion.

His principal early texts include:

- *Veritatem Facientes* (1972), on truth, interpretation, and faithful reception;
- *Mysterium Ecclesiae* (1973), on the Church, doctrine, and authority;
- and the 1975 Holy Year exhortation.

He leaves Siri, Montini, Sheen, Ratzinger, Eastern patriarchs, and major missionary figures inside a broad governing coalition.

### 1975–1980

The Vatican condemns Rockwell's murder and the claim that political sin makes assassination deserved. It does not endorse National Renewal.

Reports of an SS religion remain sporadic and unverified. Investigators can establish eccentric rites, philosophical language, internal publications, and unusual ceremonies, but not a public separate church with a complete confession. Rome remains suspicious without issuing a formal judgment it cannot prove.

The same evidentiary restraint applies to German demographic policy. Rome confirms unequal contraceptive availability, small-family incentives, and sterilization as a restricted punishment for repeat violent native criminals. It condemns contraception and the manipulation of fertility without falsely claiming a universal program of forced sterilization.

Paul develops a close relationship with Buchanan after 1977 around family policy, resistance to coercive population programs, education, aid, and the peaceful use of space. He does not treat the president's private dedication of his office as a state conversion or as Vatican control of America.

The Church creates practical structures for orbital and lunar pastoral care: jurisdiction, calendars and obligations, emergency rites, sacramental supplies, burial and death procedures, chaplaincy, and the principle that no state owns souls because it operates a station.

Public Vatican messages ordinarily celebrate achievement, courage, family, peace, and creation. Warnings are reserved for actual controversies rather than attached reflexively to every celebration.

Paul VI dies on **30 December 1981**. His funeral is celebrated on 2 January 1982.

## Pius XIII, 1982–1985

### Election

The January 1982 conclave contains strong pressure for an Italian pope after two non-Italians. Giuseppe Siri is now old enough to be understood as a transitional but authoritative consolidator.

His 1971 support for Šeper demonstrates that he will not overturn the Council of Christ the King. Ratzinger and Wojtyła help bridge the doctrinal, Eastern, and international blocs; Eastern cardinals receive guarantees that patriarchal rights will remain; and Siri's opponents divide.

Siri takes the name **Pius XIII**, claiming continuity with Pius XII while presenting the previous two pontificates as one continuous settlement rather than an aberration to be reversed.

### Program

Pius XIII's governing idea is **consolidation under pressure**.

- Gregory supplied the council's vision.
- Paul VI built its administrative reception.
- Pius XIII gives it durable canon law.

He does not launch a purge or a new liturgical revolution. Ratzinger becomes a principal doctrinal and legal collaborator. The Roman Rite remains stable; Eastern rights become ordinary law; and the council ceases to function as a perpetual partisan slogan.

### International posture

Pius uses colder language than Gregory toward Germany but communicates more clearly and avoids allegations unsupported by evidence. He makes no formal judgment that the SS possesses a separate religion.

During the Falklands/Malvinas crisis he refuses to grant Catholic Argentina automatic support against Britain. The Vatican works with Brazil and others on the Rio formula, then seeks limits on escalation, protection of civilians and chaplains, and a settlement not defined as a war of Catholic solidarity.

The 1984 election of Ronald Reagan reduces Vatican access to the American presidency. Reagan is culturally conservative and anti-communist in temperament but Protestant, economically liberal, and more explicitly American nationalist. Cooperation persists on family and religious issues while disagreement grows over market individualism, foreign policy, and the autonomy of Catholic political judgment.

By 1985 Pius XIII has made the Council of Christ the King the normal constitutional order of the Church.

## Relations by bloc

### Latin bloc

The Vatican regards Spain, Portugal, Occitania, and Umberto's Italy as friends whose public Catholicism can serve the common good. It does not govern their cabinets or bless every imperial policy.

- **Occitania** is the closest working model of a decentralized Catholic public order.
- **Spain** supplies confessional-state confidence, traditionalist scholarship, and Hispanic diplomacy.
- **Portugal** connects Atlantic, African, Brazilian, and missionary worlds while remaining more cautious.
- **Italy** protects Rome, governs major Christian populations, and supplies the strongest political ally, but its empire and proximity make every disagreement consequential.

### Germany

Relations are correct, cautious, and increasingly tragic. German bishops gain room to survive and some capacity to shape local life. The SS conceals its religious and colonial order. Wolfgang hears moral scrutiny as geopolitical hostility, and Rome interprets reduced evidence as possible reform.

### Britain and Commonwealth

Britain is Protestant and imperial but institutionally familiar. Catholic hospitals, missions, schools, and Commonwealth churches remain important. The Kuwait victory and Falklands war produce humanitarian and just-war questions without turning Rome into Britain's ally.

### United States

Kennedy normalizes Catholic eligibility while trying to privatize his faith. Buchanan openly integrates Catholic conviction with public duty, creating the high point of Vatican-American access and the danger of partisan Catholic capture. Reagan restores greater distance.

### Japan

The Church works through missions, schools, associated states, and minority protections. The council rejects the permanence of Shinto or imperial hierarchy as a religious settlement while recognizing real cultural patrimony.

### Orthodoxy

Orthodox Christianity is the Church's principal ecumenical partner. Russia, Greece, Serbia, Armenia, the Syriac churches, the Copts, and Ethiopia give the rapprochement both theological and geopolitical weight. Near-reunion is real, but neither side is treated as already identical.

### Judaism, Islam, and Protestantism

The Church rejects coercion and collective mistreatment while continuing to seek conversion. It does not endorse dispensationalism, parallel salvific covenants, permanent religious pluralism, or the claim that political control of territory fulfills a prophecy Christians are obliged to enforce.

## State of the Church in 1985

By 1985 the Church is more institutionally confident and more Eastern than its real-world counterpart, but not politically triumphant.

- The Roman Rite has continuity rather than rupture.
- Catholic–Orthodox relations are close, sacramentally serious, and still short of full reunion.
- Confessional Catholic states are legitimate but not self-authenticating.
- The Latin bloc is a field of friendship and influence, not the Church's temporal government.
- American Catholicism has high cultural and political prestige.
- Germany remains the largest information and moral failure: Rome sees fragments of the SS and Reichskommissariat systems without understanding their full architecture.
- The clandestine currents survive as regional appointment, seminary, administrative, and interpretive networks rather than one master conspiracy.
- Pius XIII has converted the council from a recent event into the durable constitutional settlement of the Church.

The Church's central danger is no longer simple persecution. It is the temptation for every successful state, party, or bloc to claim Catholic universality as confirmation of its own national mission.
