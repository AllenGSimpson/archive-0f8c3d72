## How this dossier should be used

This is a source map, not an endorsement of Marshall's synthesis. The book
joins documented events, theological judgments, private revelations, oral
testimony, press reporting, and conjectural connections into a single
infiltration narrative. Those categories should remain separate in setting
development.

Recommended evidence labels:

- **P — Primary:** an official document, archival testimony, contemporary
  record, or a participant's own published account.
- **S — Secondary:** later history or biography based on identified sources.
- **A — Anecdotal:** an interview, recollection, hearsay report, or privately
  transmitted story.
- **C — Contested:** authenticity, interpretation, or inference is materially
  disputed.
- **M — Marshall synthesis:** the connection is principally Marshall's own
  narrative construction rather than something established by the cited
  source.

## Thesis map

Marshall's narrative can be reduced to the following chain:

1. Nineteenth-century anti-clerical and secret-society texts advocate changing
   the Church indirectly by shaping future clergy.
2. Marian apparitions and papal anti-Modernist or anti-Masonic texts are read
   as warnings about the same program.
3. Communist testimony about institutional infiltration is treated as evidence
   that the program entered seminaries and the priesthood.
4. Twentieth-century theological and liturgical reformers are presented as
   heirs, conscious or otherwise, of Modernism.
5. Vatican II and the post-conciliar liturgical reforms are treated as the
   institutional breakthrough of that tendency.
6. Vatican banking scandals, clerical sexual abuse, and organized blocs among
   cardinals are treated as later expressions of the same infiltration.
7. The Sankt Gallen group, Benedict XVI's resignation, and Francis's election
   are presented as the culmination of the long program.

Steps 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6 each contain real documents or events. The claim that
all seven form one continuous, coordinated operation is the book's central
interpretive thesis and is not itself established merely by the existence of
the component events.

### Paul VI and the authority of Vatican II

Marshall quotes Paul VI's statement that the Council did not issue
"extraordinary dogmatic pronouncements" and then concludes that "nothing
binding came from Vatican II." The first proposition is documented; the second
does not follow from the cited speech.

The same Paul VI address says that the Council made known its **authoritative
teaching** while preserving its "authority and force":

<https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/speeches/1965/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19651207_epilogo-concilio.html>

The closing brief also directs that what the Council established be religiously
observed:

<https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_letters/documents/hf_p-vi_apl_19651208_in-spiritu-sancto.html>

For corpus purposes, record Marshall's conclusion as **M/C**, not as the plain
meaning of the primary document.

### McCarrick: later evidence unavailable to the 2019 book

The most important post-publication cross-check is the Holy See's 2020
*Report on the Holy See's Institutional Knowledge and Decision-Making Related
to Former Cardinal Theodore Edgar McCarrick (1930 to 2017)*.

- Official full report:
  <https://www.vatican.va/resources/resources_rapporto-card-mccarrick_20201110_en.pdf>
- Secretary of State's publication statement:
  <https://www.vatican.va/resources/resources_dichiarazione-parolin_rapporto-mccarrick_20201110_en.html>

This 461-page report should supersede Marshall's short 2019 timeline wherever
the two conflict. It supplies documents and interviews concerning what bishops,
nuncios, curial officials, and popes knew at different times. It also provides a
much stronger basis for modeling institutional failure, patronage, rumor,
deference, and accountability than a single all-purpose infiltration theory.

## High-risk claims requiring special verification

These claims are to be used as reference material, but due to setting
divergence are to proceed differently:

1. Leo XIII heard a literal dialogue between God and Satan in the exact form
   repeated in devotional literature.
2. Mélanie Calvat's 1879 La Salette text has the same status and wording as her
   sealed 1851 statement.
3. Bella Dodd personally placed, or knew the identities of, four future
   cardinals who were Communist agents.
4. A Masonic dossier found in Bugnini's briefcase caused his removal.
5. The published Third Secret of Fatima omits a second text.


These claims should never enter setting canon with a simple "historical" label:

1. The Alta Vendita documents prove one continuous organization directed
   twentieth- and twenty-first-century Church events.
2. Cardinal Siri was elected pope in 1958 and forced to withdraw.
3. John Paul I was murdered.
4. Aleister Crowley's connections to Sankt Gallen or the burial place of his
   ashes establish an operational connection to McCarrick.
5. McCarrick's youthful visit to Switzerland made him an agent of the Sankt
    Gallen group decades before that group existed.
6. The Vatican ATM shutdown was leverage that forced Benedict XVI to resign.
7. Pre-conclave support for Bergoglio rendered the 2013 election invalid.
8. Paul VI's refusal to make new extraordinary dogmatic definitions means
    Vatican II produced no binding or authoritative teaching.

For worldbuilding, these may be valuable as:

- beliefs held by characters;
- rival intelligence assessments;
- propaganda narratives;
- partially true conspiracies enlarged by later myth;
- true scandals misjoined into a false master narrative; or
- fictional conspiracies whose documentary trail is deliberately modeled on
  the ambiguities above.

They should not be silently represented as settled real-world history.
