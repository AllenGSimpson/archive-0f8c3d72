# Setting Bible

## Purpose and authority

This is the working reference for the alternate-World-War-II setting developed in [transcript.md](./transcript.md). It records the setting as established by the conversation without treating every brainstormed alternative as settled fact.

When sources disagree, use this order of authority:

1. A direct user declaration or correction.
2. An assistant proposal explicitly accepted or built upon by the user.
3. The newest version of a detail.
4. A plausible but unconfirmed assistant proposal, marked here as tentative.

Status terms used below:

- **Canon:** directly established or subsequently relied upon.
- **Working canon:** the current best version, but details may still change.
- **Open:** discussed but not decided.

The spellings **Occitania** and **Ostatania** both occur in the imported chat. This bible standardizes the southern French state as **Occitania** unless a later decision establishes a different formal name.

## High concept

The Second World War ends in 1947 with Germany, Italy, and Japan surviving as victorious or dominant imperial powers. The United States never enters the general war, Britain is defeated but neither invaded nor dissolved, and nuclear weapons never become a wartime technology. The result is not a German world empire. It is a multipolar cold war among five unequal power systems:

- the German continental empire;
- the Japanese maritime and resource empire;
- the Italian-Spanish-led Latin Catholic bloc;
- the battered British Commonwealth;
- the United States and its increasingly coercive American hemisphere.

The setting begins as a study of ordinary life in a surviving National Socialist Germany and expands into a global political, social, and technological history from the late 1940s through the 1970s.

The central tonal rule is that no society should be reduced to a caricature. Germany is neither a flawless fascist consumer paradise nor a uniformly gray prison. Its visible prosperity, modernity, social programs, repression, racial hierarchy, forced labor, imperial extraction, and propaganda all coexist. The same rule applies to the other blocs: each has attractive surfaces, internal contradictions, victims, beneficiaries, and institutions that behave rationally within a morally compromised order.

## Foundational divergences

### The United States stays out

Japan does not attack Pearl Harbor. It pursues the Strike South strategy while avoiding a direct war with the United States. American ships in and around the Philippines watch Japan take the European colonial possessions of Southeast Asia but do not intervene in time to alter the war.

The United States therefore receives none of the transformative effects of full wartime mobilization:

- no victory culture;
- no mass veteran generation comparable to the historical United States;
- no wartime industrial and aviation expansion on the same scale;
- no wartime cure for the Great Depression;
- no Manhattan Project or combat use of atomic weapons;
- no historical Sherman/Patton-centered armored development;
- no postwar presumption that America is responsible for the whole world.

### Britain loses without occupation

Operation Torch and the Normandy landings fail or never produce a successful return to continental Europe. German submarines prove that Britain can be isolated from its trade. The Royal Navy helps keep the home islands from invasion, but Britain ends the war bomb-damaged, indebted, strategically defeated, and stripped of key positions.

Britain retains much of its empire because prestige and imperial resources become more important after defeat, not less. Decolonization is delayed, diverted, or made far more violent.

### Germany wins in the east

Germany controls European Russia to approximately the Urals and builds a colonial-slave system across the conquered east. Finland is rewarded with custodianship of Karelia, Murmansk, Nenets territory, and an Arctic frontier reaching toward the Urals. The lands beyond the Urals fragment into Soviet successor administrations, military enclaves, refugee zones, and local regimes rather than one clean Russian state.

In this setting Germany never makes the same wartime policy transition to industrialized extermination described in the chat; racial persecution, deportation, dispossession, forced labor, mass death, and colonial enslavement nevertheless remain foundational to the Reich. Slave labor continues through the war and remains hidden within the postwar imperial economy.

### Japan wins an empire without fighting America

Japan occupies or dominates French Indochina, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies, much of the South Pacific, China, and the eastern Russian rim. Siam is an associated ally or protectorate. Japan sponsors a Vladivostok zone, an Eastern Siberian Republic, and a Chukotka republic. Burma and India remain contested frontiers.

### Nuclear weapons never define the age

No power gives nuclear weapons enough priority to make them a wartime or early Cold War reality. Atomic science develops mainly around electricity, industrial heat, propulsion, and prestige. Civilian nuclear power becomes one of the defining economic races of the 1960s.

## The world order

### German sphere

**Core:** Greater Germany and the territories incorporated into it.

**Direct eastern empire:** European Russia to the Urals, administered as racial-colonial territory with German settlement, coerced labor, deportations, and frontier militarization.

**European dependents and aligned states:** northern France, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Hungary, Romania, the General Government in former Poland, and other occupied or subordinated administrations. Their exact constitutional labels remain open.

**Colonial possessions:** former French, Belgian, and British assets in Africa, including the Congo, with boundaries and administrative arrangements still to be mapped precisely.

**Political character:** National Socialist, bureaucratic, technically ambitious, racially hierarchical, and increasingly pseudo-dynastic after Hitler. The state presents itself abroad as orderly, cultured, prosperous, and open to curated inspection. Foreign visitors can see German streets, consumer life, resorts, Olympics, technology, and model settlements; forced labor, eastern repression, and colonial brutality are concealed, euphemized, or geographically screened.

**Economy:** a managed imperial economy that uses coerced labor and colonial extraction while trying to provide visible security and consumer gains to favored German citizens. It is not fully autarkic, but trade is politically managed. Chronic manpower demands encourage mechanization, standardization, and automation.

**Military and technology:** the leading land-technology and rocketry power. Germany fields jets, assault rifles, guided weapons, Panzerfaust descendants, modern main battle tanks, increasingly effective night vision, advanced submarines, satellites, and a calculation-heavy space program. By the late 1960s it is the world's largest arms exporter.

**Succession:** Hitler and Eva Braun have a son in late 1947, celebrated as a victory child. When Hitler dies or becomes incapacitated in the early 1960s, the boy is too young to govern. A regency of party, SS, military, technical, and household factions rules in his name. Eva Braun guards the founder cult and the child's private formation. The regime denies that it has become a hereditary monarchy even as the eventual accession is staged like a coronation.

### Japanese sphere

**Core:** the Japanese home islands and the preexisting imperial system.

**Dominated territories:** Korea, Manchuria, China, Indochina, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies, Pacific possessions, and the eastern Siberian client belt. Exact legal categories vary between colony, military administration, puppet state, protectorate, and associated ally.

**China:** formally unified under the Nanjing-based **National Government of the Republic of China**, descended from the wartime Reorganized Government. In practice it is a weak confederation of provincial authorities dependent on Japan. Communist resistance is largely cleared from its major bases during the 1950s, but local unrest continues. Japan encourages agriculture, mining, and resource extraction while restricting higher Chinese industrial development. A revived examination system and traditionalist rhetoric give the regime cultural legitimacy while also helping freeze social mobility and technical progress.

**Imperial justification:** Japan presents itself as China's guardian against Western contamination rather than its conqueror. Industrialization is described as a burden Japan bears for Asia. This rhetoric binds the empire while making Japan responsible for manufacturing, transport, defense, and administration across an enormous area.

**Economy:** abundant imperial resources but severe skilled-labor shortages. Japan responds with mass education, assembly-line production, automation, early robotics, rapid nuclear adoption, and an enlarged industrial middle class. The zaibatsu remain powerful but must coexist with a more directive imperial state.

**Military:** carrier-first maritime doctrine. Yamato survives as a prestige, invasion-support, and shore-bombardment asset rather than the core of battle doctrine. Japan maintains strong carrier task forces and increasingly modern armor, aircraft, and missiles.

**Philippines:** initially an American-associated quasi-independent state, then the site of the 1950s Philippine War. After American withdrawal it becomes genuinely independent but enters Japan's sphere as an associated ally similar to Siam, not as a directly ruled colony.

### Latin Catholic bloc

The Latin bloc grows out of wartime Italian and Spanish success but becomes more cohesive only after Mussolini's decline and the German-Italian split. Its major members are Italy, Spain, Occitania, and—more cautiously—Portugal. It competes with Germany, Britain, Japan, and the United States for cultural and political influence in Latin America.

#### Italy

Italy survives as an imperial Fascist state. It controls or dominates Albania, Greece, parts of former Yugoslavia, Tunisia, Syria, and other Mediterranean positions still to be precisely mapped.

Mussolini remains the central figure through the 1950s but has no convincing political heir. Victor Emmanuel III eventually abdicates because of age, not defeat. Umberto II accumulates authority as Mussolini and the Grand Council decline, and by about 1960 becomes an effective **King-Duce**. Fascism survives as royal-corporatist administrative machinery, but the regime becomes more Catholic, military, aristocratic, and monarchical. Umberto's son Vittorio Emanuele is groomed as the next imperial prince.

#### Spain

Francoist Spain is stronger and less isolated than in real history, but still begins 1947 damaged by the Civil War and industrially behind Germany, northern France, and Occitania. It controls Morocco and seeks influence throughout the former Spanish Americas.

Franco's regime is more Catholic-restorationist than Italian Fascism. Franco prepares a serious monarchical succession, likely forming Juan Carlos earlier and more deliberately. Spain's relationship with post-Mussolini Italy improves as both systems become Catholic and monarchical.

#### Portugal

Salazar's Portugal is no longer isolated and benefits from the survival of authoritarian Catholic Europe and its colonial markets. It remains more conservative-corporatist than Fascist and distrusts Spanish domination. Its succession may remain civilian under a Caetano-like figure; monarchical restoration is possible but not settled.

#### Occitania

Southern France becomes an Italian-aligned but increasingly autonomous state with Spanish and Vatican influence. It includes major industrial and commercial regions such as the Rhône valley, Toulouse, and Bordeaux-related interests, though the exact border with northern France needs a map decision.

Occitania is Catholic, regionalist, and aristocratic without necessarily having a king. Its provinces deliberately evoke the old duchies of France. The emerging system is an oligarchy or corporate chamber of dukes, provincial notables, industrial interests, clergy, and military commands. Historical noble blood carries cultural authority but does not automatically confer territorial rule.

Each province/duchy maintains a military formation that grows from battalion to regiment to division as the state rearms. Formations use regional symbols and specialize for local terrain and industry: Rhône armored units, Bordeaux marines, Toulouse mechanized forces, and unusually deep organic air-ground integration. Occitania is relatively naval only because German-controlled northern France has essentially no navy; its own force remains land-centered.

#### The two Frances

Northern France is a German client. It is subjected to an official ideology of national culpability and self-denunciation comparable in function to East German antifascist identity, except redirected to justify German victory and supervision. Controlled expressions of anti-German sentiment may be tolerated as a counterintelligence trap.

Occitania develops a separate generation and political identity. The German-Italian split turns the two French states into mirror rivals: Occitan rearmament justifies a German-controlled French army; that army then justifies further Occitan expansion. Their relationship rhymes with the two Germanies of real history without copying them exactly.

### British Commonwealth

Britain is defeated, bomb-damaged, indebted, and strategically constrained, but retains the Crown, Parliament, the Royal Navy, and much of the empire. Its political culture is dominated by reconstruction, imperial survival, anti-submarine vulnerability, and the need to remain a great power. Historical postwar Labour-versus-Tory assumptions do not transfer cleanly; every major party is a reconstruction and security party with different priorities.

**Territorial losses and concessions:** Britain loses Egypt. Italy takes Syria. Morocco falls under Spain and Tunisia under Italy; authority across the Maghreb between them is confused and contested. Germany acquires former European colonial holdings, including the Congo. Britain retains many Atlantic, African, Indian Ocean, and Commonwealth positions.

**India:** the Raj cracks rather than cleanly decolonizes. Britain fortifies selected ports and enclaves, including Ceylon and likely Bombay or comparable strategic positions. Congress, Muslim, princely, southern, British-backed, and Japanese-backed forces create prolonged Indian Wars. The subcontinent becomes more fragmented and violent than Siberia in some periods. Britain never restores total control.

**Canada:** the Commonwealth's crucial North American pillar. Britain encourages east-west transport, refining, defense, and trade so Canadian resources do not flow automatically south. After the 1968 Atlantic/Mid-Atlantic Ridge Accord and Rockwell's American plurality, Canada develops public invasion paranoia, defense-in-depth planning, militia concepts, Arctic infrastructure, and stronger Crown identity.

**Australia and New Zealand:** remain Commonwealth-aligned under intense fear of Japan. Britain prioritizes their defense industries and standardization. America and Japan both court them, but neither displaces Britain in the current period.

**South Africa and Rhodesia:** Britain sacrifices moral scruples to retain them. Apartheid and white-minority rule persist and may be sanitized as a renewed “civilizing mission.” Germany cultivates Boer particularism while avoiding direct war with Britain. Radical demographic policies may circulate in elite discussion; whether any become state programs remains open.

**Late-1960s doctrine:** after America effectively places Britain on the European side of the Atlantic dividing line, London fortifies Canada, Bermuda, Jamaica, Belize, the Falklands, and other Atlantic positions. The Commonwealth increasingly plans to survive without American protection.

### United States and American hemisphere

The United States is prosperous later and more slowly than in real history. It emerges from the Depression without wartime mobilization, turns against the New Deal as a governing identity, and develops a foreign policy centered on keeping all rival blocs out of the Americas.

#### Presidential sequence

- Franklin D. Roosevelt, reelected in 1944, dies in 1945.
- James F. Byrnes, 1945–1949, dismantles or narrows much of the New Deal emergency order.
- Thomas E. Dewey, 1949–1957, elected in 1948 and narrowly reelected in 1952 with Earl Warren as vice president.
- Earl Warren, 1957–1961, selected by the House after the fractured 1956 election.
- John F. Kennedy, 1961–1969, wins in 1960 and a landslide in 1964; he is not assassinated.
- Nelson Rockefeller, 1969–1973, selected by the House after losing the 1968 popular and electoral pluralities to George Lincoln Rockwell.
- A fused Democratic-Republican administration, likely Rockefeller with a hawkish Democratic running mate, narrowly wins 1972.

#### National Renewal

National Renewal is a durable American fascist-adjacent party, not a marginal American Nazi Party. It combines national mission, corporatism, labor discipline, youth formation, hemispheric expansion, racial politics, military modernization, anti-bank populism, and selectively American constitutional imagery.

Charles Lindbergh is its early national figure. George Lincoln Rockwell enters through legitimate party politics rather than founding a fringe party, learns public discipline and coded rhetoric, and replaces Lindbergh by the mid-1960s. National Renewal tactically endorses Kennedy in 1964 because his activism gives America “mission,” then wins a popular plurality under Rockwell in 1968. The House chooses Rockefeller through a Democrat-Republican compact, giving National Renewal its foundational **Stolen Mandate** myth.

By 1972 the Democrats and Republicans fuse into a defensive Democratic-Republican party. National Renewal loses the presidency narrowly but wins the House and helps secure a constitutional “free association” principle that blocks federal anti-discrimination law. Rockwell begins grooming a much younger successor; the exact person remains open.

#### Hemispheric policy

American restraint gives way to coercive hemispheric order:

- Greenland becomes formally independent but functions as an American base.
- The Philippines becomes the first template for undeclared “associated-state intervention,” and also the first major failure.
- Honduras becomes the first successful American client-state intervention in 1962.
- Venezuela becomes the bloodier second test during Kennedy's second term.
- Cuba is prepared as another intervention but spared when the 1968 legitimacy crisis freezes Washington.
- Rival powers penetrate Argentina, Peru, Ecuador, Cuba, and other states through arms, ports, corporations, volunteers, and cultural alliances.

America describes this as enforcement of a modern Monroe Doctrine. Its opponents describe it as a hemispheric empire that refuses the word empire.

### Russia, Siberia, and Central Asia

The former Soviet space is divided among:

- German rule west of and along the Urals;
- Finnish-controlled Arctic territories and frontier posts;
- Japanese-aligned eastern coastal republics;
- Soviet successor bureaucracies centered on surviving cities and military districts;
- local military strongmen and refugee governments;
- Central Asian republics containing large Russian refugee populations.

Omsk, Magnitogorsk, and other interior cities may anchor successor states, but no government commands universal recognition. American maps may show a formal Russia that has little practical authority. The United States sends Arctic “humanitarian” aid through northern rivers, mixing food and medicine with radios, machinery, technicians, and intelligence support.

### Middle East and eastern Mediterranean

This region remains deliberately unstable and incompletely settled.

- Britain loses Egypt.
- Italy controls or dominates Syria and Greece and presses toward a neo-Roman Mediterranean order.
- Palestine's mandate and a possible Jewish state become an international crisis without a strong victorious Britain to impose a settlement.
- Turkey is strategically isolated between Italian expansion, German influence, and control of the Straits.
- Persia, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, and competing Arab movements maneuver among Britain, Italy, Germany, and local nationalists.
- Italian rule over Orthodox populations creates pressure and opportunity for Byzantine Catholic institutions and Vatican diplomacy.

The exact borders, governments, and chronology of Palestine, Persia, Iraq, Arabia, and Turkey remain open and should be resolved before stories center on the region.

## Technology and industry

### Computing

Computing and cryptology form the quiet race beneath the public space race. Germany favors engineering, calculation, orbital mechanics, military control, and state-directed technical systems. America begins from a more commercial and pilot-centered tradition. Britain remains a major computing and signals power. Italy participates meaningfully; Japan initially trails but accelerates under imperial labor pressure.

### Space

Germany follows the rocket-and-calculation path and wins the first satellite around 1957–58. America responds with emergency rockets but makes the reusable, pilot-centered spaceplane its public national project. Britain and the Commonwealth provide tracking, aviation, and technical infrastructure. Japan pursues an imperial technical program. Italy announces prestige projects that are initially more symbolic than operational.

America achieves orbital spaceplane flight in 1969. Germany lands the first person on the Moon in 1970, seven months before a planned American attempt. The image of a German astronaut saluting beside a swastika produces a global prestige shock and militarizes the next phase of space competition.

### Nuclear power

The 1950s emphasize research and pilot plants. Germany and Japan adopt nuclear power at scale in the 1960s because energy independence and industrial capacity are strategic necessities. America lags because oil, gas, coal, and hydro make atomic power an expensive option rather than a necessity, then catches up rapidly between 1968 and 1972. Nuclear weapons remain outside the current setting unless deliberately introduced later.

### Military divergence

- Germany leads in modern armor, jets, submarines, night vision, guided weapons, and assault-rifle doctrine.
- Japan leads in carrier-centered maritime operations and adapts German-style land technology to imperial needs.
- Britain emphasizes anti-submarine warfare, jets, Commonwealth standardization, and fortress logistics.
- America retains battleship prestige longer, develops light expeditionary armor after the Philippines, adopts tracked APCs, missiles, radar, and jungle warfare, and treats heavy armor primarily as homeland or prestige capability.
- Occitania pioneers division-level integration of armor, mechanized troops, interceptors, and close air support.
- Argentina becomes overwhelmingly German-equipped by the late 1960s, including Panzer 10/11 tanks and advanced aircraft.

## Culture, propaganda, and everyday life

### Curated openness

The German sphere is more visible to foreigners than the historical Soviet bloc. Germany hosts foreign tours, exhibitions, Olympics, technical delegations, and carefully managed journalism. This makes it harder for Americans to imagine the Reich as a totally alien sealed society. German consumer life and achievement are real enough to be persuasive, while the empire's worst violence remains screened.

### Propaganda competition

German propaganda is the world's most professional and scandal-resistant. It uses respectable front groups, cultural exchange, selective truth, audience-specific messages, and deniable intermediaries. Japanese operations are more likely to be exposed. One such scandal causes Americans to recast Philippine independence as Japanese subversion, worsening the settlement and helping cause the later war.

### Social mood by bloc

- **Germany:** disciplined prosperity for favored citizens, veteran prestige, youth formation, managed consumption, imperial distance, and normalized hierarchy.
- **Japan:** rising industrial wealth, long working hours, education, technical merit, imperial duty, and chronic manpower pressure.
- **Britain:** reconstruction, austerity, siege mentality, imperial nostalgia, and fear of strategic irrelevance.
- **United States:** delayed prosperity, anti-New Deal memory, anxiety about national drift, televised mission politics, and recurring constitutional legitimacy crises.
- **Canada:** Commonwealth loyalty hardened by fear of absorption into the American hemisphere.
- **Occitania:** Catholic regionalism, aristocratic civic symbolism, industrial ambition, and a generation increasingly separate from France.

## Narrative principles

1. Prefer lived experience and institutional texture over abstract bloc descriptions.
2. Show the gap between what a citizen sees, what a foreign visitor sees, and what the state hides.
3. Do not let technological competence imply moral legitimacy.
4. Do not assume every authoritarian state is identical; German National Socialism, Italian royal Fascism, Francoism, Salazarism, Japanese imperial corporatism, and National Renewal have different institutions and goals.
5. Preserve multipolarity. No state can simply dictate to the whole world.
6. Treat logistics, manpower, trade, administrative capacity, and domestic legitimacy as constraints.
7. Mark proposals as open until a later entry clearly selects them.

## Major open questions

- The exact sequence of military events and treaties ending the war in 1947.
- Formal borders and administrative names across occupied Russia, Poland, the Balkans, Africa, and the Middle East.
- The exact northern France–Occitania border and status of Bordeaux.
- The constitutional titles and personnel of the German regency.
- The formal name and personality of Hitler's son.
- Japan's postwar prime-ministerial and imperial political leadership.
- Exact dates of Mussolini's death or retirement and Umberto II's full consolidation.
- Portugal's succession and whether monarchy returns.
- Palestine's settlement and the governments of Egypt, Persia, Iraq, Arabia, and Turkey.
- The final factions and ceasefires ending the main Indian Wars.
- The timing and combatants of a later Falklands/Malvinas war.
- The precise name/designation system for later German aircraft and armored exports.
- The identity of Rockwell's younger National Renewal heir and the result of the 1976 election.
- Whether nuclear weapons remain permanently absent or emerge after civilian nuclear maturity.
