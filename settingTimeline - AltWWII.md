# Setting Timeline

## How to use this file

This chronology consolidates events established in [transcript.md](./transcript.md). It is a working timeline, not a verbatim transcript.

Detailed land, aerospace, and naval specifications are maintained in the [Land Armaments Reference, 1944–1985](./landArmamentsReference%20-%20AltWWII.md), [Aerospace Armaments Reference, 1944–1985](./aerospaceArmamentsReference%20-%20AltWWII.md), and [Naval Armaments Reference, 1944–1985](./navalArmamentsReference%20-%20AltWWII.md). P0-09 and this Timeline supersede the Aerospace Reference's shuttle-like American operational architecture until its dedicated reset. The [Material Culture and Civilian Industry Reference](./materialCultureReference%20-%20AltWWII.md), [Greater East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere Reference](./coProsperitySphereReference%20-%20AltWWII.md), [SS Religion Reference](./ssReligionReference%20-%20AltWWII.md), and [Catholic Church Reference](./catholicChurchReference%20-%20AltWWII.md) control their respective internal chronologies.

The timestamp-ordered Japanese, German, and Computing specialist branches occupy Turns 334–411 of the transcript; the material-culture, Latin-bloc, Catholic-Church, SS-religion, German-naming/Mittelafrika, post-1985 American, and Co-Prosperity Sphere continuation branches continue in Turns 412–743. Later direct corrections in those branches control the 1990 crash calendar, 1992 election and 1993 schism, American runway-to-orbit architecture, Weltnetz identity model, and the corrected constitutional and geographic order of Japanese Asia. Flag discussion is archival where the already-settled Dienstflaggen differ.

Unresolved chronology conflicts are tracked by stable identifier in [Questions and Confirmations](./questionsAndConfirmations%20-%20AltWWII.md). Named original people are indexed in the [AltWWII Character Dossier](./characterDossier%20-%20AltWWII.md).

- **Canon** means the event was directly established or relied upon later.
- **Working** means the event is the current best synthesis but its date or details remain adjustable.
- **Open** means the event was proposed but not selected.

Dates with a `c.` prefix are approximate. Where the conversation supplied only a range, this file keeps the range rather than inventing a precise day.

## The long war, 1941–1947

| Date | Status | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Dec. 1941 | Canon | Konoe's half-success and credible evidence that American intervention is not automatic permit the **Imperial Limited-War Directive**. Hirohito sanctions war against Britain and the Netherlands while explicitly excluding Pearl Harbor, the Philippines, Guam, Wake, and deliberate American casualties. Yamamoto supplies the decisive warning about American capacity; the policy is an Imperial Conference settlement, not his personal plan. |
| 1941 onward | Canon | The United States remains outside the general war. The Philippines stay under American control beyond the promised independence schedule and become the exposed edge of the American Pacific position. |
| 1941 onward | Canon | Germany is not placed under the same losing-war pressures as in real history. The setting's Nazi persecution develops around deportation, colonial conquest, racial rule, mass forced labor, and mass death rather than the exact historical extermination sequence. |
| 27 May 1942 | Canon | The Prague assassination bomb detonates after Heydrich's car has traveled slightly farther than it did historically. He suffers severe fragment wounds, fractured ribs, internal injury, infection risk, and a long visible recovery, but survives without the fatal septic collapse. Chronic pain, stiffness, and scarring shape his later security administration. |
| 1941–1945 | Working | Finland makes a major contribution against the Soviet Union, including operations associated with Leningrad and Arkhangelsk, and earns a large Arctic sphere after victory. |
| 1942–1944 | Canon | Japan takes Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies, Burma, and a deliberately short southern defensive perimeter by spring 1942. It refuses overreach toward Midway, Fiji, Samoa, or New Caledonia, then redirects the main effort to the 1943 Chongqing campaign and a stronger 1944 Ichi-Go while American forces observe without intervention. Siam enters Japan's associated sphere. |
| c. 1943–1944 | Canon framework | Britain's Commonwealth-supported but principally British Operation Torch lands around the Algerian coast and secures an initial bridgehead. Vichy, Italian, and German reinforcement and Axis air/submarine interdiction isolate it. General Sir Harold Alexander surrenders the expedition when no Dunkirk-style evacuation is possible. The Algiers Capitulation becomes Britain's Gallipoli, “African Dunkirk,” or “Dunkirk Without the Boats.” Exact date and order of battle remain open. |
| 1944 | Canon | Franklin D. Roosevelt wins a fourth term. James F. Byrnes is the selected anti- or post-New-Deal vice-presidential figure in this branch. |
| Late 1945–early 1946 | Canon | Stalin is removed and executed by an emergency party-state conspiracy. Malenkov, Molotov, Beria, Zhukov, and Khrushchev form a collective survival leadership; Khrushchev becomes the public advocate of ending Stalin's ruinous resistance. |
| 1945–1946 | Canon | Moscow, Leningrad, and Stalingrad cease to function as viable Soviet strategic centers. The Ural settlement ends Soviet sovereignty west of approximately the Urals and permits evacuation of current populations, factories, machine tools, rolling stock, archives, stores, and military formations eastward. Germany accepts a thinned and deindustrialized Lebensraum frontier it can actually supply. |
| Late 1945–1947 | Canon | The Second Battle of Britain escalates from pressure into full offensive. Arado jets attack radar, communications, airfields, depots, factories, and naval bases; German and British jets fight over Dover; limited Me 264 forces strike and reconnoiter northern and Scottish targets. The RAF survives but moves depth north and loses secure use of the southeast. |
| 1946–1947 | Canon | British exploitation of the German naval cipher becomes obvious through desperate convoy routing and U-boat attacks. Dönitz replaces it with a more secure electromechanical/rotor architecture developed ashore with assistance from Konrad Zuse's computational work. Britain loses its decisive cipher advantage. |
| Early 1947 | Canon | Congress and the Byrnes administration terminate American material support to Britain as “loans to a drowning man,” removing supplies, shipping replacement, spares, and the expectation of eventual rescue. |
| 1947 | Canon | Germany carries out **Unternehmen Friedensbrot**, the White Parachutes or Bread Raid: Me 264s reach London and drop food, medicine, and leaflets rather than bombs. Crowds move on Westminster. George VI refuses personal rule and refuses mass violence against the demonstrations. Coalition MPs withdraw confidence from Churchill, while the legally surviving ministry remains long enough to negotiate peace. |
| 1945 | Canon | Roosevelt dies. Byrnes becomes president and begins narrowing the New Deal emergency state while the United States remains formally outside the war. |
| 1945 | Canon | The United States seizes or secures Greenland. Greenland later receives nominal independence but remains, in practice, an American strategic base. |
| 1945–1947 | Canon | Nuclear weapons are not developed into a usable wartime system. Atomic research remains limited and later turns toward power and industry. |
| 1945–1947 | Canon | German forced labor continues through the end of the war. The later Reich conceals or sanitizes this system from foreign audiences. |
| 1947 | Canon | Portugal hosts the **Lisbon Conference** without the United States. Germany, Italy, and Japan impose a restrained settlement designed to end the naval war, secure existing conquests, and preserve Britain as a solvent counterweight rather than occupy it. Britain is defeated but retains the Crown, Parliament, Royal Navy, dominions, most Atlantic/sub-Saharan possessions, the Gulf system, Aden, Ceylon, and initially Burma. |
| 31 Dec. 1947 | Canon | The armistice naval ledger records 2,588 surviving major combatants among the five principal navies. Afloat totals are: United States 25 capital ships, 13 carriers, 47 cruisers, 145 destroyers, 60 escorts, and 125 submarines; Britain 11, 24, 43, 168, 260, and 74; Germany 5, 2, 11, 48, 64, and 820; Italy 6, 2, 18, 58, 88, and 82; Japan 10, 15, 37, 126, 105, and 96. Operational totals are lower, particularly Germany's 310 front-line U-boats. |
| Late 1947 | Canon | Hitler and Eva Braun enter a discreet legal marriage before **Wolfgang Friedrich Hitler** is born and becomes the regime's *Siegeskind*. The marriage is publicized only as necessary to establish household and succession legitimacy. |

## Immediate settlement, 1947–1949

| Date | Status | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Germany consolidates rule to the Urals, begins or intensifies German settlement, deportation, forced-labor, and colonial administration in the east, and supervises dependent European states. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Poland is not restored. Germany retains annexed western Polish territories and the General Government around Kraków. Expulsions from Poznań/Warthegau, Danzig/West Prussia, and other settlement zones crowd Poles into the General Government or German forced-labor systems. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Finland receives Karelia, Murmansk, Nenets territory, and an Arctic custodial zone reaching toward the Urals. German mountain troops and Finnish forces man parts of the frontier. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Japan formalizes the Nanjing government as the recognized government of China, begins reorganizing the Russian Far East, and keeps Vladivostok/Urajio as a separate maritime protectorate. Kamchatka and Chukotka later remain inside the single Far Eastern State rather than becoming sovereign clients. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon/working borders | France is divided into four political forms: German-client northern France centered on Paris; Italian-aligned Occitania centered on Toulouse; nominally independent but German-guaranteed Brittany; and the underground/exile claim to a united France. Germany annexes Alsace-Lorraine and transfers Pas-de-Calais to German-aligned Belgium. Exact Breton and Occitan borders remain open. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Yugoslavia is abolished. Italy controls or annexes the Adriatic/Dalmatian coast, Greece, and Montenegro and favors Croatian/Bosnian clients; Serbia is reduced; Slovenia is split; Hungary receives Vojvodina/Bačka/Baranja and other northern gains; Macedonia is contested or pressed toward Bulgaria. Germany dominates the Danube and interior while Italy dominates the Adriatic and Aegean. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Hungary receives all Transylvania but inherits a large hostile population and deeper German dependence. Romania loses Transylvania, receives eastern compensation and an Odessa-oriented sphere, and remains dependent on Germany for oil, rail, and military policy. Berlin preserves the dispute as leverage over both. |
| 1947 | Canon | The Lisbon settlement transfers Malta, Cyprus, Egypt/Suez strategic control, Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, Syria-related claims, and Tunisia to the Italian system; Malaya, Singapore, Hong Kong, and selected Pacific islands to Japan; and a smaller Malayan concession or base to Siam. Italy's Nile–Red Sea corridor now includes Sudan. |
| 1947 | Canon | Gibraltar is not ceded at Lisbon. A debt clause offers cancellation of a major share of British obligations if London sells the Rock to Spain. The government accepts quickly because Gibraltar is isolated and the relief makes the settlement survivable, creating the “sold Rock” and “banks own Parliament” myths. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Suez remains open to international commercial shipping, but Italy controls its strategic environment and extracts substantial tolls, concessions, preferences, or political leverage. Britain abandons Suez as a dependable strategic artery and reorganizes imperial planning around the Atlantic–West Africa–Cape–Indian Ocean route. |
| 1947 | Canon | Germany forms Reichskommissariat Mittelafrika with ceremonial capital at Buea. Belgian and French evacuation, record destruction, and German loyalty purges create an administrative vacuum; German remains the permanent official language. German officials seize Belgian and SYNEBA Congo-development archives and compel German-aligned Belgium to supply engineers and records. |
| 1947–1949 | Canon | Pierre Van Deuren's inherited Lower Congo concept becomes the **Van-Deuren-Plan**. The 1948 **Kongo-Ausbaukommission** studies Inga-only, regulated Lower Congo, and full-basin alternatives. Speer prefers the middle course, but Hitler chooses the maximal **Gesamtplan zur Regulierung und Erschließung des Kongostroms**, commonly the **Kongo-Gesamtwerk** or **Kongowerk**. |
| 1948–1952 | Canon | The **General Registration** and **Counting Wars** combine census coercion, translation failure, village massacres, strategic clearances, early Kongowerk surveys, and the first great refugee wave into British Africa. Former colonial clerks, missionaries, and French-speaking personnel remain indispensable despite racial doctrine. |
| 1949–1953 | Canon | Kongowerke AG opens survey roads, quarries, camps, preliminary Inga works, registration districts, and clearance corridors. The infrastructure project and colonial census become institutionally inseparable. |
| 1953–1959 | Canon | Construction advances at the **Inga-Werke**; Lower Congo transport is improved; the practical capital moves onto high ground above the Léopoldville–Brazzaville complex as **Kongostadt**; major urban relocation begins. The African Language and Mediation Service, Tribal Cadastre, recognized communities, and Katanga technical estate establish the early Mosaic Order. |
| 1959–1965 | Canon | The **Malebo-Sperrwerk**, located immediately below Pool Malebo, closes in stages. A restricted initial reservoir forms while generation, ports, channels, and high-ground urban districts enter service. Old low-lying sections of Léopoldville and Brazzaville are protected, cleared, or abandoned. |
| 1965–1969 | Canon | Reservoir water spreads into the western Cuvette. New ports, dredging, resettlement, and paper-to-computer **MOSAIK** administration struggle to follow the changing shoreline. Wolfgang's 1966 review fixes a final operating band of roughly **330–335 metres**, stage-gates funding, separates accounts, and prioritizes control, power, transport, health, communications, and resettlement over monumental expansion. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | Britain chooses to retain the empire. The new Cape strategy raises the importance of West Africa, South Africa, Rhodesia, Aden, the Gulf, Ceylon, and Indian Ocean stations. Dependence on South African ports and resources sharply reduces London's willingness to confront apartheid. |
| 1947–1949 | Canon/working borders | The Raj cracks into British-held ports and bases, Congress/Hindustan forces, competing Muslim successor projects, princes, communal armies, southern regional powers, and Japanese-backed movements. Britain fortifies Ceylon and selected Indian Ocean gates but never restores control of the whole subcontinent. |
| Aug.–Oct. 1947 | Canon | **Operation Homeward** returns British prisoners in bulk aboard carefully staged German and Italian ships under Royal Navy escort. A sergeant's “They treated us very well on the way home” is clipped to “The Germans treated us very well.” Emergency service-elector certificates give repatriated prisoners and rapidly demobilized personnel a lawful but administratively ragged vote. |
| 6 Nov. 1947 | Canon | The five- or six-week **White Parachute Election** overturns normal constituency geography. Bevanite Labour wins 218 seats, Conservatives 169, Mosley's Union Commonwealth 104, Acland's Common Wealth 82, Liberals 40, and regional/servicemen/other independents 27. Approximate votes are 30, 27, 18, 13, 8, and 4 percent. |
| Nov. 1947 | Canon | George VI asks Aneurin Bevan to form a minority Reconstruction Ministry. Common Wealth and Liberals permit the King's Speech and emergency supply without entering Cabinet; Conservatives cooperate on demobilization, shipping, and defense; UC does not obstruct veteran and maritime reconstruction. The informal **Reconstruction Truce** or **November Understanding** begins the Parliament of Strangers/Brick Parliament. |
| 1947–1948 | Canon | The Release and Resettlement Scheme uses immediate, training, and reserve release. The civilian **National Reconstruction Corps** or Brick Corps employs demobilized personnel, former prisoners, workers, women, apprentices, and Commonwealth volunteers in rubble clearance, housing, utilities, schools, hospitals, transport, ports, airfields, and later imperial works. |
| 1948 | Canon | The **Merchant Fleet Renewal Act** and Commonwealth Standard Ship Programme implement “Ships are bread”: state finance, guarantees, charters, standardized designs, private operators, and Dominion orders rebuild cargo, tanker, refrigerated, bulk, repair, personnel, and feeder fleets around the Cape route. |
| 1948 | Canon | The **Windsor Arrangement** permits the Duke of Windsor a controlled part-time return and veterans' role in exchange for permanent exclusion from succession and constitutional office, no partisan activity or rival court, and Palace control of foreign contacts and speeches. |
| 1949 | Canon | The Brick Corps peaks around 250,000–300,000 direct workers. The **Strategic Industries and Arsenal Continuity Act** sustains shipyards, ASW escorts, fleet submarines, minesweepers, light/escort carriers, aircraft, radar, signals, ammunition, and merchant auxiliaries rather than a symmetric prestige-fleet race. |
| 1950 | Canon | The compromise **National Health and Family Security Act** establishes universal essential care, regional hospital finance, maternal/child services, veteran rehabilitation, subsidized medicine, public health, and vaccination while keeping most doctors independent and preserving the identity of voluntary, municipal, religious, and charitable hospitals. |
| 1950 | Canon | The **Commonwealth Council of Defence and Reconstruction**, ceremonially chaired by George VI, gives Dominion ministers, service chiefs, and shipping, financial, and technical advisers a permanent information and consultation forum without power to command governments or appropriate money. |
| 1947–1949 | Canon/working labels | Germany establishes the Northern Shield: an occupied-aligned Norwegian fortress, economically subordinated but formally sovereign Sweden, tightly controlled Denmark, Germanized Baltic frontiers, and an enlarged Finland burdened with the Karelian and Arctic custodial zone. |
| 1947–1949 | Canon/working labels | Japan regularizes its differentiated empire: integral Korea and Taiwan, Manchukuo's industrial monarchy, Nanjing's confederal republic, separate Indochinese kingdoms, regional East Indian states, the Singapore commercial hub, associated Thailand, and Pacific/northern clients. Britain initially retains Burma; Japanese predominance there comes through the 1948–53 war and Rangoon Settlement. |
| 1947–1950 | Working canon | The Palestine War follows Britain's retreat from the Mandate and mass Jewish refugee movement from German Europe. A reduced Israel survives on the Tel Aviv–Haifa coast and in part of Galilee; Jordan takes the Arab highlands, Jerusalem, the Jordan Valley, Gaza, and most or all of the Negev under the Amman Settlement. Exact borders remain open. |
| 1948 | Canon framework | Germany carries out the **Reichswährungsbereinigung**, protecting current wages and modest German savings while converting large balances, government debt, and foreign clearing claims. The selective default clears the monetary overhang for reconstruction. |
| 1948 | Canon framework | Japan's **Imperial Financial Rectification** stabilizes the yen, converts wartime debt and large balances, and begins the layered imperial yen and clearing system. |
| 1948 | Canon framework | Britain's **Sterling and Commonwealth Reconstruction settlement** consolidates debt, blocks or converts overseas balances, preserves exchange controls, and formalizes the controlled sterling area and Commonwealth development system. |
| 1948–1950 | Canon framework | Donato Menichella, backed politically by Giuseppe Volpi's circle, imposes the **Imperial Stabilization of the Lira**: blocked large balances, long reconstruction bonds, contracted credit, price discipline, imperial circulation and clearing, and IRI-led reconversion. It is a hard stabilization, not mass redenomination or printing-led recovery. |
| 1948 | Canon | A four-way American election pits Dewey Republicans, Byrnes/Recovery Democrats, a Progressive-New Deal-Labor ticket, and National Renewal against one another. Thomas E. Dewey and Earl Warren win without a landslide. |
| 1948 | Working | The first large postwar deportation and resettlement waves reshape former Poland, the German east, Siberia, and Central Asia. |
| 1949 | Canon | Dewey takes office. The **National Recovery and Enterprise Act** or equivalent restores investment through tax, credit, infrastructure, labor, and strategic-industry measures while burying the New Deal as a governing brand. |
| 1949 onward | Canon | Germany begins a sophisticated foreign-propaganda offensive aimed at American workers, business, Catholics, racial minorities, isolationists, and National Renewal. Its operations remain unusually clean and difficult to expose. |

## The first postwar decade, 1950–1955

| Date | Status | Event |
|---|---|---|
| c. 1950 | Canon | The American economy experiences a mild recovery-era slowdown, then increasingly uses strategic infrastructure and defense procurement—“Fortress Fordism”—without restoring New Deal language. |
| Early 1950s | Canon | A Japanese influence scandal in the Philippines allows Washington to redefine independence activism as foreign subversion. The Philippines receives “supervised sovereignty”: elected institutions with American bases, security rights, and intervention authority. |
| Early 1950s | Canon | Pro-American Philippine elites gain disproportionate power. Nationalist opposition radicalizes as peaceful demands are dismissed as Japanese manipulation. |
| 1948–1949 | Canon | The **Breaking of the Raj** follows the failed all-India succession conference. Bengal, Punjab, princely states, Congress and Muslim authorities, Sikh formations, Azad Hind, militias, and local armies divide the state. Britain's Operation Tideway evacuates Calcutta; the British-protected Burmese rear area enters the Companion War. |
| 1949–1952 | Canon | Britain adopts **Bastion India**. Colombo and Trincomalee command a maritime system of Ceylon, Maldives, mainland defense leases, and southern or western clients. The BIEC fields roughly 45,000–65,000 direct field and garrison personnel; theater-wide British and Commonwealth strength approaches 90,000–110,000. |
| 1952–1956 | Canon | The Indian Wars reach their first proxy high tide. Britain, Japan, and smaller German networks supply clients, advisers, volunteers, and weapons. Madras, Mysore, Travancore–Cochin, and the Maratha States Union stabilize as British-aligned but non-British states. The Rangoon Settlement ends British protection of Burma in 1952–53, but the 1954–56 Federal Crisis proves that its union remains unstable. Burma's breakdown helps stop Azad Hind's last plausible final offensive. |
| 1956–1961 | Canon | Exhaustion replaces victory. The **Colombo Armistice System** freezes ceasefire lines, transit, port, minority, pilgrimage, customs, and foreign-troop questions without creating one India. The region remains divided among Congress India, Hindustan, Azad Hind, TPS, Hyderabad, Rajputana, Khalistan, Kashmir, Sindh, western successors, British-aligned southern states, and unrecognized districts. |
| c. 1950–1951 | Working canon | The Amman Settlement ends the main Palestine War. Israel and enlarged Jordan emerge; Britain retains security and transit relationships, while Jerusalem's holy places receive international guarantees. |
| Early–mid 1950s | Canon framework | Italian Syria becomes a route for influence into Iraq, Palestine, and Transjordan. Pro-Axis and revolutionary officers threaten the British-backed Hashemite order in Baghdad, making Iraq the central coup and oil crisis of Britain's Middle Eastern system. |
| Early–mid 1950s | Working | Encircled Turkey preserves formal independence by exploiting German–Italian rivalry. A proposed Straits Security Settlement would admit Axis passage, observers, and economic privileges while excluding Anglo-American military missions; its date and terms are not yet canon. |
| Early 1950s | Working | Spain begins an earlier technocratic opening and industrial recovery because victory prevents its historical diplomatic isolation. Portugal deepens its Atlantic and colonial strategy as the Azores, Madeira, Angola, and Mozambique become more valuable to competing route systems. |
| Early–mid 1950s | Canon framework | Africa becomes Britain's primary imperial theater. South Africa and Rhodesia gain leverage as the Cape hinge; German Mittelafrika expands extraction and repression; British West Africa absorbs refugee flows; and Portuguese colonies act as buffers between the rival systems. |
| 1950s | Canon | China remains nominally unified under Nanjing. Japanese campaigns gradually suppress the largest Communist bases. Provincial forces continue to use obsolete Japanese equipment for gendarmerie and anti-rebel work. |
| 1950s | Canon | Japan channels China toward agriculture, mining, and low-level support industry while accelerating home-island education, factories, assembly lines, and automation. |
| 1950s | Canon | Germany and Japan confront chronic manpower shortages and accelerate mechanization and industrial automation. |
| 1952 | Canon | Dewey wins a narrow reelection. Charles Lindbergh heads the National Renewal ticket, wins at least Minnesota, and gives the party durable electoral legitimacy. |
| 1952 | Canon | Japan promulgates the **Imperial Armored Materiel Designation Standard**. Existing wartime names remain valid, but new projects receive standardized functional prefixes; `Shu` identifies main battle tanks, `Yu` armored transports, and `Ho` infantry combat vehicles. |
| 1956 | Canon | Japan creates the **Imperial Joint Aircraft Register**, standardizing public service names as Type year + role + name while preserving Army `Ki-` and Navy letter-number-manufacturer development codes. |
| Oct. 1951 | Canon | The **India Election** gives Conservatives 239 seats, Labour 224, UC 94, Common Wealth 41, Liberals 25, and others 17. R. A. Butler forms a Conservative ministry through a Commonwealth Supply Agreement with UC, accepting Bastion India, planning machinery, shipping and ASW programs, veteran guarantees, and the social settlement while refusing UC Cabinet office or corporative government. |
| 1951–1955 | Canon | The first Butler ministry governs by **“Contracts, not commands.”** It defines defensible Indian obligations, builds the Contract Commonwealth, expands technical education and nuclear power, winds down the Brick Corps, and restores ordinary constitutional administration. |
| 1952 | Canon | George VI dies and Elizabeth II succeeds. |
| 2 June 1953 | Canon | Elizabeth II is crowned before delegations from every major bloc. Edward and Wallis attend surrounding family events, but Edward does not sit in the Abbey: restored to the family, not the Crown. |
| 20 Oct. 1955 | Canon | Butler wins vindication: Conservatives 326, Labour 235, UC 48, Liberals 15, Common Wealth 8, and others 8. The twelve-seat majority ends UC supply dependence; Labour becomes the sole plausible alternative government. |
| 1951–1952 | Canon | Shigemitsu authorizes the compartmented Philippine Independence Assistance Program. Legal relief, scholarships, publishing, political finance, trade, and radios precede covert weapons; armored components and cadres move through Davao under the Siam Formula. |
| Feb.–Mar. 1953 | Canon | A lorry accident at Panabo exposes armored components. Raids uncover the **Davao Papers**, Japanese technicians, arms inventories, and a draft association treaty. Washington and the associated government arrest nationalist leaders and impose emergency powers. |
| 4 July 1953 | Canon | The **Luneta shootings** trigger army and constabulary defections and full nationalist revolt. Dewey sends American forces without a conventional declaration of war; Shigemitsu resigns and Kishi forms the Imperial Development and Security Cabinet. |
| 1953–1956 | Canon | The **Philippine War** becomes America's first postwar “police action.” Japan sustains the rebellion “one degree below war”; American and Japanese fleets nearly clash. America controls cities and wins most field battles but cannot make its client government legitimate. |
| 1953–1956 | Canon | The United States wins tactical battles but cannot make its client government legitimate. National Renewal demands maximal war and later claims vindication when limited intervention fails. |
| 1955–1956 | Canon | Refugee testimony from the Congo becomes an international scandal. Britain adopts emergency Commonwealth food support, frontier controls, and the **African Refugee and Frontier Act of 1956**. |
| 1957 | Canon | The **Accra Compact** establishes internally self-governing Associated Crown States under continuing Crown sovereignty or British external defense. The Gold Coast becomes the model; Nigeria federalizes, Southern Cameroons becomes a frontier territory, and East African common services deepen. |
| Mid-1950s | Canon | The American military learns expeditionary lessons from the Philippines: reliable light armor, tracked carriers, jungle logistics, and deployability matter more than extremely heavy European-style tanks. |
| 1949–1953 | Canon framework | Germany's first reconstruction plan converts war plants, expands housing and transport, and integrates imperial resources. Berlin formalizes the Verrechnungsmark clearing order and controlled currency hierarchy. |
| 1949–1957 | Canon framework | Japan restores civilian shipping, ends most home-island rationing, develops ports, steel, petroleum, power, housing, Korea, Taiwan, and Manchukuo through zaibatsu, banks, trading companies, and development corporations. |
| 1950s | Canon framework | Italy's IRI system drives steel, shipping, telecommunications, power, southern development, motorization, and the consumer boom; Britain rebuilds merchant shipping, housing, public utilities, exports, and the distributed Commonwealth industrial system. |

## The Warren transition and technological shock, 1956–1960

| Date | Status | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 1956 | Canon | A fractured American election goes to the House. Earl Warren is selected president. National Renewal again wins states and gains kingmaker legitimacy; Warren begins his term with a constitutional but politically damaged mandate. |
| 1956 | Canon | Japan adopts the 36.0-tonne **Type 16 Shu-Ha** as its first main battle tank and retires `Chi` as the family prefix for new principal tanks. |
| Jan.–4 July 1957 | Canon | The Baguio Truce leads to the **Manila Accords**. American withdrawal and a Philippine government of national reconciliation begin. |
| 12–26 June 1958 | Canon | Complete Philippine sovereignty is restored on 12 June. The Cebu Declaration of 26 June admits it as a Sovereign Associated Member under Japanese-led collective guarantee. American evacuation completes without Japanese entry into the withdrawal zones. |
| 1959–1960 | Canon | The Treaty of Cebu regularizes defense access, constitutional and religious guarantees, peso–yen clearing, reconstruction, training, and associated status. Laurel, Recto, and Tañada provide elder civilian legitimacy while fictional Mateo de Vera, Lena Magsalin, and Isidro Cabahug organize military, civic, and maritime networks. A normal elected nationalist government replaces the transition in 1960. |
| 1960 | Canon | Japan gifts the core Philippine **Independence Fleet**: IJN *Tama* becomes BRP *Lapu-Lapu*; *Shiranui*, *Kuroshio*, and *Hatsukaze* become BRP *Andrés Bonifacio*, *Antonio Luna*, and *Diego Silang*; *Chikubu*, *Shisaka*, *Habushi*, and *Kōzu* become BRP *Samar*, *Leyte*, *Palawan*, and *Mindoro*. Smaller transfers remain unnamed pending registry identification. |
| 1957 | Canon | Germany launches **Raumbote I**, the world's first artificial satellite. A polished retrospective documentary reveals selected development footage and establishes Germany's “from projectile to spaceship” propaganda tradition. |
| 1958 | Canon | Philippine-war experience and the German satellite lead Washington to create **United States Aerospace Command** for strategic interception, bombers, missiles, high-altitude research, and military space. The Army and Navy retain their organic air corps. |
| 1958 onward | Canon | America deliberately gives its public prestige program a reusable, pilot-centered **spaceplane** identity rather than merely copying German ballistic rockets. The operational objective is a true horizontal-takeoff, horizontal-landing, runway-to-orbit aircraft using integrated air-breathing and closed-cycle propulsion, not a booster-launched shuttle glider. |
| Late 1950s | Canon | Germany fields advanced jets, assault rifles, Panzer X-class main battle tanks, guided weapons, and increasingly modern submarines. Japan fields comparable imperial systems. America begins a hurried military modernization. |
| Late 1950s | Canon | Germany, Britain, America, and Italy are major computing competitors. Japan is initially behind but closing. |
| 1955–1959 | Canon | Butler's majority ministry deepens the Siege Commonwealth through merchant shipping, ASW, nuclear power, technical exports, Cape-route bases, Dominion bargaining, and the first unified response to Germany's satellite. |
| 1957–1958 | Canon | Germany's Raumbote I produces Britain's **Silent Overflight** shock. The Commonwealth Space and Signals Board and High Mast tracking network are established in 1958; Black Arrow and Blue Streak receive orbital authorization. |
| 1959–1960 | Canon | Labour returns narrowly under Aneurin Bevan, who preserves the space and strategic programs under civilian-scientific language. Bevan's death in 1960 brings Hugh Gaitskell to office. |
| 1956–1960 | Canon | The Great Depression is finally understood to be over in the United States, though prosperity lacks the victory glow and global consumer dominance of real history. |
| By 1960 | Canon | Victor Emmanuel III has abdicated from age. Umberto II has absorbed practical Italian leadership as Mussolini declines, turning Italy toward royal Fascism and Catholic corporatism. The Duce remains a one-man Mussolinian office rather than a hereditary title. |
| By 1960 | Working | Franco accelerates preparation of a restored Spanish monarchy. Portugal and Occitania debate their own succession and constitutional forms. |

## Long-running developments without fixed dates

### German-Italian split

The wartime Axis partnership gradually becomes a rivalry comparable in structural importance—not ideology—to the historical Sino-Soviet split. France/Occitania, the Balkans, the Mediterranean, Latin America, Catholic politics, and military technology are the principal friction points. The split becomes overt after Mussolini's decline and Umberto II's consolidation.

### Britain's route reconstruction

The loss of Gibraltar, Malta, Cyprus, Egypt, Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, and secure Suez access is a single continuing transition rather than unrelated concessions. From 1947 onward, budgets, bases, diplomacy, and colonial policy are reorganized around the Atlantic–West Africa–Cape–Indian Ocean route. This raises South Africa, Rhodesia, West Africa, Aden, the Gulf, Ceylon, Australia, and New Zealand in British priorities and makes every threat to the Cape or Gulf an imperial-system crisis. Commercial Suez transit continues under Italian tolls and political leverage, but British war plans assume it is unreliable.

### The French successor states

Northern France, Occitania, and Brittany consolidate separate institutions and postwar generations while an underground/exile France contests all three. German and Italian rivalry drives reciprocal rearmament in the north and south. Occitania gradually converts industrial strength into bargaining power against Rome; northern France acquires a German-supervised army; Brittany's formal independence remains constrained by German control of strategic Atlantic facilities.

### Northern and Balkan settlements

Germany's Northern Shield and Danubian client system persist through a mixture of formal sovereignty, economic integration, garrisons, and deliberately unresolved grievances. In the Balkans, German control of the interior and Italian control of the Adriatic/Aegean overlap rather than meeting at a clean border. Hungarian–Romanian rivalry, the Yugoslav successor map, Bulgaria, and resistance in Greece remain recurring pressure points.

### India

The Indian Wars break the Raj in 1948–49, harden around British maritime bastions in 1949–52, reach a proxy high tide in 1952–56, and subside through the 1958–61 Colombo Armistice System. Britain retains Ceylon, Maldives, mainland leases, and southern or western clients but no claim to govern the subcontinent. No single independent India replaces the Raj; later conflict occurs among the successor states and unrecognized districts.

### China

The Nanjing government survives under Japanese guardianship. Large Communist formations are defeated over the 1950s, but provincial dependence, local revolt, economic extraction, and deliberately limited industrialization persist into the 1960s.

### Siberia

No government reunifies the former Soviet interior. American Arctic aid keeps some successor administrations alive. German, Finnish, and Japanese frontier zones contain the region without fully governing it.

### Decolonization

The victorious imperial powers and defeated-but-imperial Britain prevent a clean global decolonization wave. Independence struggles become civil wars, associated-state bargains, fortified enclaves, and proxy competition rather than a single postwar process.
