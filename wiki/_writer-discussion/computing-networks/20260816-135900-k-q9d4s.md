The comparative account established how five institutional systems converged on Weltnetz without surrendering their platforms, applications, laws, or strategic networks. I have added the economic distinction implicit in that history: common carriage was valuable not only as a revenue-producing service but as a reusable capability which allowed existing machines, firms, banks, carriers, and institutions to reach a wider field through shared interfaces and gateways.

The addition does not treat interoperability as self-justifying. It proposes observable tests for new downstream use, qualified suppliers, outage survival, restoration, congestion, coverage, and service quality; requires shifted traffic to be distinguished from genuinely enabled activity; and keeps patents, certification delays, proprietary extensions, subsidies, queues, and displaced local investment visible. Bounded public direction may specify interconnection, continuity, coverage, emergency priority, and migration while firms retain construction, design, maintenance, services, and ordinary prices. The final account also treats German influence over standards and chokepoints, national backbones, unequal access, surveillance, censorship, and the identity of users shed during crisis as economic evidence rather than external commentary.
