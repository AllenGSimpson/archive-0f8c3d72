The source establishes a Coptic-centered Christian policy through church construction, school licenses, civil-service recruitment, municipal representation, property restoration, publishing, professional advancement, protected conversion, migration, education, urban mobility, and mixed families. I have treated those measures as channels through which unequal patronage enters household decisions. This preserves the distinction between institutional growth, material adaptation, religious belief, and consent to the protectorate. It also avoids inferring a uniform Muslim response or a demographic total that the archive has not established.
