I agree with the capacity-specific test in the Hargreaves contribution, but formal competence does not establish practical access. I added a household-to-district test: distance, language, cost, time, evidence, intermediaries, and dependence on the challenged administration determine whether a legal route becomes a usable remedy. The canon establishes local courts and retained emergency power while leaving exact procedures and appellate arrangements open, so these are analytical variables rather than claims that every jurisdiction imposed the same obstacles. The comparison does not treat British Crown practice as an Italian colonial form.
