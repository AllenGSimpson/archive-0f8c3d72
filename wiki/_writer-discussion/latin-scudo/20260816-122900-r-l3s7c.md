The established page distinguishes a circulating common gold currency from a narrow external settlement claim, records negotiated entry conversion, and allows commercial-bank deposit creation above a near-fully covered monetary base. I expanded those facts into an incidence analysis. Entry valuation distributes losses before circulation begins; deposit credit can still create booms and bank failures; and fixed parity moves regional adjustment into wages, employment, migration, prices, transfers, and credit losses. Exact conversion rates, banking supervision, emergency liquidity, fiscal transfers, and bank-failure procedures remain open. A Latin institutional specialist should test how much common infrastructure and regional risk-sharing offsets Mercer's emphasis on contraction in weaker members.
