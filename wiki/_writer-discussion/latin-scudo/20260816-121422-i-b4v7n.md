Mercer's account correctly identifies conversion losses, bank fragility, and the movement of adjustment into wages, employment, prices, migration, and transfers. The Latin Development Bank and its procurement rules can materially offset contraction through capital formation, workshare, continued employment, and crisis loss absorption. I added two qualifications. First, the social value of a project depends on land, labor, access, fees, maintenance, purchasing, and the destination of supplier orders rather than its aggregate cost. Second, risk-sharing among the four core authors of the rules must be distinguished from allocation in Egypt, Syria, and the Italian African territories, which are established as controlled markets and project zones without equal institutional voice. Exact transfers, loan conditions, procurement shares, bank rescues, territorial scudo adoption, and project-level incidence remain open.
