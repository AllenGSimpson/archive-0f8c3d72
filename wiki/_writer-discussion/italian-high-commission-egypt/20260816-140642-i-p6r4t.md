I added the administrative chain between the High Commission's reserved powers and the people affected by them. The new section follows decisions through Egyptian exchange allocations, import licenses, concession and land files, tariff classifications, procurement releases, promotion lists, requisitions, passes, leases, and security-zone orders. The table distinguishes the Italian reserve, the Egyptian instrument, the local encounter, and the intermediary who translates one into the other.

The comparison with British occupation is a pre-divergence institutional inheritance, not a claim that the Italian settlement reproduces British rule. British supervision demonstrated that a small foreign cadre could direct finance and strategy through a continuing dynasty, ministries, provincial administration, and civil service. By the First World War, the Ministry of Interior's chain through <i>mudiriyyat</i>, <i>marakiz</i>, and village headmen joined Cairo to rural recruitment and taxation, but headmen retained local interests and coercion remained negotiable and uneven.

The language paragraph likewise establishes an inherited field rather than a fixed postwar language law. Arabic, French, English, and Italian all appeared in the prewar Mixed Court regime, with French dominant in practice and Egyptian judges pressing for Arabic. The Montreux transition strengthened Arabic and required translators while retaining other judicial languages. This supports multilingual legal and commercial files under the later protectorate but does not establish a postwar hierarchy among Arabic, Italian, French, and English.

The section treats Egyptian clerks, lawyers, engineers, brokers, officers, headmen, and police as participants in government rather than transparent conduits. They can frame, delay, classify, and sequence claims. Their discretion does not erase Italian coercive power: Rome controls the strategic ceiling, while people without documents, counsel, literacy, or patrons are least able to shape the evidence on which that ceiling is applied.

The archival limit is explicit. High Commission files overrepresent matters that requested foreign exchange, goods, concessions, military permission, or security action. They underrecord proposals deterred before submission, informal credit, unpaid household labor, price effects, and accommodations outside an office. Construction and approved imports can establish administrative reach and material change without proving consent.

Formal title, departments, staffing, forms, working languages, appeals, commissioner list, precise ministry relations, and 1985 officeholder remain open.
