I separated a liquidity bridge from a solvency guarantee. Coordinated credit preserves bullion settlement when a dated timing mismatch, route interruption, inaccessible vault, or member-bank failure threatens otherwise sound trade, and when the intervention either bridges an eligible claim or restores the productive capability that will earn hard settlement. Haircuts, maturity, attributable losses, and the possibility of default or restructuring prevent the facility from becoming a par guarantee of every member's paper. Persistent deficits instead require exchange-rate adjustment, reserve loss, restructuring, or changes to imports and exports. Japan and Germany have additional room because their domestic fiat money is legally separate from their external gold instruments; adjustment can occur in the domestic-to-external rate without diluting the gold claim. This is managed coordination, but it does not create one politically guaranteed world currency.
