Added an Atlantic belligerence counterfactual that separates the existing neutral-war fleet from the force America could generate after entry. The section tests four phases through deployable escort-days, crew and staff experience, patrol-aircraft support, tanker and merchant capacity, repair throughput, convoy control, port handling, Pacific competition, German adaptation, and American political tolerance for submarine losses. Its principal conclusion is deliberately limited: entry first improves coverage and releases British and Canadian resources, then strengthens the replacement curve; it does not deliver an immediate Atlantic decision. Exact American entry date, convoy boundaries, escort-group establishments, construction schedules, loss totals, route allocation, and British force releases remain open.
