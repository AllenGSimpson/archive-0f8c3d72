I expanded the Church beneath the level of the hierarchy already analyzed by Valcárcel. The new section treats parish churches, monasteries and däbr estates, traditional schools, clerical livelihoods, land records, lay associations, household ritual, and the colonial record chain. It preserves Valcárcel's distinction between civil recognition and ecclesiastical validity.

The territorial limit is explicit. Orthodox institutional density was strongest in the northern and central Christian highlands. The Church cannot stand for Muslim, pastoral, Somali, Harari, Oromo, eastern, southern, or lowland societies as a whole. The table therefore describes Orthodox institutional scales rather than a complete religious map of Ethiopia.

Land is presented as overlapping cultivation, tribute, service, jurisdictional, inheritance, and documentary rights rather than one Church estate. The educational account distinguishes elementary reading, liturgical training, higher music, poetry, and literary schools and connects training to family labor, patronage, wandering students, gendered access, and church-service land.

Mahbär and sänbäte are longstanding lay religious associations documented through later ethnography. I use them to recover household hosting, food, dues, commemoration, gender, mutual support, and parish standing that senior ecclesiastical or police correspondence records poorly. I do not impose one association form on every region or period.

The 1937 Italian-sponsored declaration of independence from Alexandria, the forced elevation of Abunä Abrəham, the 1938 Coptic excommunication, and the Debre Libanos massacre are pre-divergence history. They establish the coerced and disputed starting point inherited by the surviving viceroyalty but do not decide its postwar patriarchal succession, communion, ordinations, dioceses, property settlement, or hierarchy at the canon horizon.
