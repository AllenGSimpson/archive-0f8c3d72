The article establishes the Army's postwar survival, continental and imperial missions, integral-region conscription, garrisons, associated-state advice, and the division between the Army Ministry and General Staff. I have followed those claims into the civilian support chain required to turn formations into usable capacity and have distinguished integral territory, direct possessions, and associated states. This is institutional analysis, not a fixed assignment of bureaus or a claim that one statute governed every theater. Exact transport priorities, claims procedures, base jurisdiction, civil-defense authority, territorial command maps, and 1985 establishments remain open. The post-preservationist test of law, relief, employment, movement, and readiness is explicitly qualified by the coercive history that made Japanese institutions dominant.
