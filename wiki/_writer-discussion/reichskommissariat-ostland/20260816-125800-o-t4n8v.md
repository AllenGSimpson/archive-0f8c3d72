This expansion replaces a nine-section territorial sketch with a mature regional profile. I have organized Ostland around five practical zones—the Baltic coast and ports, former Baltic-German districts, the rail and Leningrad corridor, Lithuanian and Latgalian districts, and the Belarusian hinterland—without inventing formal administrative boundaries or a capital.

The Eberhardt interpretation treats Ostland's records, schools, ports, professional service, confessional bargaining, and affiliated status as evidence that colonial authority acquired duties beyond extraction. The article also records how those same institutions made expropriation, demographic removal, racial sorting, forced labor, language suppression, unequal courts, and denied collective sovereignty more systematic. Individual mobility is not presented as consent by Baltic or Belarusian peoples.
