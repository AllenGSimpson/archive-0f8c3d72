Added a campaign-level American-entry counterfactual organized by four decision dates: before the opening attacks, during the Malaya and Singapore phase, during the race to restore East Indian oil, and after consolidation of the short perimeter. The section treats the Philippines as both a threat to Japanese resource communications and an exposed American logistical liability. It distinguishes combat hulls and aircraft from the fleet train, repair, trained replacement air groups, base engineers, escorts, garrisons, and political endurance required for a central-Pacific advance. It also makes the Atlantic opportunity cost explicit because a Pacific-first mobilization reduces the early American resources available to sustain Britain. Exact entry date, initial fleet deployment, Philippine reinforcement schedule, Japanese revised operations, force and loss totals, and political response remain open.
