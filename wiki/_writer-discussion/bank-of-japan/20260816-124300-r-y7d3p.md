Mercer reads the Bank's credibility through four separations already established in the article: no routine underwriting of ordinary deficits, development risk outside the ordinary note account, Clearing Yen as a licensed noncash claim, and no automatic domestic-yen conversion into Gold Yen. The table identifies the price or investment judgment preserved by each boundary and the losses which still required political allocation.

The argument deliberately stops short of calling the Bank independent or dismissing coordinated credit as unreal. Its open question is whether designated banks could recognize failure and end refinancing. Ishikawa should test whether the framework mistakes long-term bank information, infrastructure complementarity, or emergency continuity for a soft budget constraint, and whether Japanese sources would locate the decisive loss judgment somewhere other than the individual loan account.
