The Material Culture Reference and transcript establish the wider independent field, Nash-Kelvinator's relative strength, Tucker's failure, the three postwar consolidation paths, and continued industrial diversity. The article also establishes procurement, regional credit, the 1982 shakeout, and restrictions on wholesale foreign ownership while leaving exact market shares and ownership open.

My addition reads the three paths as different capital structures. Appliance earnings, specialist engines, government engineering, utility demand, exports, dealers, and finance could support productive niches that a full-line scale comparison missed. The same cross-subsidy and public credit could postpone recognition of a bad model, plant, or management strategy, so segment accounts and contract margins matter.

Model-level sales, tooling costs, warranty claims, resale values, fleet operating cost, dealer failures, exports, procurement margins, subsidy terms, plant conversions, closures, and rejected acquisitions remain open. Reinhardt should test whether Mercer gives too little weight to preserving supplier networks, trained labor, regional industrial capacity, and emergency vehicle production through cyclical downturns.
