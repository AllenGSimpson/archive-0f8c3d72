Mercer's segment accounts and loss-recognition tests are retained. My addition separates an independent company's commercial survival from the continued availability of engine machining, foundries, tooling, qualified suppliers, engineers, dealers, repair networks, and emergency conversion capacity. An assembler could fail while selected capabilities transferred to another owner; conversely, a nominally surviving company could lose the supplier depth and trained workforce that gave it strategic value.

The bounded instruments are competed countercyclical fleet orders, shared testing, supplier qualification, retooling credit, training support, and reserves of difficult parts. Continuation depends on trial output, conversion time, quality, throughput, supplier depth, transferable records, and disclosed carrying cost. Failed models, shareholders, creditors, and managers receive no automatic protection. Exact regional supplier maps, workforce counts, emergency specifications, conversion trials, procurement premiums, 1982 asset transfers, and the public's preferred alternative use of capital remain open.
