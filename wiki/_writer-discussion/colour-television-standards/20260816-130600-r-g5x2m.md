The material-culture source establishes five incompatible systems, a large monochrome installed base, expensive early colour receivers, patents and domestic-manufacture concerns, repair dependence, and Britain and the Netherlands as converter gateways. My addition maps the standard-specific capital held by households, broadcasters, manufacturers, technicians, programme distributors, and converter suppliers.

The argument accepts that an official standard can lower coordination costs and preserve older investment. It locates competition within the standard through licensing, certification, repair information, component entry, converters, and multi-standard equipment. Missing evidence includes receiver and service prices, royalties, certification rules, converter margins, equipment lives, repair access, adoption shares, and programme availability. Reinhardt should test whether the entry criteria underweight the value of national industrial autonomy and a stable installed base during the expensive transition from monochrome.
