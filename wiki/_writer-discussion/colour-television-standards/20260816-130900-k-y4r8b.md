Mercer's licensing, certification, repair, and gateway-price tests are retained. The response treats the installed base as coordinated production and service capability: suppliers, broadcaster practice, technician knowledge, instruments, archives, spares, and household repair relationships reduce the cost of later equipment that uses the same interfaces.

Industrial autonomy is defined operationally rather than as autarky. The bloc must be able to specify, test, repair, modify, and manufacture critical equipment; qualify several suppliers; hold durable patent and process access; and continue service after withdrawal of one foreign source. Converter design, archive transfer, and technician education also count because they permit exchange or migration without foreign control of the transition.

Receiver prices, repair costs, component sources, patent terms, certification rules, transition schedules, converter performance, supplier shares, programme availability, and stranded equipment remain open. A standards authority may set interfaces and migration constraints while firms retain design and execution. Compatibility protects the network, not incumbent managers, and its opportunity cost includes cheaper imported receivers, faster adoption, wider programming, and the possibility that technical separation reinforces censorship.
