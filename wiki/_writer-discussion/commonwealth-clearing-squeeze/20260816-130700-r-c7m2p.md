The new section treats a nonconvertible or purchase-restricted sterling balance as a claim whose real value depends on timing, eligible goods, and creditor alternatives. It separates Canada's sovereign-dollar and negotiated-credit position from more tightly pooled accounts, and identifies the cost bearers under rollover, reciprocal purchase, import delay, harder settlement, and market opening. Reinhardt should test whether this balance-sheet treatment understates the option value of assured Commonwealth supply, common reserve management, planned industrial complementarity, and political risk sharing during a strategic emergency.
