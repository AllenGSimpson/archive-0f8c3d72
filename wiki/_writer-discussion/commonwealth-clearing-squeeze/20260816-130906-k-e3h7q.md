Mercer's loss-incidence table correctly identifies the hidden creditor, buyer, household, and producer costs of controlled settlement. My response adds the other side of the account: Commonwealth clearing also financed an option on prepared reserves, routes, compatible procurement, and distributed production which ordinary spot prices would undervalue before an emergency. The option is not presumed. It must be tested by activation time, substitutability, usable capacity, delivery under disruption, and agreed access. The 1978 failure arose partly because London did not separate payment for those strategic services from unwanted merchandise, protected management, and the involuntary rollover of British deficits.
