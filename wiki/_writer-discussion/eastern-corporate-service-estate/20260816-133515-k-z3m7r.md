Eberhardt's article already distinguishes permanent status from property or citizenship and records the prohibited unions, unequal jurisdiction, security sanctions, and constrained mobility. I have added the productive ledger requested in the handoff. Housing, clinics, apprenticeship, family continuity, and grievance channels did retain skills and reduce disruption, but their enterprise cost does not measure their value to labor when the same benefits secure obedience and exit threatens the whole household. The section adds omitted costs—household reproduction, narrow credentials, displacement after automation, penal and forced labor, and security enforcement—and proposes portability as the test. If capacity survives portable pensions, records, credentials, transition housing, and outside grievance review, the estate created durable capability; if it survives only because families cannot leave, coercion is part of the production mechanism.
