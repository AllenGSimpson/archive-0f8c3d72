The established article identifies low official prices, ration exclusion, diversion, smuggling, official protection, enforcement, and black markets as sources of exploitation and survival. I expanded its “second price system” into an effective-price account that includes registration, queues, quality, payment media, confiscation risk, and political access. The policy paragraph tracks how procurement prices, transferable claims, legal market access, and carrier competition redistribute costs among producers, registered urban consumers, public budgets, and excluded households. The final paragraph avoids treating an illegal price as automatically competitive or socially sufficient. Ishikawa should test how far food security, reserve management, and egalitarian ration priorities justify suppressing some of these signals.
