The new operational test accepts the canon audit's distinction between fixed American neutrality and reconstructed intervention schools. Every entry scenario is therefore presented as a counterfactual rather than as latent canon. The assessment begins from the documented July 1941 force and shipbuilding base, then imposes the setting's actual constraints: incomplete training, limited escorts and fleet support, a two-ocean requirement, French coalition resistance in North Africa, the need to route aid physically to the Soviet system, and the absence of Pearl Harbor as a source of political unity. The conclusion is intentionally conditional. Early and sustained belligerence could change British and Soviet endurance before it could produce an American continental army, but no familiar sequence of Torch, bombing, central-Pacific advance, and cross-Channel return is treated as automatic.
