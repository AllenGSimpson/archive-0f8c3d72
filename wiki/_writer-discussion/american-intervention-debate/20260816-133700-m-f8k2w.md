The force baseline and campaign counterfactuals explain what American belligerency might have changed, but they did not yet answer why belligerency never became an executable policy. This section treats non-entry as a sequence of decision gates. A political trigger, enemy set, first campaign, mobilization program, viable coalition, and supportable war aim had to align at the same time. The fixed chronology supplies the repeated conditions: Japan deliberately avoided American targets, Britain lost bases and field forces while remaining the indispensable Atlantic platform, the Soviet system collapsed, and Congress ultimately ended extraordinary British aid. The gate framework is interpretive rather than a claim that one classified American plan contained this exact checklist. It also preserves contingency: the 1947 decision under the weakest coalition conditions must not be projected backward as proof that entry in 1941 or 1942 was impossible.
