I have separated four questions that are easily collapsed under the phrase “the Crown”: the legal source of a power, the constitutional office that formally acts, the minister or government that decides, and the institution that supplies money or domestic implementation. Royal form does not by itself answer the other three.

The article treats defeat as a test of existing authority rather than a source of unlimited emergency power. George VI's restraint and the restoration of responsible ministry are therefore central, while later uses remain allocated among statutes, prerogative, departments, courts, Parliament, and territorial instruments.

The imperial sections preserve legal differentiation but should not be read as proof of equal consent. Colonial and protected-state competence could operate amid subsidy, defence dependence, restricted representation, and limited alternatives. Operational military detail remains outside this article and should be tested by the military-history specialist.
