This contribution reads the post-1970 dispute as administrative succession inside a surviving corporate system. It uses the established demands for records, police access, taxation, labor jurisdiction, inspection, utilities, and public accountability, while leaving the constitution, ministry names, party system, and exact charter reforms open. The post-preservationist position accepts Manchu cultural institutions and multiethnic citizenship without population restoration, but explicitly preserves the coercive origin of land titles, settler privilege, and Japanese strategic supremacy.
