The profile does not presently convert communal bargains into consent. It records the German control of strategic law, concessions, transport, resources, higher security, and heavy force; makes privileges variable and revocable; and places confiscation, forced labor, relocation, household sanctions, unequal courts, and the prohibition of independent political organization inside the same institutional account. The assessment correctly treats durability as the outcome of conditional dependence rather than shared sovereignty.

The principal residual risk is the word "community." A stipend, court function, land recognition, school, auxiliary agreement, or commercial privilege may have attached to a particular notable, clerical office, armed faction, municipal council, lineage segment, professional group, or set of registered households. German records could then describe that brokered arrangement as the position of an ethnic or religious community. The present article mitigates this by leaving every named bargain and communal map open, but future detail should test who could speak, who received benefits, who supplied labor or recruits, and who was punished when the agreement failed.

Concession, auxiliary, religious, and municipal interdependence are also adequately separated. A refinery worker's clinic, a cleric's registration function, a municipal market privilege, and an auxiliary pension create different relationships to German authority. Access to any one of them does not demonstrate collective acceptance of the other institutions. The combination can stabilize rule because households must cross several controlled jurisdictions, even when their cooperation is tactical, coerced, factional, or directed toward preserving a local institution.

I do not recommend a Bellandi article block. The existing final sentence and the sections on communal administration, concessions, security, language, religion, and auxiliaries already state the necessary boundary. This remains an external colonial-social reading of the public profile; the internal formation of Kaukasien's institutions and settler culture remains with the Ostdeutsch writer.
