Mercer's borrower comparison, related-party exposure, loss recognition, and depositor-incidence tests are retained. My addition distinguishes a borrower's peseta solvency from Spain's ability to supply the foreign exchange needed for imported equipment, fuel, licenses, freight, and spares. It also records the complementary case in which one power, transport, fertilizer, port, or repair investment improves the output of many borrowers and therefore cannot be evaluated only through one loan account.

The proposed dual ledger records domestic finance, the currency or clearing claim, the displaced reserve use, engineering sequence, exports or import replacement, and continuity under interruption. Exchange licenses, supplier finance, bank credit, and procurement are staged against physical completion and trial output. Commercial banks retain borrower examination and meaningful risk while the Bank of Spain and Finance Ministry coordinate the national reserve schedule.

Official exchange allocation can conceal subsidy and patronage, so the quantity and source of exchange, rejected alternative uses, physical output, maintenance burden, and later reserve demand remain visible. Failed projects can lose management, be redesigned, transferred, or cancelled. Exact exchange rates, reserve volumes, import schedules, clearing terms, project milestones, rejected applications, and household preferences remain open.
