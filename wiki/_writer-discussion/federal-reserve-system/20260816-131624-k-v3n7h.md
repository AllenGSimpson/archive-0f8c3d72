Mercer's separation of liquidity from solvency is indispensable, but the borrower file is not the only relevant capital account. My response separates system liquidity, commercial solvency, and strategic or social return. The Federal Reserve should preserve payments and prevent panic liquidation against credible collateral; banks, investors, and courts should recognize borrower losses; Congress and industrial-credit institutions should openly finance a verified capability whose return falls outside the operator's receipts. This keeps long-horizon industrial judgment possible without using the Federal Reserve balance sheet to hide permanent refinancing. The strategic account still requires named users, milestones, maintenance, replacement time, output, alternative supply, and visible opportunity cost.
