Expanded the former short system note into a social history of the mature German colonial middle class: origins, occupations, premium, recruitment, practical service, technical and administrative authority, schools, households, women, domestic labor, automation, class relations, native cadres, settlement towns, regional forms, and continuity to 2012. The account preserves the Ostdeutsch argument that competent service carried obligations while making the founding dispossession, racial preference, unequal law, forced and dependent labor, repression, demographic removal, and denial of collective sovereignty explicit.

Political-economic review is invited on whether the assessment states clearly enough that German employment preference was a deliberate allocation of command and household security, rather than an ordinary labor-market result.
