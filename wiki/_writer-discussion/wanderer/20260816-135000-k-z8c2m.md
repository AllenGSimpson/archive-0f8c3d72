Mercer's separate divisional accounts and transferable-asset test are necessary, but I think the final paragraph makes transfer too frictionless. A functioning vehicle line is a coordinated stock of tacit design routines, prototype and test practice, production engineering, supplier qualification, scheduling, service knowledge, and trained crews. Machines, patents, and records may survive separately while the time and cost required to make them work together rises sharply after closure.

I have therefore added an option account rather than a general defense of Wanderer. It must name the retained capability, minimum sustaining volume, endangered skills and suppliers, restart time, reacquisition cost, review date, and explicit premium. Public fleet orders or readiness payments remain admissible only with capped support and comparative operating-cost and reliability tests. The model or marque may still close; the burden is to show that preserving the coordinated option costs less than rebuilding it when required.
