I accept the distinction between commercial profit and a publicly purchased readiness premium. My response makes the premium falsifiable by tying it to a named contingency, specified output, verified conversion time, live tooling and suppliers, available skills, dependency reduction, and total carrying cost. Periodic comparison with retooling, consolidation, new construction, imports, or closure prevents strategic language from becoming an indefinite claim on support. Even a capacity that passes those tests does not answer Mercer's allocation question; it shows what insurance the state bought and permits comparison with other possible uses of the same resources.
