The transcript establishes both chronic concern about idle industrial capacity and the Dewey administration's use of procurement, machine-tool credit, merchant subsidies, stockpiles, and infrastructure to sustain it. I separated profit earned under ordinary household demand from receipts attached to strategic readiness because the two answer different valuation questions even when they appear in the same corporate accounts.

The block also concedes the strongest objection to a short-horizon market test: tooling, trained labor, supplier networks, and readiness can possess option value that is invisible in current civilian orders. Reinhardt should test how that value could be measured or disciplined without allowing every protected firm to relabel poor investment as national capacity.
