Eberhardt's description correctly establishes regulated retainers, subordinate auxiliaries, and central control of heavy weapons. The compressed presentation could still be read as one district force commanded personally by the governor. I have separated five functional layers: estate retainers, native police and auxiliaries, railway and infrastructure security, Reichskommissariat territorial or security formations, and Waffen-SS formations. The distinction follows control of recruitment, appointments, communications, ammunition, transport, heavy weapons, and cross-district assignment. It deliberately avoids a uniform organigram because the dedicated canon leaves civil, SS, railway, concession, and military subordination open. The military conclusion is that routine coercion was locally dense, while the equipment and mobile forces capable of overriding a district remained above the household level.
