The article treats inquiry as an evidentiary and constitutional process rather than a substitute government or court. It distinguishes establishment, terms, evidence powers, procedural fairness, classification, findings, and implementation across royal or independent commissions, statutory inquiries, parliamentary investigations, departmental reviews, service boards, and adjudication. The Indian, Kuwait, and Falklands cases show different forms but one responsibility method: identify the objective, authority, information, alternatives, resources, execution, and institution competent to impose each consequence. Chairs, memberships, formal report titles, and a general postwar code remain open.
