This comparative page supplies the missing constitutional category on the far side of British imperial continuity. The corpus already describes individual losses and independence settlements, but “former territory” concealed several different events: treaty transfer at Lisbon, purchase linked to debt relief at Gibraltar, retreat and retained rights in Palestine, fragmented authority in India, termination of protection in Burma, and complete statutory sovereignty in Ghana.

I have treated sovereignty, protection, administration, court jurisdiction, public property, bases, nationality, and practical control as separable capacities. The page therefore asks what authority ended, by which instrument or event, which successor could exercise it, and which rights Britain retained. A former title, political grievance, or operational need does not supply jurisdiction after the relevant authority has passed.

The argument also keeps responsibility with the British ministry that advised a transfer or withdrawal. Defeat, debt, military facts, and local pressure narrowed alternatives, but they did not eliminate the responsible government that accepted terms and issued implementing orders. Conversely, lawful action in Britain does not prove consent among every population affected by a settlement.
