The constitutional taxonomy is sound within its stated boundary. In particular, the distinction between a competent British instrument and local consent prevents treaty, statute, or purchase from becoming retrospective evidence that the governed population chose the result. I would preserve that distinction rather than importing an Italian colonial model into British Crown, mandate, or protectorate succession.

The page would nevertheless be misused if its forms were treated as complete social descriptions. For each transfer, later territorial articles should ask separately who could remain, return, move, inherit, register a marriage or birth, recover wages or pensions, hold land, use a port, and obtain a hearing. Flight, expulsion, military screening, missing documents, family separation, inaccessible courts, and the conversion of communal or customary tenure into successor-state title may turn an orderly instrument into a coercive household history without changing the instrument's legal classification.

Local institutions also require a separate ledger. Municipalities, village councils, religious courts and foundations, churches, mosques, customary authorities, unions, firms, and refugee committees might retain records, property, patronage, or practical jurisdiction while possessing no international title. Their survival does not make them sovereign; their exclusion from the negotiations does not make their claims socially negligible. A useful case study should therefore distinguish the government that received authority, the intermediaries through which it reached inhabitants, and the institutions through which inhabitants resisted, bargained, or remained administratively visible.

Finally, “bargaining” should identify the parties and their unequal alternatives. Agreement among London, a victorious power, a successor cabinet, or a recognized ruler may leave displaced residents and subordinate communities with no seat at all. The present responsibility-and-consent section marks this problem adequately for a comparative page. I recommend that the detailed articles carry the evidentiary burden through evacuation lists, ration rolls, land and tenancy files, school and parish or mosque registers, pension claims, court petitions, municipal minutes, labor records, and oral histories, while treating the colonial archive's enumeration of people as neither consent nor a full account of social life.
