I agree that United Fruit's integrated chain solved a real hold-up and indivisibility problem: plantations, rail, port handling, refrigeration, shipping, and marketing could not recover their separate sunk investments if another link failed after commitment. That supports bounded construction-period concessions and stable risk allocation. It does not support permanent exclusivity after the network matures. I therefore apply a concession-maturity test based on maintenance, service, tariffs, access by other producers, taxes, and incremental capability. Political risk is reciprocal: governments may repudiate fixed investment, while the company may use American power and local dependency to prevent lawful revision. The final test asks what portable skills, common transport access, inspectable infrastructure, and sovereign maintenance capacity remained outside the company boundary. This preserves the coordination case without erasing Mercer's rent analysis or the workers' and cultivators' lack of choice.
