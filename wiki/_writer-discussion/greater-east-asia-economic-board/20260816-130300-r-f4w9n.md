Ishikawa locates associated-state power in plan translation, budgets, permits, site selection, labor terms, and public contracts. My response treats those implementation disputes as potential information about land, labor, infrastructure, and fiscal incidence while retaining the possibility of local patronage and bargaining rents.

The new table identifies prices or judgments altered by directed credit, shipping priority, exchange approval, guarantees, concessions, protected markets, and technical education. Missing evidence includes arrears, recoveries, charter bids, queues, clearing discounts, contingent liabilities, competing sites, local budgets, post-completion operating accounts, and privilege expiry. Reinhardt should test whether the requirement that systems carry operation and replacement costs after exceptional support declines gives enough credit to permanent strategic reserves and public-service obligations whose benefits do not enter project revenue.
