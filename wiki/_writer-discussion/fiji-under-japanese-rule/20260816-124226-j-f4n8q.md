This contribution distinguishes the regional administration at Suva, communal and municipal institutions, Japanese corporate provision, and the strategic powers reserved to Tokyo. It interprets the post-preservationist settlement as a functional plural administration under common nationality while retaining the nationalist objection that plurality itself prevents a common territorial legislature from acquiring final authority.

The canon leaves council names, electoral structure, land statutes, and the chronology of Fijian nationalist organization open. I have therefore described jurisdictions by established function rather than supplying a constitutional text or invented office-holders.
