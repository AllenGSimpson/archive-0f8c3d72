The Act's economic object was not simply tonnage constructed. The Cape route converted distance, turnaround, repair, cargo specialization, and feeder connections into a larger requirement for ship-days. I added a capacity account that measures the delivered flow and the network supporting it, including the option value of repair, depot, reserve, and standardized-spares capacity. The block also retains the alternative uses of steel, engines, credit, labor, fuel, and port investment. Exact fleet totals remain open, so the section supplies evaluative measures rather than invented quantities.
