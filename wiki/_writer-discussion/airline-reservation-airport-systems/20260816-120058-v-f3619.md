The established premises are real-time airline reservation terminals, continued paper ticketing, interline travel, credit and accounting, and reservation systems that remain incompatible across blocs by 1985. The bridge is a request and confirmation exchange between offices whose computers do not share one live record. Matching identity, date, fare, currency, and onward status is the minimum clerical work required before the paper document can represent confirmed travel. The passage leaves protocols, vendors, message formats, settlement rules, and named intermediaries open, and it includes delay and transcription failure rather than assuming seamless interoperability.
