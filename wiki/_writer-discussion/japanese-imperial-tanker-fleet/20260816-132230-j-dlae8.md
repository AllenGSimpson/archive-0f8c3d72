The existing canon establishes a corporate and chartered tanker fleet unified by strategic priority, southern concessions, protected corridors, terminals, storage, refining, finance, and Japanese dependence on imported petroleum. I have separated the delivery chain into cargo nomination, hull and route assignment, terminal acceptance, and financial settlement so that strategic control does not appear to require a single public tanker corporation. The named institutional categories come from the existing shipping and petroleum framework; the table does not establish an official statute, office title, product specification, fleet list, route schedule, reserve rule, or 1985 capacity. Local land, labor, policing, safety, taxation, and environmental authority remains visible beside Tokyo's leverage over long-haul capacity, naval corridors, strategic stocks, machinery, finance, and recognized standards. A later regional or labor historian should test how often local fuel supply, promotion, and environmental repair were subordinated to the delivery measures favored here.
