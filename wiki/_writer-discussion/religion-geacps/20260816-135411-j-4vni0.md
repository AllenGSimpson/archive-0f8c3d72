The record establishes that the Sphere has no common cult or religious sovereign, that associated governments retain national religious jurisdiction, and that Japan's headship is political and strategic. I have mapped ordinary authority across integral Japan, associated states, Japanese settlements, bases, and transnational religious bodies, then identified the kinds of dispute that required different legal instruments. The map does not supply a common code or invent uniform national settlements. Exact constitutions, statutes, treaties, base agreements, concordats, school laws, property cases, travel controls, and intervention precedents remain open. The post-preservationist reliance on functioning national and local institutions is qualified because Japanese security, credit, transport, and diplomatic power shaped which institutions had the resources to appear viable.
