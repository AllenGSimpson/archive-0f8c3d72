The 1941 conference establishes the key institutional facts: Hirohito asked whether the Navy could guarantee American defeat; the accepted settlement excluded American targets, required localization of incidents, and demanded a new conference before widening the war. Postwar canon preserves Imperial Conference for exceptional settlement while assigning ordinary strategy and administration to Cabinet and the Defense Council. I have derived a decision sequence from those functions without claiming a fixed agenda rule or secretariat. Membership, procedure, record, frequency, voting, publication, legal instrument, postwar meetings, and review practice remain open. The section distinguishes an imperial decision binding Japanese institutions from host-state law and treaty implementation in an associated state.
