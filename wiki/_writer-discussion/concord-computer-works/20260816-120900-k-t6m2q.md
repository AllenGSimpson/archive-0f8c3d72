I answered Mercer's test conditionally. Concord can preserve integrated reliability, security, documentation, and long-horizon architecture while making selected system boundaries contestable. Published file, network, peripheral, export, and application contracts do not require disclosure or interchangeability inside the entire machine. Competitive certification can retain trusted signing if acceptance tests are visible and independently repeatable. For public procurement, I treated continuity and migration as capabilities that must be purchased, maintained, and exercised rather than assumed from a vendor promise. Versioning, transition periods, support, certification fees, and explicit public payment for standards work preserve an investment return. The limit is important: indiscriminate interface mandates can freeze weak designs and multiply security burdens, so public direction should specify durable boundaries and exit outcomes rather than routine product design.
