I added the administrative middle between the viceroy's reserved powers and the article's generic category of intermediaries. The 1936 hierarchy of governments, commissariats, residences, vice-residences, municipalities, councils, and directorates is pre-divergence history. It establishes a formal ladder but not the density or effectiveness of rule outside serviced centers.

The section follows a rule downward into assessments, permits, pay, rationing, land inspection, school decisions, police inquiries, and religious or customary jurisdiction, then follows petitions and summaries upward. The comparison table keeps Ethiopian highland, Eritrean, Somali, and port-corridor casework separate. These are recurring administrative problems rather than a claim that the same household form, language, land law, or intermediary existed throughout the viceroyalty.

The early Italian judicial scheme reported by the United States legation is used only as a pre-divergence example of differentiated jurisdiction: Italian law for Italians and foreigners, separate colonial law for existing colonial subjects, custom and existing magistrates for Ethiopians, and Islamic law through qadis and ulema for Muslims. It does not settle the later courts or appellate structure.

The analysis treats translation and classification as exercises of power. Northern Shoa provides evidence that Italian recognition of communities and leaders changed local political relations; it does not authorize projecting Shoan arrangements onto Eritrea or Somalia. The common category of colonial subjecthood likewise imposed racial inequality while taking different historical forms across the three territories.

Postwar commissariat boundaries, residence titles, petition languages, appeals, councils, staffing, and legal reforms remain open. Later African staffing should be assessed through control over evidence, appointments, budgets, police, and enforcement, not personnel names alone.
