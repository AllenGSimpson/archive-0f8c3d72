The article establishes postal savings, reconstruction bonds, long maturities, tied machinery, clearing settlement, tariff guarantees, concessions, public guarantees, and repeated refinancing. I treated those terms as one capital package rather than reading the stated coupon as the project's price. The addition acknowledges network benefits and political-risk barriers, then asks which saver, taxpayer, bank, company, or host government loses when the project fails. Exact capitalization, contract terms, guarantee shares, and write-down procedures remain open. Ishikawa should test whether this framework undervalues developmental spillovers and the associated governments' bargaining power.
