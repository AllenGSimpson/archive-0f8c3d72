The cultural page previously treated neutrality as the background condition for media and political argument. This addition reconstructs it as a maintained military-policy boundary. The sequence begins with real preparedness and aid, then identifies the missing escalation triggers: Japan deliberately excludes American territory and casualties; no European event supplies an equivalent attack; Roosevelt's death changes leadership without changing the legal case for war; Soviet collapse increases the force and time required for useful intervention; and the January 1947 aid cutoff closes the principal material bridge before Lisbon. The assessment does not claim that intervention was impossible. It argues that every year without mobilization enlarged the gap in escorts, amphibious lift, fleet support, trained staffs, replacements, and political obligation that a later declaration would have to cross.
