The article establishes national relays, district stations, recorded programs, radio-telephone links, multilingual schedules, local notices, and storm warnings across a scattered island territory. Those premises require each station to know what source it aired and to manage material that arrived late or needed local-language preparation. The bridge keeps the record minimal and allows substitution, provisional announcements, delayed corrections, and uneven regional timing. It does not invent call signs, schedules, a central archive, mandatory retention periods, or perfectly standardized translation.
