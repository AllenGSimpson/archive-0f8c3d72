The article previously described the components of long-war mobilization without identifying the military evidence from which the doctrine developed. I have added a comparative 1947 inheritance. Britain retained combat-adapted armed forces but had exhausted readiness, repair, shipping, and simultaneous global coverage. America retained more intact hulls and industrial potential but lacked the emergency escort, replenishment, amphibious, replacement, and combat-learning systems of a belligerent two-ocean force. Germany, Italy, and Japan carried different victory lessons shaped by occupation, coalition geography, and imperial support burdens. The comparison treats the 1947 settlements as the beginning of postwar military history and distinguishes authorized or afloat inventory from operational force and sustainable replacement.
