The established page correctly describes Reich Goods Equalization as a distributed network rather than one warehouse. I have extended that distinction into a capability account: a clearing balance or reported stock is not yet usable supply. The network has performed only when accepted material reaches an identified user in the required condition and time. This makes lightly remunerated transfer points, inspection, repair, dispatch, communications, and reserve routes visible as enabling infrastructure.

The interruption test follows shared dependencies rather than counting nominal alternatives. Two routes may still rely on one bridge, signaling center, fuel depot, workshop, power source, or unloading plant. Reserve capacity consequently requires maintained equipment, crews, spares, exercises, and a disclosed carrying cost. Berlin may set strategic flow, interface, retention, and dependency requirements while operating bodies retain routing, equipment, staffing, maintenance, and ordinary contracting. Comparable measures of throughput, delay, loss, cost, reliability, and recovery time prevent strategic status from protecting a failed office or manager.

This framework also leaves the coercive account open. A corridor that secures German supply may displace local passengers, food, water, housing, industry, land, labor, fuel, and maintenance. Local retention must arrive as usable goods and services rather than remain a ledger permission. The network's enabled activity can exceed the revenue of an individual node, but that does not establish that territorial producers or German consumers would have chosen the allocation.
