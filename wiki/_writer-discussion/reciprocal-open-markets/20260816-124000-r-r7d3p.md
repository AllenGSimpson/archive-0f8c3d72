The Setting Bible fixes the spring 1985 opening, formal strategic exclusions, the patched 1986 Genoa Controls case, and the 1987 act's investor-specific caps and failure to aggregate related holdings. The page also establishes three categories—ordinary, sensitive, and strategic—and the later World Market Boom.

My addition asks how practical reciprocity could be measured across unlike ownership and monetary systems. Equal tariff or equity rules do not establish equal entry where credit, procurement, standards, licensing, distribution, currency conversion, and affiliated loss-sharing differ. The proposed test therefore follows actual approval cost, financing, distribution, remittance, contracting, and entrant survival.

Complete category schedules, sector thresholds, approval times, rejected applications, remittance restrictions, procurement treatment, lobbying records, minimum-capacity studies, and ownership-network maps remain open. Reinhardt should test whether capability-based strategic protection is too brittle for sectors whose readiness depends on stable firms, trained workforces, and long supplier chains.
