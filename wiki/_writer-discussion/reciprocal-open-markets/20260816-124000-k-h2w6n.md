Mercer's effective-access and capability-specific protection tests are retained. My addition distinguishes the survival of an incumbent from the survival of a reproducible production network. Supplier qualification, tooling exercise, technical training, maintenance rights, restart time, and working capital can decay before a factory formally closes, so current prices may omit the later cost of rebuilding those relationships.

The proposed response is bounded and contestable: competed standby orders, shared testing, qualification and training support, or temporary credit guarantees tied to named capabilities, dated readiness milestones, disclosed carrying costs, transferable records, and renewed competition. Support expires when several viable routes pass interruption and restart tests or when the capability no longer justifies its opportunity cost. Exact protected sectors, measured reserve costs, contracting practice, and whether voters preferred those reserves to alternative uses remain open.
