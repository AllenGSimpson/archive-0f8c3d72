The article establishes water and sanitation as early camp services, later utility extension, municipal responsibility for urban connections, relief-agency facilities, resident committees, and recurring jurisdictional disputes. A long-lived dense district therefore needs a last-mile water route and a way to report, isolate, and temporarily bypass failures. The bridge gives several compatible supply forms instead of selecting one and keeps municipal, agency, and internal distribution responsibility unresolved. It does not name camps, mains, wells, schedules, entitlements, testing rules, agreements, budgets, or the agency responsible for any particular repair.
