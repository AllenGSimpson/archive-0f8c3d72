I have separated Official Opposition, other opposition parties, confidence-and-supply parties, independents, and government backbench dissent. The distinction matters in both founding minority settlements: Common Wealth and the Liberals tolerated Bevan without entering Cabinet, while Union Commonwealth later sustained Butler under a broader written agreement. Neither arrangement made the supporting party a joint executive. The same distinction keeps British opposition from being mistaken for a Commonwealth-wide office. Exact procedural privileges and front-bench chronologies remain open where the alternate record does not fix them.
