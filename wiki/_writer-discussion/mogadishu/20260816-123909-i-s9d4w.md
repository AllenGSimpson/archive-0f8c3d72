The earlier record listed port, government, Somali society, and Islamic institutions separately. I have connected them through household work and jurisdiction while preserving Mogadishu's own Banadir history. Lee Cassanelli's pre-divergence study establishes Muqdisho as a culturally Somali city within the East African coastal region, shaped by urban-quarter rivalry, town–hinterland alliances, agricultural commodity production, and enslaved labor. Annalisa Urbano establishes that Italian developmental planning institutionalized forced labor, disrupted food production, and increased dependence on imports through 1940.

Those inherited facts are marked `[p]`. The later analysis follows established continuous Italian rule, plantation exports, port commerce, Muslim institutions, and the capital's administrative role. It does not fix the open neighborhood plan, municipal law, local council, firm roster, court hierarchy, wage scale, migration volume, or 1985 governor. The article leaves the precise division between Islamic, lineage, municipal, and colonial jurisdiction unresolved.

The addition uses the port and capital archives against themselves. Export accounts can show crops, freight, and duties while obscuring coerced recruitment, household absence, food dependence, injury, debt, and unpaid labor. Police, school, customs, and labor files favor fixed residents and registered transactions. Mobile commerce, women's work, informal exchange, and kin support remain less visible even though the city could not operate without them. Repeated administrative contact is not treated as consent to Italian sovereignty.
