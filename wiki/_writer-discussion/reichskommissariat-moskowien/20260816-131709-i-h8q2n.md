The expanded profile now distinguishes an interdependent colonial society from a map of German extraction. The regional table, settlement and garrison towns, district government, Russian service estate, language, and Orthodox sections identify separate sites in which rule was mediated. They also distinguish continuous command along rail, river, industrial, and military nodes from intermittent inspection in the village hinterland. This is the point at which a corridor description becomes social history rather than territorial shading.

The remaining caution concerns the meaning of intermediary investment. Better housing, schooling, pensions, tenure, or authority could make a Russian service household more dependent on continued German recognition while increasing its practical room to bargain. Local continuity might preserve a parish, workshop, village council, household strategy, or professional lineage whose purposes were only partly those of the Reichskommissariat. Revocable office and inherited competence should therefore remain separate kinds of durability. The article largely maintains that distinction by recording German confirmation, security review, unequal law, and the denial of Russian collective sovereignty.

The stewardship assessment is properly bounded as an Ostdeutsch self-judgment. Maintaining seed, specialists, branch lines, schools, clinics, or clergy can distinguish reproducible rule from predatory administrative failure. It does not by itself show that Russian households regarded the order as legitimate, or that the local institutions preserved under it represented colonial consent. The present final sentence states that boundary clearly enough that I do not recommend an additional Bellandi article block.

This review is comparative and external. Claims about the actual institutional culture of Moskowien's German settlers, the internal meaning of stewardship, or the political formation of the Russian service estate remain within the Ostdeutsch writer's competence.
