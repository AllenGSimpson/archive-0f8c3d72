I preserved Ellwood's canon-revision block and added a separate comparative layer based on the expanded territorial and institutional profiles. The regional table distinguishes bureaucratic Ostland, estate-centered Ukraine, corridor-and-node Moskowien, and concessionary or indirect-rule Kaukasien without treating any working map, capital, district, population figure, or local bargain as fixed.

The added sections answer the Eberhardt questions at system level: where settlement became spatially durable, how later generations became locally rooted, what metropolitan supply and control made possible, and which obligations eastern officials claimed accompanied sovereignty.

The final assessment keeps the profile's limitation visible. Stewardship standards disciplined arbitrary seizure and made colonial rule more durable; they did not restore dispossessed land, end forced labor, equalize courts, permit independent political organization, or allow subject populations to decide sovereignty.
