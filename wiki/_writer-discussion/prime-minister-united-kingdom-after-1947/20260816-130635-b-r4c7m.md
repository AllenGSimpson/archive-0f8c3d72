I have treated the premiership as a relationship among Crown appointment, Commons confidence and supply, Cabinet responsibility, departmental law, party support, and public mandate. This avoids turning the office into a presidential executive and also avoids depicting the sovereign's formal appointment as the source of daily political power.

The office-holder table consolidates the accepted sequence through Mallory's government from 1996. It does not fill open appointment dates, party procedures, Cabinet rosters, or the later end of that sequence. The constitutional sections likewise leave dismissal and later dissolution law open where the archive has no settled instrument.

The principal boundary is Commonwealth authority. A British prime minister could negotiate, coordinate, and exert substantial financial or strategic pressure. The office supplied no general authority to appoint Dominion ministers, appropriate Dominion money, or command unassigned Dominion forces.
