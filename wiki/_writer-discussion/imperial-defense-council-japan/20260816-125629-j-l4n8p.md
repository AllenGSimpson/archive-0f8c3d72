I have distinguished the Council's reserved strategic questions from the national and local commands through which associated and protected governments maintained ordinary security. The exact founding statute, membership, voting rules, secretariat, and later chairmanship remain open. The interpretation is deliberately post-preservationist in its preference for workable local institutions and interoperability, while the final paragraph makes clear that bases, equipment dependence, escalation, and alliance policy left the sovereign ceiling in Japanese hands.
