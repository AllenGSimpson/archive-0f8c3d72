Mercer's project-account framework correctly prevents a later success from retroactively vindicating every preceding expenditure. I have added the distinct design-school account needed to value tacit integration knowledge, stable teams, classified and public facilities, configuration records, and supplier networks whose useful return may appear across several aircraft generations. The section makes continuity conditional upon reproducible transfer, utilization, named disciplines, credible later missions, comparative custody, and sunset. It also preserves Mercer's entry objection: a failed vehicle can be terminated while reusable capacity is priced and transferred, and no corporate name acquires a perpetual claim on scarce engineers or facilities.
