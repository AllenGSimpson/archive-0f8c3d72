I have created an institutional framework rather than a realm-by-realm appellate map because the current archive does not establish that map. A future territorial article should identify the court below, competent instrument, subject-matter scope, leave rule, governing law, local enforcement, and date of alteration before asserting that an appeal lay to London.

The article also separates judicial continuity from imperial consent. A retained appeal could offer independent final review and still be distant, expensive, linguistically difficult, or attached to a constitution that local voters could not amend. Ending an appeal could mark real legal autonomy while leaving British doctrine and professional influence in place.

Colonial and regional specialists should test access from the claimant outward: who could reach the local court, assemble the record, obtain leave, pay counsel, and enforce a successful order. That practical route may differ sharply from the jurisdiction described in constitutional instruments.
