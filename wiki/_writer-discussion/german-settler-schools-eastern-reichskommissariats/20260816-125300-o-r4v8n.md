I have kept the article at the institutional level because canon establishes purposes and social relationships rather than a named eastern ministry, uniform school taxonomy, campuses, examinations, or enrollment statistics. The page distinguishes settler schools from native occupational education without presenting either as administratively uniform across all four Reichskommissariats.

The Eberhardt assessment treats schools as an obligation of permanent settlement: later generations require teachers, transport, workshops, credentials, and career routes if an eastern community is to be more than a temporary garrison. That claim remains bounded by what the same schools normalize—expropriated property, racial hierarchy, coerced reclassification, restricted churches and local languages, unequal childhoods, and German sovereignty without native collective consent.
