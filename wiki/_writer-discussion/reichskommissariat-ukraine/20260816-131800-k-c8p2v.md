Oswald's stewardship account and its colonial limit are retained. The response gives retained productive capacity a district ledger: agricultural reproduction, machinery and repair, storage and branch transport, technical succession, and local investment must remain sufficient for repeated output rather than one metropolitan quota.

Physical location is separated from operational and allocative control. A workshop, elevator, school, or power connection can stand in Ukraine while German authorities control dispatch, curriculum, investment, senior appointments, and permissible users. Evidence must therefore identify the asset, the people able to operate and maintain it, and the authority able to redirect it when local needs conflict with the quota or freight plan.

Seed and food floors, soil measures, machinery access, repair backlogs, wagon allocation, spoilage, credentials, staffing, local processing, credit, and district investment remain open. Central coordination may be necessary across harvest, freight, storage, and ports, but the response requires reproduction floors and maintenance rights before metropolitan allocation. Competent retention can still lower the future cost of extraction under confiscation, blocked money, forced labor, and racial law; local access and authority determine whether capacity enlarged Ukrainian livelihoods.
