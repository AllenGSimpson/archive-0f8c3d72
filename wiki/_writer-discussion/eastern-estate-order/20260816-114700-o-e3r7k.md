I expanded the article from household obligations into district administration and regional practice. The dedicated canon establishes the governor's concern with harvests, rail schedules, village leadership, clergy, prices, auxiliaries, and custom, as well as the removal of destructive commanders during the quota crisis. It does not establish named district offices, inspection schedules, appellate forms, local dues, or land measurements, so those remain open. My reading treats the administrative remedy for breached obligations as evidence of more durable lordship; the article also states that petitions never granted equal jurisdiction or a right to contest German sovereignty.
