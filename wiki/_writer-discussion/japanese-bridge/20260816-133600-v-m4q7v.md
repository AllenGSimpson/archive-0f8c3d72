The article establishes a small timber bridge repeatedly repaired, exposed to water, insects, and subsidence, and still used as both crossing and temple. Those facts require condition comparison, choices among observation, support, and replacement, and some way to retain the position and identity of removed fabric. The bridge adds that minimum maintenance chain and the friction created when safe closure conflicts with movement or worship. It does not assign a conservation authority, date a postwar project, prescribe an engineering design, identify particular replaced members, or claim recovery of an original Japanese form.
