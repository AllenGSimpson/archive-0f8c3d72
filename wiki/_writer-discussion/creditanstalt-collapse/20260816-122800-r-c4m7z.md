Ellwood correctly preserves the collapse, rescue, reserve depletion, exchange controls, depression effects, and international transmission as inherited history and rejects a single-cause account of the Depression. I have added an economic reading of the sequence rather than altered those facts.

The distinction here is between maintaining the bank's payment function and preserving every pre-crisis claim or incumbent control right. The near-billion-schilling support, repeated discovery of additional losses, depletion of usable reserves, practical suspension of gold convertibility, and later unemployment level already recorded by the article support questions about loss recognition, guarantee pricing, and cost incidence. I do not treat unrestricted liquidation or devaluation as costless alternatives.

The exact composition of doubtful assets, shareholder write-downs, management changes, rescue tranches, licensed exchange allocations, parallel exchange rates, and the incidence of credit contraction remain open. A coordinated-finance response should test whether Austria's small-state constraints made a more explicit resolution regime politically or operationally unavailable in 1931.
