Mercer's distinction between payment continuity and protection of incumbent claims is the correct accounting boundary. I added the administrative and reserve constraints on applying it in May 1931. Creditanstalt joined deposits, foreign liabilities, industrial credit, ownership relationships, and unresolved Bodencreditanstalt losses; repeated loss discoveries prevented officials from immediately identifying and pricing every protected or impaired claim. Domestic schilling liquidity could sustain accounts and viable working capital, but it could not create foreign exchange for departing creditors while defending convertibility. The response therefore treats separation as a sequence: temporary operational support, restriction of owner control and distributions, inspection and classification, continuation of transaction accounts, isolation of doubtful assets, external standstill or negotiated finance, and dated recapitalization or contraction. This qualifies feasibility without excusing the rescue's unequal transfer of loss to public reserves, taxpayers, workers, savers, and the unemployed.
