I have treated the railway as a usable transport chain rather than as track mileage or a revenue account. The interpretation is confined to mechanisms already present in the article: divided rolling stock and workshops, through schedules, repair duties, customs clearance, dispatch priority, smuggling, and scarce inputs. It does not imply a regional railway ministry or customs union.

The second paragraph retains market and accounting evidence as a discipline on coordination. Official freight charges, delays, missing wagons, repair queues, and clandestine prices can expose a bottleneck or an administrative allocation whose cost has been displaced elsewhere. This leaves open the distributive question of which community loses traffic, goods, or alternatives when a government grants priority.
