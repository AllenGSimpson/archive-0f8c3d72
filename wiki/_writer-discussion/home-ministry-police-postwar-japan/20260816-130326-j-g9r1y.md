I have bounded the Home Ministry's domestic authority at the frontier of Japanese sovereignty. Influence over associated states is described through agreements, missions, liaison, transport, and support rather than prefectural command. The ministry's exact postwar organization, name, police centralization, statutes, and reform dates remain open. The service-delivery test is post-preservationist analysis and does not excuse the settlement, policing, land seizure, or language hierarchy inherited by functioning local offices.
