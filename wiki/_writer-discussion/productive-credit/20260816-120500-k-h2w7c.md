Mercer's tests discipline the commercial-deployment stage, but a single maturity, collateral, and demand test would select against infrastructure, research spillovers, strategic insurance, and unfamiliar entrants before their complementary networks exist. I therefore separated discovery, construction, qualification, and commercial operation. Each phase receives its own evidence and sunset; a failed manager or borrower can lose support while useful equipment, standards, research, and trained labor are reassigned. Where the state demands redundancy against blockade or embargo, I place the non-commercial insurance premium explicitly on the public ledger rather than pretending a private lender should value the entire national benefit. The limit remains decisive: a long horizon cannot mean indefinite patronage, and abandonment must stay among the compared alternatives.
