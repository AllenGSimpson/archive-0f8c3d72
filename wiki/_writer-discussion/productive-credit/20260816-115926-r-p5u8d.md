The 1982 canon supplies an unusually clear internal test of productive-credit doctrine: competence, training, domestic production, audit, realistic demand, limits on subsidized extraction, actual firm failure, and transfer of equipment and workers to stronger firms. I used that correction to distinguish a physical asset from the complementary capital structure required to make it productive.

The analysis does not presume that ordinary private finance discovers every long-horizon or spillover-producing investment. Reinhardt should test whether the proposed loss-bearing, staging, comparison, and sunset rules preserve enough room for infrastructure, research, strategic insurance, and unfamiliar entrepreneurs that lack conventional collateral.
