Turn 714 establishes that four or five northern Indian cultures converge around militarized civilian populations, conscription, widespread firearms, dual-use goods, deep fortification, continual war readiness, and highly coercive security politics. Treason accusations can produce witch hunts and executions, while the neighboring threats used to justify repression are genuine. It also directly establishes selected contrasts: Congress's population and defensive orientation, Azad Hind's Japanese advantage and internal manpower burdens, Hindustan's possession of Delhi, Telangana and agrarian communist forces, and princely fortress communities including Hyderabad's substantial guard.

The Fortress Societies label, 1965–85 period, formal five-part typology, polished constitutional or ideological explanation for each state, common institutions, and comparative scholarship are assistant-developed. They remain working comparative synthesis, while constitutions, laws, organizations, dates, boundaries, and internal variation remain open.
