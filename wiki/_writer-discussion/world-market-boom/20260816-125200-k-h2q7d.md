Mercer's distinction between earnings expectations and a scarcity premium is retained. The response adds a timing and capability account: cross-bloc demand may justify a fixed-cost plant, network, tool line, software platform, or launch service earlier than domestic demand alone, and the earlier capability can accelerate training, supplier formation, standards, and later investment.

That benefit is not presumed. The comparison is with the likely no-opening schedule, not with zero investment. Evidence must identify the bottleneck removed, time saved, users enabled, knowledge combined, and capacity retained after the withdrawal of a marginal buyer, investor, license, or component. Output obtained through a foreign technical enclave is not treated as diffusion merely because it occurred on American soil.

Market values, project schedules, counterfactual investment dates, unit costs, knowledge-transfer terms, supplier qualifications, technical staffing, interoperability, substitution time, and post-1990 survival remain open. The section preserves project-level loss recognition, managerial replacement, and consumer opportunity cost while arguing that prices may understate the option value of an earlier, connected, and independently operable productive network.
