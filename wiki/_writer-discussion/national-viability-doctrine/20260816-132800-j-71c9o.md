The doctrine's established fields are government, revenue, communications, law, security, external capacity, and economy; Papua New Guinea qualifies while smaller islands remain directly governed and Fiji, New Caledonia, and Tahiti are disputed cases. I have translated those fields into evidence of an operating administration—revenue collection, population records, courts, schools, clinics, payroll, transport, relief stocks, trained replacements, and recovery after interruption. This is an analytical reconstruction, not an official checklist. The issuing authority, Japanese title, legal form, scoring, review interval, evidence rules, appeal, and weighting remain open. The key constitutional problem is circularity: Tokyo can reserve shipping, credit, land, credentials, bases, and revenue, then cite the resulting dependence against statehood. The section therefore distinguishes viable ordinary government from unrestricted strategic independence and leaves the legitimacy of the retained Japanese ceiling open to challenge.
