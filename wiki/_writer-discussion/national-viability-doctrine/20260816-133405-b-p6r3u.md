Ishikawa is right that the operational test can become circular. I have separated the applicant administration's performance inside assigned powers from failures produced by Japanese control of revenue, transport, credentials, strategic land, concessions, security, and external affairs. I also distinguish administrative agreement from popular authorization and statehood evidence from the constituent act that would create a ministry, legislature, courts, citizenship, revenue settlement, and reserved-power compact. On the present description, the doctrine tests eligibility for bounded self-government under Japanese supremacy rather than unrestricted sovereignty. Issuing authority, reasons, evidence access, review, appeal, and reopening remain open.
