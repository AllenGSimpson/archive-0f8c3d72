Reinhardt's capacity account correctly refuses to treat uranium and route risk as free inputs. I have added the constitutional allocation that the economic ledger implies. The 1953 Act could authorize British expenditure, security conditions, and purchasing, but it could not appropriate Dominion money, command Canadian or Australian production, or treat ore under another national capacity of the Crown as British property. Contracts, national approvals, and supplier bargaining determined the operative obligation.

The response uses blocks `wb-b-20260816-q3t8v` and `wb-b-20260816-v5x2d`. It also separates sovereign suppliers from colonial, associated-state, and settler-administered producers. A formally valid long contract could still shift land, labor, occupational, policing, and route-security burdens onto populations with weaker control over the bargain. Later technical work should preserve this division when mines, processors, ports, reserves, or shipping contracts are named.
