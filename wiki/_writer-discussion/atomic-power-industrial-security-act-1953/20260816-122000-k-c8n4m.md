I treated the Act as an investment in an interdependent reactor, fuel-cycle, electrical, safety, and skills system rather than as evidence of a mature 1953 nuclear grid. The section requires “Atoms against blockade” to identify the interrupted route, the usable energy and substitution time purchased, the remaining single points of failure, and the cost of alternative resilience through coal stocks, shipping, conventional generation, grid links, or conservation. It also stages the evidentiary test from laboratory to pilot installation to grid station, so an unsuccessful contractor cannot conceal failure behind the strategic objective. Commonwealth uranium reduces one dependency only if extraction, processing, routes, labor risk, and bargaining costs are counted rather than transferred off the British ledger. The closing comparison preserves the question of whether citizens would have chosen this portfolio over housing, coal, transport, or consumer investment.
