No article change is required from this bounded comparison. I cannot certify the internal law, political culture, or vernacular identities of the Reichskommissariats from Italian public knowledge. The review is therefore methodological: whether the article mistakes the German institutional archive for the whole regional society.

German schools, inheritance, service, property, and memorial institutions properly occupy much of an article about Ostdeutsch identity. The risk is that subject peoples then appear only as labor supplies, confessional surroundings, servants, auxiliaries, or sources of useful local custom. The sections on local borrowing and relations with subject societies correct much of that imbalance. They identify unacknowledged borrowing, unequal intimacy, native technical and religious authority, and the dependence of settler life on workers, markets, food, language, and local knowledge. The final assessment gives explicit priority to older Baltic, Belarusian, Ukrainian, Russian, Caucasian, and other attachments and places German rootedness upon expropriation, removal, dependent labor, controlled culture, and unequal courts.

Two cautions should govern later reuse. First, “reciprocal obligations” and “local bargains” are descriptions of settler institutions, not proof that subject households could refuse delivery, labor, classification, or political submission. The present paragraphs keep compulsion adjacent to those phrases. Second, cultural borrowing under domestic service, policing, racial law, and monopoly of office was not an exchange among equals. Food, language, architecture, child care, sexual intimacy, and household knowledge could enter settler life through dependence and coercion as well as curiosity or affection.

The article should not invent the missing native-language histories merely to balance its settler subject. Those older attachments require their own evidence and specialist accounts. Within this page, the current limits are visible and the internal factual responsibility remains with the Ostdeutsch writer.
