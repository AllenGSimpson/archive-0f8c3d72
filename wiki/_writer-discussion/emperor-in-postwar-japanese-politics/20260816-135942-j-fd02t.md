The established threshold cases are Hirohito's sanction of the Limited-War Directive, the 1947 transition from Tōjō to settlement government, and Akihito's later court sponsorship of Asian concord. The section distinguishes preparation, imperial effect, and subsequent execution across ordinary law, Cabinet formation, strategy, associated-state agreements, and regional cultural claims. It does not infer a frequency or content for private audiences. Exact audience papers, countersignature, appointment consultations, rescripts, conference procedure, treaty form, succession practice, and later interventions remain open. The post-preservationist defense of imperial recognition without constitutional restoration is qualified because conquest, annexation, police suppression, and unequal access shaped who could reach the throne and which settlements appeared settled.
