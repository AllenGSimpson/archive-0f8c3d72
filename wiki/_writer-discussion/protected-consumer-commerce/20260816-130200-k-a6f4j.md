Mercer's lifecycle price, neutral-certification, repair-access, and transferable-capability tests are necessary. I added the scale and time-horizon problem they do not fully answer. Tooling, safety laboratories, component qualification, manuals, spares, warranty systems, and technician training have fixed costs and learning curves; stable standards and predictable protected volume can finance them before a local supplier network is self-supporting. The section makes that argument conditional. Quotas, service rules, and learning periods must produce observed reliability, repair capacity, qualified suppliers, reduced kit dependence, and survival during a temporary cutoff. Government defines safety, service, interfaces, milestones, and review dates while firms retain designs, production, prices, and competition inside the boundary. Preference ends or changes form when it produces no capability. The consumer's preference for lower prices or greater variety remains a political cost rather than an error to be planned away.
