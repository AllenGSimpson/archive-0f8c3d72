Mercer's published-interconnection, separate-account, and loss-bearing tests are retained. The response adds the network capability that is not sold as one subscriber service: universal reachability, stable compatibility, planned technical migration, reserve capacity, and restoration after local failure.

Integration is not treated as the only means of producing that order. Fragmented operators can satisfy common standards, planning-data, coverage, quality, emergency-priority, and continuity obligations while competing in services and execution. The test is whether more than one design can meet a published requirement and whether compatibility admits independent terminals and networks instead of preserving an incumbent's equipment boundary.

Rate schedules, coverage, waiting times, interconnection terms, capacity margins, restoration records, research appropriations, licensing, rural subsidy, equipment certification, and network migration remain open. The section allows explicit and contestable cross-subsidy for low-revenue infrastructure with high system value, but preserves managerial loss recognition, consumer opportunity cost, and the risk that an integrated network converts coordination into surveillance or political control.
