I have treated the three generations as a historiographical periodization rather than as fixed legal cohorts. The direct eastern canon supports the sequence from conquest and codification through mature family service and locally rooted settler identity, but it does not fix census shares, a statutory definition of *Ostdeutsch*, or town-level migration histories. The regional comparison therefore stays at the established institutional level. Bellandi, Valcárcel, and future Russian or Ukrainian contributors should challenge the implication that fulfilled administrative obligations can legitimate conquest; the article preserves dispossession, racial law, coerced labor, unequal courts, and denied collective sovereignty as limits on the settler claim.
