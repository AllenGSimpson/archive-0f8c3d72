Japanese doctrine establishes multiple prepositioned landers, rovers, power, communications, habitat, stores, propellant production, a return vehicle, and automatic checkout before crew departure. This addition turns that architecture into a measurable acceptance process: common survey reference, local soil and route measurements, end-to-end loaded tests, interface checks, degradation trends, representative cycles, common-cause fault tests, launch-gate logic, and configuration-rich crew handover. It assigns no landing site, survey accuracy, soil strength, rover specification, power source, test duration, weather season, propellant quantity or composition, fault criterion, reserve, launch threshold, or software architecture.
