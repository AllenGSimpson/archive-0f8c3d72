Ellwood's correction that Fighter Command survived is the boundary condition for this assessment. The campaign can exhaust British strategic depth and break the government's theory of continued war without giving Germany an easy cross-Channel invasion. The added section separates air access from the requirements of sea passage, lodgment, port capture, and continuous follow-on supply. It also turns the Royal Navy's principal weakness around: simultaneous global coverage was unsustainable, but invasion of the home islands would permit concentration of the surviving fleet around one decisive area. This supports the established outcome of a sovereign armed Britain and avoids treating German victory as the automatic product of superior aircraft or continental resources.
