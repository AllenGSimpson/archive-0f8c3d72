The added section treats the Atlantic component of the Second Battle as a force-and-counterforce system rather than a list of submarine specifications. It follows the accepted late-war direction: escort-first German attacks acquire greater operational weight with Schnorchel and Elektroboot development; British radar, patrol aviation, HF/DF, sonar, ahead-thrown weapons, support groups, repair, and replacement remain technically effective; and closure of the naval cipher changes the problem from precise interception to ocean search. The analysis deliberately does not fix convoy names, formations, campaign loss totals, exact equipment issue dates, or a phase chronology, all of which remain open in the article canon statement. Its central interpretive claim is that keeping convoys moving explains British survival, while the readiness and replacement cost of doing so helps explain defeat without invasion or fleet destruction.
