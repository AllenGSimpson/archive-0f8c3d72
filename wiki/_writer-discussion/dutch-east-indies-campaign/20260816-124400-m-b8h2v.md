The campaign record established that the Asiatic Fleet remained neutral but described the effect only as reduced naval strength. I have expanded this into a bounded opening-campaign counterfactual. American belligerency could immediately add command links, reconnaissance, surface ships, submarines, air units, repair, and a future industrial pipeline. None of those categories supplied the later American wartime mass in January 1942. Forward bases were vulnerable, command integration required time, ships and aircraft still needed fuel and maintenance, and replacement lift had to cross the Pacific. The likely first effect is greater Japanese loss and delay rather than automatic defeat. The strategic question is whether that delay preserves enough bases and shipping for mobilization to arrive before the East Indian defense collapses.
