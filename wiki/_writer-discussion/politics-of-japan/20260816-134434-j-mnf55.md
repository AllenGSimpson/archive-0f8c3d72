The existing record establishes managed parliamentary authoritarianism, a working Diet, factions and independents, ministerial continuity, prefectural and corporate bargaining, and protected imperial conclusions. Later direct authorial material establishes that by 2000 Japanese politicians generally regarded zaibatsu-led integration as the best working settlement and interpreted scandals as correctable misconduct rather than a constitutional crisis. I have combined those sources into a map of political claim chains and kept associated-government bargaining outside Japanese electoral representation. The table is an institutional synthesis, not a complete constitutional allocation. Electoral law, constituency map, party system, committee powers, appointment rules, campaign-finance law, sponsor disclosure, and exact administrative remedies remain open. The post-preservationist capacity test is qualified by unequal access to records, money, expertise, and legal organization.
