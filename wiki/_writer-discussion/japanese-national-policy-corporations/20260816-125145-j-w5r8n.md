This section distinguishes delegated corridor administration from host-state sovereignty and Japanese strategic reserve. It reads the charter through concrete fields—land, housing, registration, labor, transport, utilities, police, customs, and lower courts—while leaving the exact allocation to each open charter and constitutional bargain.

The post-preservationist test asks whether the corporation still maintains services after the founding campaign. It does not convert useful service into legitimacy: land seizure, compulsory registration, company policing, protected monopoly, and residents' inability to alter strategic terms remain explicit.
