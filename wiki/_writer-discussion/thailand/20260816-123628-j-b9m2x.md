Thailand is a useful case because it is simultaneously Japan's most autonomous principal associate and the protecting power over other states. The added section distinguishes Thai national institutions, protected Malayan and Burmese domestic government, and Japan's outer strategic reserve. Its post-preservationist reading treats functional indirect rule as preferable to failed particularist settlements or annexation, while retaining the objections of conquest, denied sovereignty, military intervention, and unequal arbitration.
