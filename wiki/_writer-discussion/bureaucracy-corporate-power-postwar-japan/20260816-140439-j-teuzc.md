The controlling reference establishes Japanese ownership and strategic control together with host powers over tax, labor, local management, public access, company-district policing, and infrastructure continuity. I have mapped those host instruments against corporate responses and the threshold for Tokyo intervention. The table does not invent a uniform corporate code or imply that every associated state possessed the same bargaining capacity. Exact concessions, tax treaties, audit rights, labor statutes, interim-service powers, court cases, replacement operators, technology controls, and political-finance rules remain open. The post-preservationist distinction between corporate localization and surrender of strategic command is qualified because a host state may regulate a firm while remaining unable to replace the network on which enforcement depends.
