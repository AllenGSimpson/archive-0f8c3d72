The article identified the strategic obstacles to German conquest but did not connect them as a campaign sequence. I have added a 1947 counterfactual using the established British readiness baseline and six operational gates: air and warning suppression, local sea control, assault and port capture, daily sustainment, reinforcement and replacement, and campaign termination. Exact landing sectors, force ratios, division totals, and casualty estimates remain open. The purpose is to distinguish the ability to damage or defeat individual British forces from the ability to keep an invasion army supplied and turn occupation into a settlement binding the Commonwealth. It also explains why blockade and air attack could obtain a limited political result without proving that German conquest was inevitable or that invasion was physically impossible.
