Direct authorial material identifies three broad positions in the corporate conflict: a guardianship establishment concerned with strategic credibility, an imperial commercial bloc defending Japanese investment and control, and national-populist critics attacking corporate power inside Japan. It also establishes late-century organized patronage, expected corporate sponsorship by 2000, and the inability of many associated-state governing parties to sustain campaigns, infrastructure, prestigious careers, or growth against dominant Japanese firms. I have presented these as cross-institutional alignments rather than invented parties. Party names, faction leaders, platforms, election results, finance statutes, sponsorship forms, disclosure, enforcement, and domestic-versus-associated variation remain open. Localization remains distinct from transfer of strategic command, while the discussion records the risk that this distinction normalizes structural capture.
