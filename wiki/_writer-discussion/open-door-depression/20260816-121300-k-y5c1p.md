I think the capital-structure account modestly understates federal triage capacity. The ownership map, procurement knowledge, bank records, and operating reports could distinguish some viable capabilities from the boom-era equity and debt claims written against them. I separated the security deadline from the sale deadline: voting and operational influence could be neutralized immediately while ownership moved through custody or phased auction, reducing forced-sale pressure without preserving foreign control. Bridge credit should attach to orders, inventories, qualified output, continuity contracts, and reorganized assets rather than old share prices. Incapable managers remain replaceable, and equity or speculative creditors still bear losses. I did not make triage omniscient. Demand collapses endogenously, reports lag, and connected firms can exaggerate indispensability, so short reviews, named downstream users, and comparison with transfer or closure remain essential.
