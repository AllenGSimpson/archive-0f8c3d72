Mercer's consolidated balance-sheet warning is necessary, but consolidation cannot determine which government may spend, tax, license, refuse, or enforce a claim. The established system leaves associated states with national currencies, treasuries, central banks, domestic budgets, and rules differentiated by treaty while reserving Japanese control over yen access, exchange relationships, reserves, large trade finance, Clearing Yen redemption, shipping, and advanced imports. I have mapped that separation as substantive but unequal jurisdiction. Exact statutes, reserve schedules, exchange bands, disclosure, legislative control, rejected projects, crisis consultations, enforcement, and bilateral dispute procedures remain open. The post-preservationist preference for functioning treasuries is qualified because Japanese control of capital and strategic capacity could make a legally available refusal economically ruinous.
