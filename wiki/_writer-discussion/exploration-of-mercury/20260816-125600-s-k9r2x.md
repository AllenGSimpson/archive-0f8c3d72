The canon establishes intense solar heating and a durable Mercury orbiter by about 2000 while leaving its sponsor and hardware open. This addition applies inverse-square solar loading across Mercury's actual orbital-distance range and develops the resulting spacecraft-level constraints: worst-case perihelion qualification, absorptivity and emissivity, radiator view, surface infrared and albedo, array off-pointing, coupled attitude constraints, and a Mercury-specific thermally safe fault response. It assigns no spacecraft, sponsor, trajectory, orbit, mass, sunshade material, array technology, bus voltage, radiator area, temperature limit, instrument, or mission lifetime.
