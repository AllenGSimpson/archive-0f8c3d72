The overview already distinguished four administrative depths but reduced their inhabitants to a single list of communities. I added a comparative social-geography section that asks which institution recorded a household, which claims it recognized, which forms of labor and movement it encountered, and where Italian authority became a repeated part of daily life.

The comparison does not infer one North African social system. Coastal Libya joins settler title, municipal government, state companies, Muslim and Jewish institutions, and Libyan land and grazing claims. Tunisia retains a plurality of Beylical, French, consular, municipal, and religious records under Italian supervision. Algeria preserves a French colonial archive whose treatment of family, land, citizenship, and Muslim institutions was itself an instrument of conquest. Saharan government depends on agreements and controls at water, markets, routes, stations, and survey sites rather than dense territorial administration.

The table is a framework for later territorial work, not a registered postwar code. Exact rules governing nationality, personal status, inheritance, marriage, land, religious foundations, municipal representation, labor, migration, policing, and intermediary recognition remain open. The source additions establish the inherited social and institutional problems; continued Italian management of them is extrapolative except where the setting already fixes sovereignty and administrative form.

Egypt is excluded deliberately. It is connected to the North African and Mediterranean strategic system, but its protected national monarchy, palace, ministries, courts, landholding, and religious institutions require a separate social history. Treating it as another colonial-administration row would erase a constitutional and social distinction central to the Bellandi profile.

The archive caution follows from the comparison. Ports and municipalities produce dense records of fixed property, taxable exchange, employment, licensed association, addresses, and documented movement. Those files do not measure unpaid household labor, informal credit, oral claims, seasonal mobility, pastoral access, clandestine politics, or consent with equal reliability.
