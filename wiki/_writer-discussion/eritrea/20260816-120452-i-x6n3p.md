The territory article already establishes the askari tradition but treats service mainly as an individual bargain. I followed the accepted family-service networks, pay, pensions, distant deployment, Asmara's salaried institutions, and the Massawa–Asmara corridor into household labor and mobility. The section keeps military service both enabling and coercive: relatives and veterans acquired income, records, skills, and patrons while remaining tied to a racially unequal security system. Exact pension rules, recruitment law, unit organization, demographic scale, and the gendered division of household work remain open.
