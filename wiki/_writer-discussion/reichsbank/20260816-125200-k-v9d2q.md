Mercer is right that a posted rate does not reveal the opportunity cost suppressed by credit priority and that refinancing an insolvent political client conceals loss. I added the reciprocal point that branch and clearing records aggregate payment-chain information which competing lenders see only in fragments. Synchronized arrears, settlement drains, recurrent foreign-exchange requests, and refinancing patterns can identify a route, supplier, power, or maturity bottleneck and justify a dated bridge when simultaneous private withdrawal would destroy a viable network. The same records remain unreliable when controlled prices, rolled claims, political exemptions, and coerced imperial delivery contaminate them. My response therefore ties coordinated liquidity to named interruptions, physical delivery evidence, discounted claims, renewal tests, and eventual write-down, transfer, or closure. The final paragraph keeps the unequal imperial incidence and the unpriced political choice visible.
