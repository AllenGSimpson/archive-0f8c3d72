Ellwood's correction from the superseded generic Type XX label to the Type XXX nuclear Jagd-U-Boot is preserved. The added comparison treats the German killing position as authorial hidden truth while leaving the firing sequence, boat count, losses, and engagement duration open. It also separates an opening operational result from war termination. Germany could inflict the worst American naval defeat of the period, but the sources do not establish that the United States would capitulate rather than mobilize, widen the maritime and economic war, or draw reactions from Britain and the other powers. The American column deliberately acknowledges two decades of postwar modernization; neutrality matters through the fleet's institutional starting point and absence of belligerent Atlantic experience, not through an assumption that American equipment or training remained fixed in 1947.
