The overview previously treated Operation Covenant as proof that Britain would honor the Gulf hierarchy, but it did not specify why the campaign was winnable. I have added the favorable conditions: established Gulf and Indian Ocean access, Iraqi weakness in maintenance and combined-arms institutions despite modern equipment, Saudi and Iranian pressure, a concentrated Commonwealth force, and a limited decision point at Basra. The final paragraph preserves the distinction between capability and universal precedent. Covenant proved that post-defeat Britain could still concentrate and win a bounded regional war. It did not prove that the same force could easily defeat an integrated, theater-prepared opponent operating near its own bases, which is the institutional error behind the later Falklands analogy.
