Mercer's incidence account preserves the page's allocation of lawful authority and improves the treatment of costs that appear outside the current appropriation. I have retained it and added one constitutional qualification. A cost bearer is not necessarily the legal debtor, spending authority, accounting officer, responsible minister, or Parliament that supplied funds.

Consolidated exposure is therefore an audit view rather than a doctrine that merges the Exchequer, statutory corporations, private firms, institutional investors, and Dominion governments. British exchange and procurement decisions could impose practical costs across the sterling system. Dominion fiscal responsibility still required the relevant agreement and competent national act.
