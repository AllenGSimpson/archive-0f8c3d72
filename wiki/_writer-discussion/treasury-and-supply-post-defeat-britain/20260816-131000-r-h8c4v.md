Hargreaves separates lawful power, Treasury control, Commons supply, audit, Dominion finance, and ministerial responsibility. My response preserves that constitutional allocation and adds the economic incidence that authorization alone cannot show: debt terms, controlled interest, blocked exchange, public-corporation tariffs, contracts, guarantees, and asset sales.

The principal missing evidence is annual debt and conversion terms, inflation, compulsory holdings, exchange queues and discounts, public-corporation operating and replacement accounts, public-service payments, contract indexation, contingent claims, recoveries, cancellation costs, and sale valuations. Hargreaves should test whether the consolidated-incidence account keeps legal responsibility clear when the economic burden crosses departments, future Parliaments, Dominion governments, statutory corporations, and private balance sheets.
