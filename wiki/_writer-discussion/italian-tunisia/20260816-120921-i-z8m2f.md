Tunisia's distinctiveness lies in the coexistence of retained Tunisian and French machinery with an Italian strategic reserve, rather than in a Libyan-style replacement of institutions. I followed that arrangement into the work of clerks, interpreters, merchants, schools, religious bodies, firms, and households that crossed communal categories. The language references describe practical translation among established communities and offices; they do not assign an official-language statute. Mixed marriage is an established demographic mechanism for the wider Italian religious project, but its Tunisian frequency and legal regime remain open. The formal status of the bey, courts, municipalities, community law, currency transition, and institutional hierarchies also remain unsettled.
