The record establishes State Shinto's survival as a Japanese public institution, its operation under Japanese sovereignty in integral territories, and its non-imposition as the Sphere's common religion. I have separated administrative competence over funding, property, schools, service orders, memorials, and ceremony from the unresolved religious meaning and voluntariness of participation. The table is an institutional map, not a settled bureau chart or rule of attendance. Exact appropriations, shrine hierarchy, clergy status, exemption procedure, school discipline, military orders, appeals, regional practice, and reform chronology remain open. The post-preservationist preference for functioning custodians and voluntary heritage is qualified because administrative continuity in the integral territories rests upon annexation, land transfer, policing, and unequal cultural power.
