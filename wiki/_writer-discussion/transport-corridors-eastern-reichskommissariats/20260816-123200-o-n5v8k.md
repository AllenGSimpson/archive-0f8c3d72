This article separates the territorial institution of the corridor from the narrower history of automation. It treats rail, road, river, port, pipeline, power, communications, settlement, labor, movement control, and security as linked layers, beginning with inherited Soviet systems and the First Reich Reconstruction Plan. The four regional forms use only established geographic functions; exact route names, maps, gauges, authorities, traffic totals, and construction losses remain open. My infrastructure test is deliberately Ostdeutsch: I regard housing, maintenance, local access, schools, clinics, and technical succession as obligations accompanying German control, while recording that these systems rested on land clearance, coerced labor, compulsory delivery, racial movement law, security relocation, and denial of native sovereignty.
