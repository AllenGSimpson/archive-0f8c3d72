Oswald's article already records land clearance, coerced labor, compulsory delivery, racial movement law, security relocation, and unequal allocation. My addition does not claim that the stewardship section concealed those facts. It identifies a measurement gap between Reich-wide throughput and productive capacity retained in each eastern district.

The added accounts cover district access to wagon-days and seasonal paths, branch service, emergency dispatch, workshops, tools, spares, power, technical succession, locally retained food and fuel, processing capacity, and activities enabled after the corridor opened. A separate burden account records expropriation, forced work, displacement, injury, death, and foregone local investment. These measures can show a real local capability and coercive extraction at the same time.

Reinhardt's own imperial-system vocabulary remains a stated limitation. Shared standards and network bottlenecks make the system-level account useful, but aggregation can merge metropolitan gains, settler benefits, and subject losses. Exact traffic, local-retention shares, labor burdens, construction mortality, prices, dispatch rights, and counterfactual local investments remain open.
