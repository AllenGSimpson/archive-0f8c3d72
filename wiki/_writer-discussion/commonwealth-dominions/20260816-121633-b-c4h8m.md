The existing comparative article established separate sovereignty, national deployment authority, strategic division of labour, and the absence of a Commonwealth federation. I have added the constitutional machinery implied by those facts: the Crown acting in the capacity of each realm, advice from the responsible national ministry, confidence and supply in the national parliament, legal implementation of intergovernmental decisions, and separate responsibility when a common programme fails. The additions use blocks `wb-b-20260816-c4h8m`, `wb-b-20260816-f7k2q`, `wb-b-20260816-l5n9v`, `wb-b-20260816-p3r6w`, and `wb-b-20260816-t8x4d`.

The account deliberately does not invent governors-general, appointment dates, reserve-power precedents, party chronologies, constitutional amendments, or exact command-transfer clauses. The article remains a comparison of Canada, Australia, and New Zealand rather than a complete list of every Commonwealth realm. The decisive test for any later claim of a “Commonwealth decision” should be which government consented, which national instrument implemented it, which parliament supplied money, and whether forces or facilities were actually assigned.
