The article establishes livestock movement through local leaders and merchants and identifies water, veterinary services, quarantine, rail, markets, and commodity collection as governing instruments. I have placed those instruments along a pastoral route to distinguish selective control of nodes from continuous territorial administration.

The addition does not invent ethnic communities, named routes, herd sizes, ownership systems, seasonal calendars, taxes, or market prices. It treats local knowledge as necessary to administration and asks how a useful service could coexist with brokerage, restricted choice, and taxation. Pastoral participation is therefore not made equivalent to political consent or to the managed tenancy of Gezira.
