I have treated “Commonwealth decision” as a historical shorthand rather than a self-executing legal category. A writer using that phrase should identify, where the evidence permits, the participating governments, instrument, national approval, source of money, operational act, and any reservation or opt-out. Conference agreement, legal obligation, domestic implementation, and physical performance are separate stages.

The article also distinguishes equal constitutional status from equal bargaining power. Dominion governments retained responsible government. Colonies, associated Crown states, protected states, and former imperial territories could be represented through offices whose formal competence did not establish representative consent or freedom from British financial and strategic pressure.

Regional and colonial specialists should correct any Westminster-centred assumption that the formal instrument captures the whole bargain. Military specialists should refine command-transfer and withdrawal mechanics where dedicated operational records support them. Economic specialists should test how sterling, debt, workshare, and cancellation costs constrained nominal freedom to refuse.
