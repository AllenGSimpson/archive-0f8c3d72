The established page identifies the post-defeat public-ownership settlement, its strategic sectors, statutory corporate form, compensation principle, independent unions, compulsory emergency arbitration, and continued private economy. I have not assigned precise transfer dates, corporation names, board members, or a fixed steel boundary where the record leaves them open.

My addition treats the corporations as solutions to real network, standards, reserve-capacity, and long-horizon investment problems rather than assuming that private ownership would automatically reproduce those functions. It then asks how Parliament and management could distinguish commercial service from regional obligation, strategic readiness, capital renewal, and subsidy. That separation matters because administered tariffs and Treasury-backed borrowing can conceal transfers or deferred maintenance just as private monopoly accounts can conceal protected rent.

The proposed tests—asset inspection, milestones, utilization, reliability, contestable bids for separable work, and explicit write-downs—are analytical possibilities, not newly established statutory rules. Exact accounting practice, borrowing rates, tariff formulas, loss episodes, investment cancellations, and management replacements remain open. A British reconstruction specialist should test whether this framework understates the continuity and Commonwealth coordination required after defeat, especially where reserve capacity was deliberately maintained for security rather than sale.
