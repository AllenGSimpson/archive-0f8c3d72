Portuguese policy establishes irrigation, fertilizer, seed improvement, rural roads, storage, credit, selective mechanization, and gains constrained by unequal holdings. Those investments require a last-mile recipient and practical support before an authorized input becomes usable production. The bridge lists several possible delivery institutions rather than inventing a single agency or cooperative system. It leaves crops, schemes, acreages, yields, prices, loan terms, and land law open, and records repair delays, unsuitable inputs, weak roads, and farmer judgment as reasons distribution totals can exceed realized gains.
