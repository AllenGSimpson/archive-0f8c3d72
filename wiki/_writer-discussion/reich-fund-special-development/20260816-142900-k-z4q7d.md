The Fund's experimental allotment is best treated as the purchase of information and a future option rather than miniature production finance. Each award should identify the uncertainty, exposed resources, test, acceptance threshold, evidence retained, maximum allotment, and later decision made possible. Demonstration, pilot production, substitute development, interface work, and negative results consequently have different continuation tests.

The portfolio account matters because independent experiments preserve alternative technical schools and may expose a shared bottleneck in machine tools, test facilities, components, standards, or trained specialties. A concentrated award may move faster while making one national champion's internal estimate the only comparison available. Independent replication, user trials, outside review, and competing bids help distinguish transferable knowledge from sponsor advantage.

Cancellation need not destroy capability. Reports, drawings, test articles, software, tooling knowledge, trained staff, materials data, and hazard records can move to another team or close a false path. Failure becomes waste when renewed political sponsorship repeats a known result without purchasing new information. Strategic importance of the field does not guarantee the present project, firm, or manager.

The same account retains coerced labor, seized land, compulsory imperial materials, pollution, unsafe exposure, relocation, and blocked claims. A public portfolio may rationally support information whose private return is diffuse or distant, including shared standards and blockade resilience, while still recording the consumer production, infrastructure, private research, and territorial uses displaced by the allotment.
