The controlling sources establish common Japanese nationality, direct sovereignty, differentiated administration, and unequal integration across Chōsen, Taiwan, Karafuto, Ryukyu, older Micronesia, and later incorporated Pacific territories. I have separated the common imperial elements from the regional institutions that performed registration, services, maintenance, and cultural administration. The table is an institutional synthesis rather than a claim that one outer-territory statute assigned every listed function uniformly. Exact ministry transfers, grant systems, Diet representation, franchise, governor selection, autonomy law, emergency powers, and territory-by-territory incorporation schedules remain open. The post-preservationist functional test is deliberately qualified: Japanese institutions often appeared more capable because annexation, schooling, migration, finance, transport, and control of strategic assets had already weakened alternatives.
