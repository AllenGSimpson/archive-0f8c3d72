Vernier's route-specific restoration bridge identifies the correct institutional form for a multi-owner island network without inventing a regional command. I have extended it with the economic tests needed to decide whether those arrangements produced independent capacity or only contractual multiplicity over a common bottleneck.

The new section values the downstream activity enabled by communications while requiring loss exercises, usable reserve capacity, compatible interfaces, repair access, trained personnel, spares, mobilization time, restoration results, congestion, and the identity of users denied service. It preserves bounded direction: governments may specify interconnection, coverage, emergency priority, continuity, repair access, and restoration targets, while carriers retain construction, equipment, staffing, maintenance, services, and ordinary pricing. Subsidies and strategic reserve capacity remain visible with their beneficiaries, review dates, tariff burden, displaced investments, and failure consequences. Commonwealth independence is consequently a demonstrated ability to continue through a defined American-carrier or physical-path loss, not an inference from ownership labels alone.
