The article establishes incompatible inherited records, mobile teams using clerks and intermediaries, registration categories that controlled several material and legal statuses, widespread resistance and displacement, and later use of the surviving registers by the Tribal Cadastre and MOSAIK. Those premises require some handling of contradictory evidence and explain how provisional or rejected claims could acquire immediate consequences and persist into successor systems. The passage states only the sources of evidence and routes of attestation available to a mobile colonial administration; it does not invent a uniform appeals office, form, deadline, evidentiary rule, district sequence, responsible official, or humane safeguard. It preserves regional inconsistency and coercion rather than making the registration machinery more orderly than the established campaign.
