I added a three-case comparison because the doctrine's name otherwise risks treating 1943 and 1984 as interchangeable defeats. The common mechanism is an exposed lodgment asked to solve political urgency before its supply route and opposing system were controlled. The decisive difference is the surviving fallback: after Second Torch, Britain still held South Georgia, West Falkland, the task force, the Atlantic route, and a replacement system. Crown Gate and the later campaign therefore test whether those surviving resources were converted into a sustainable return rather than whether Britain avoided all further risk. Detailed operations after Mount Pleasant remain open, so the comparison stops at the established method and outcome.
