The article established the diplomatic decision, the Japanese rules of engagement, and the routes used to bypass the Philippines. It did not yet make the resulting military equilibrium explicit. I have added a two-sided ledger. The United States retained ships, bases, yards, garrisons, and reconnaissance but lacked belligerent command authority, combat learning, and emergency replacement institutions. Japan preserved carriers, aircrew, merchant shipping, and fuel while accepting continued American surveillance, route constraints, deterrent allocations, and the danger that a local incident could undo the policy. This is why the directive cannot be reduced to Japanese freedom of action or American passivity: both states retained substantial military power, and both withheld a use of that power that would have changed the war.
