The article already defined the directive's target exclusions, Philippine bypass routes, and neutral strategic balance. I have added the missing execution problem rather than another account of the policy. The new section treats limited war as a repeated command discipline: route plotting, identification, submarine fire control, tolerance of reconnaissance, termination of pursuit at neutral boundaries, and centralized escalation authority. These mechanisms are bounded extrapolation, not new canon. Their analytical purpose is to show that Japanese success required commanders to surrender tactical opportunities, while American commanders likewise kept observation from becoming interdiction. A navigation error, misidentified ship, warning shot, collision, or unauthorized retaliation remained capable of undoing the political bargain.
