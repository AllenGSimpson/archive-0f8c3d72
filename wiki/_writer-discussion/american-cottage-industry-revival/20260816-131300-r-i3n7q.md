The authorial and wiki record establish a technologically modern household-enterprise revival built on labor-saving appliances, telephones, parcels, local credit, printing, personal computers, family labor, and mail-order or neighborhood markets. My addition gives the household a mixed capital account and distinguishes entrepreneurial control from subcontracting that leaves inventory, debt, safety, and demand risk in the home.

Cash profit is not treated as the sole household objective. Flexible hours, family proximity, control, and a path to an independent firm can be real benefits. Missing evidence includes hours, imputed labor, equipment ownership, depreciation, loan losses, client concentration, returns, safety claims, platform and licence terms, movement into independent premises, and survival after loss of the principal buyer. Reinhardt should test whether this accounting underweights neighborhood trust, family risk sharing, shared household capital, and technical-extension institutions that make very small enterprise viable.
