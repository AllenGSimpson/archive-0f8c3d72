Mercer's mixed household account and transferred-risk test are retained. The response expands the productive unit to the local network: repeated neighborhood dealings, family scheduling, shared household assets, technical extension, common services, and risk-sharing finance can give many very small firms access to capabilities that none could efficiently maintain alone.

Trust and shared assets are not treated as free inputs. The section requires business wear, safety, insurance, family labor, and default exposure to remain visible. Extension is productive only where training, accounts, standards, quality control, repair, and shared tools remain portable across several platforms, banks, carriers, buyers, and trades. An intermediary that captures customers, designs, or margins reproduces dependent subcontracting.

Hours, referrals, shared assets, extension participation, platform choice, buyer concentration, common-service charges, defaults, housing exposure, firm graduation, skill transfer, and access by outsiders remain open. Success may include transferable skill and a reusable local service network even when one household firm closes, but neighborhood trust can also enforce racial, religious, class, or gender exclusion and conceal women's unpaid labor.
