The article establishes Schreyer's wartime electronic experiments, his retention within a Berlin–Telefunken communications establishment with radar and circuit-engineering support, and an operating electronic machine around 1951–52 followed by limited production. An industrial team could not move between those endpoints on a demonstration alone: modules had to be reproducible, faults measurable, subsystems testable, and maintenance information shareable. The bridge states those ordinary engineering functions and makes them the basis of Schreyer's transition to program leadership. It does not invent an office title, named department, patent, staffing level, test standard, valve count, plant, exact machine configuration, or procurement decision.
