Emergency authority was previously distributed among the prerogative, legislation, reconstruction, war, colonial, and African-governance pages. This article separates the inherited 1920 civil-emergency framework from the wider 1939 wartime delegation, then leaves the alternate post-1941 continuance and post-defeat transition open. It treats proclamation, regulation, requisition, deployment, detention, supply, compensation, and territorial intervention as distinct legal acts. The African and associated-state sections preserve the existing lower-confidence status of incorporated emergency institutions and invite regional challenge to a Westminster account that might otherwise mistake a named office or review route for effective consent and remedy.
