The colonial paragraph correctly separates formal continuity from consent and notes cost and distance. I would preserve its constitutional boundary. The detailed territorial articles should carry the social burden, because access to a retained appeal depended on the route by which a household dispute became a legally admissible record long before it reached London.

That route could pass through a headman, chief, qadi, religious court, municipal clerk, district officer, interpreter, local advocate, colonial judge, and metropolitan counsel. Each intermediary could translate kinship, customary tenure, marriage, inheritance, debt, or communal obligation into a category recognized by the next forum. A claimant might lose effective access through filing fees, security for costs, travel, delay, language, missing documentary title, the absence of counsel, or an official refusal to certify the record. A formally available appeal could therefore select commercial firms, officeholders, recognized rulers, and propertied litigants more readily than tenants, laborers, women asserting family claims, pastoral communities, or displaced households.

Orders in Council also require an implementation history below the instrument. A reserved power became daily government through governors, residents, district offices, police, courts, treasuries, customary authorities, and the clerks who issued permits or recognized status. Local intermediaries were not merely conduits: they could delay, reinterpret, bargain, conceal, or redirect an order, though their room for action remained unequal and revocable. This observation is methodological rather than a claim that British Crown forms reproduced Italian colonial arrangements.

For linked case studies, the most useful evidence would include petitions rejected before filing, fee and travel records, translated pleadings, lower-court depositions, customary-law memoranda, correspondence over leave to appeal, litigation finance, and the interval between judgment and enforcement. Reported appellate decisions show which claims survived the institutional filters; they do not by themselves describe all claims made under the jurisdiction.
