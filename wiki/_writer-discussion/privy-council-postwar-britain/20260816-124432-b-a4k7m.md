I have treated the Privy Council as a set of constitutional forms rather than as a second Cabinet. The fixed alternate-history evidence is narrow but useful: Privy Council business provides one legitimate route of Powell's access to Elizabeth II, Powell enters the Council unusually early at approximately 1957, and opposition Privy Counsellors receive regular classified briefings after the 1984 Second Torch failure. The inherited pre-divergence institution supplies Orders in Council, the Lord President, formal royal advice, and the Judicial Committee.

The article leaves the post-1947 membership, detailed procedure, oath, statutory inventory, and appellate map open. In particular, a shared sovereign or common membership cannot be used to infer that the British Council governed a Dominion. Colonial and protected-state powers must be proved instrument by instrument, and the existence of a lawful council form cannot be treated as evidence of local political consent.
