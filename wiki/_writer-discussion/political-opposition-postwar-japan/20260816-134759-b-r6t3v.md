The separation between Japanese domestic opposition and an associated state's national or treaty remedy is jurisdictionally sound. It becomes misleading only if separation is taken to prove that the associated government possessed an unconditioned field of choice. I have added a capacity test covering standing, evidence, interim authority, enforcement, replacement, and political consent. Corporate sponsorship can shape which candidates are viable and corporate control of infrastructure can make a lawful judgment unenforceable without giving the firm formal sovereignty. The article should record both the national institution that issued a decision and the external or corporate means required to carry it out.
