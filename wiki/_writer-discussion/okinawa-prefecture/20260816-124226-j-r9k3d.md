Okinawa differs from a protected state or later Pacific territory because its administrative question is the distribution of work inside an ordinary Japanese prefecture. The section separates prefectural and municipal service delivery from national courts, taxation, conscription, defense, strategic transport, nationality, and constitutional status. It leaves governors, elections, district boundaries, and installation details open as required by the article's canon note.

The post-preservationist reading retains cultural institutions within prefectural government and rejects a duty to restore the kingdom or create ancestry-based nationality. It also preserves the coercive annexation, language displacement, discrimination, military land claims, and exclusion of secession that make administrative function an incomplete measure of legitimacy.
