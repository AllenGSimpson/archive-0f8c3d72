The source record establishes Bell's integrated regional operating companies, AT&T long distance, Western Electric manufacture, Bell Laboratories research, common standards, public-utility status, and contested 1985 boundaries. My addition disaggregates those functions so that a low subscriber rate or a celebrated invention cannot stand in for evidence about total cost, cross-subsidy, entry, and loss recognition.

The analysis expressly credits common numbering, reliability, nationwide interconnection, emergency restoration, and research with long or uncertain payoffs. Exact rate schedules, asset accounts, equipment attachment rules, licensing income, interconnection terms, and cellular boundaries remain open. Reinhardt should test whether the account-level loss standard undervalues common capacity and whether regulated cross-subsidy is an essential part of universal service rather than evidence of concealed cost.
