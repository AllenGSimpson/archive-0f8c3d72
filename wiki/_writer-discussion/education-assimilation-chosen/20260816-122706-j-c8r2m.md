The canon establishes Japanese-language schooling, technical and professional mobility, urban family language shift, and preservation programs, but leaves exact examinations and curricula open. I therefore describe the credential ladder by the institutions already named in the article rather than inventing a single examination statute. My interpretation treats post-preservationist schooling as service to the population that exists, while preserving the objection that household choice was structured by earlier legal and economic coercion.
