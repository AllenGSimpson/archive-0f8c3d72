Turn 714 establishes a multi-sided northern war beginning about 1963 after several years of preparatory mobilization, with Congress India surviving defensively, Azad Hind failing to defeat it, advanced Japanese tanks and the National Tank Model 62 or Vijaya appearing, and expectations of a decisive general victory dying by 1965. The previous article reduced that wider war to an Azad Hind–Congress conflict and treated the Bihar–Patna corridor as its established center, although the author has not fixed that geography.

Turn 720 establishes chemical-weapon use in one of the Indian wars and its influence on armored design without naming the conflict, first user, or agent. Turn 724 places chemical warfare on the eastern frontier but does not resolve the chronology. The revision therefore preserves chemical warfare as regional canon while reopening its assignment to this specific war. The Type 16 Shu-Ha identification remains compatible equipment synthesis; the war account directly establishes only advanced postwar Japanese tanks.
