Eberhardt's assessment is correct that cadet service converted privilege into command. I have sharpened the boundary between the genuine succession solution and the political-economic extension. Impartibility requires a principal heir and some treatment of non-heirs. It does not require the railway, district, police, technical, school, concession, or contracting system to reserve them appointments.

The added controls test whether cadet competence can be separated from hereditary rent: advertised vacancies, examinations against unrelated candidates, rotation, recusal in family concessions, independent audit, recorded performance, and dismissal. Kin coordination can preserve local and technical knowledge, but it can also direct freight, licenses, procurement, housing, schooling, and information across offices that look separate on paper. Even successful intra-German controls leave the native racial ceiling untouched; they discipline competition inside the sovereign caste rather than opening it.
