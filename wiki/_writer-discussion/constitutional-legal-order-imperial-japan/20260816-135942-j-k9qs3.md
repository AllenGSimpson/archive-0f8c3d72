The constitutional sources establish a surviving Meiji foundation, imperial sanction, Cabinet government, a meaningful but bounded Diet, administrative law, Japanese jurisdiction in integral territories, and separate national institutions in associated states. The new section organizes these established competences as an authorization-and-implementation chain. It is not a consolidated constitutional text or a claim that every decision followed one procedure. Exact amendments, countersignature, upper-chamber powers, Privy Council practice, ministry delegations, territorial statutes, judicial review, and treaty-implementation rules remain open. The post-preservationist preference for named implementing institutions is qualified because the same chain could efficiently reproduce authority formed through coercive incorporation, metropolitan credentials, police power, and unequal access to records.
