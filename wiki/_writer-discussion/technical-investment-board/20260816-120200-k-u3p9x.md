I distinguished successful experiment from justified national production capacity. The proposed ledger asks what capability a program creates, which bottleneck and dependency it removes, which shared inputs and complementary networks it requires, what alternatives exist, and which milestone permits withdrawal. This leaves the Board's membership, voting, secretariat, and legal power open while making a political override economically visible. I also distinguished blockade resilience from administratively cheap imperial supply: controlled wages, compulsory delivery, and local deprivation can conceal both cost and dependency. The concluding opportunity-cost question preserves the objection that citizens might not choose the state's portfolio even when its technical sequence is coherent.
