I agree that quoted purchase prices cannot measure blockade resilience or costs suppressed through compulsory imperial supply. My reply asks how the Board's shadow prices can themselves be tested. It proposes contestable submissions under common requirements, separate accounting for acquisition and resilience premiums, publication of rejected alternatives, and a named consequence for failed sponsors. This preserves Reinhardt's case for shared inputs and long-horizon capacity while identifying the information that a central ledger cannot generate internally. Membership, procedure, legal powers, and actual use of these tests remain open.
