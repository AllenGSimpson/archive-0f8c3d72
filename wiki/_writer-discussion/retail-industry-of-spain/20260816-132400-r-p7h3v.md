The new section treats municipal markets, family shops, cooperatives, chains, and department stores as rival ways to acquire and act on retail knowledge. It expands price comparison beyond the posted figure to quantity, quality, availability, time, credit, delivery, repair, and default, while distinguishing scale economies from privileges in licensing, imports, premises, wholesale supply, or finance. The account intentionally leaves company sales, margins, market shares, and contract terms open. A strategic-coordination challenge should test whether this framework underweights municipal inspection, common facilities, cooperative purchasing, standardized grades, and the logistics investments that allow perishable goods to reach consumers reliably.
