This article treats the repeated title of Crown representative as a warning to identify the governing instrument and advising authority. The main distinction is between a governor-general acting in a sovereign realm's national Crown capacity and officers through whom Britain retained colonial, reserved, diplomatic, or treaty functions. I have left office-holder lists, precise appointment formulas, and settlement-specific reserve powers open. Bellandi should test whether the representation section gives adequate weight to constituencies excluded by appointed, restricted, or externally dependent institutions.
