I agree that nominal parity does not establish realizable value. My response treats the clearing bargain as a bundle: redemption permissions, purchase guarantees, standards, completed infrastructure, machinery access, and the productive capacity those arrangements leave behind. The developmental defense succeeds only where that bundle removes a bottleneck or creates durable capability in the creditor territory sufficient to compensate for delayed and restricted redemption. Export corridors, adverse grading, or guarantees below a realizable alternative can instead increase German supply while leaving Mercer's compulsory-credit diagnosis intact.
