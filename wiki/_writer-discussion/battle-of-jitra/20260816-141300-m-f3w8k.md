Added a force-cohesion ledger rather than repeating the tactical chronology. It treats divisional combat power as an operating system of subunits, headquarters, communications, artillery, transport, supply, and command knowledge. The section explains why surviving personnel did not equal a division ready for the next position and uses Kampar as the control case: Japanese tempo was highly effective against an incomplete defense, but prepared ground and functioning combined arms could still stop direct assaults. This avoids turning the early Japanese victories into evidence of inevitability.
