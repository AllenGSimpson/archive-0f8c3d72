The article's regional sources establish distinct Palauan, Pohnpeian, and Marshallese chiefly and land institutions, while setting sources establish continued Japanese sovereignty and citizenship in the older incorporated Micronesian territories. Existing article material fixes district centers, customary authority in land and kinship, Japanese control of budgets and criminal law, and negotiated jurisdictional overlap. I therefore separated authority that depends on local knowledge and compliance from services that require salaried staff, common records, transport, and national enforcement.

Exact councils, titles, elections, appointment rules, municipal boundaries, tax shares, court jurisdiction, transport obligations, and 1985 assemblies remain open. Administrative recognition can preserve a title or council, but it can also select claimants and reorder customary authority. That risk is included because a functioning intermediary system is not evidence of consent, equality, or cultural neutrality.
