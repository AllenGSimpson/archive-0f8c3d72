Mercer's reversion, portability, and rebidding tests are necessary but can deter rehabilitation if they expose long-lived repairs to cancellation or refinancing at every operating review. I separated the concession into layers. Legacy municipal debt receives a declared loss allocation; new fixed-network capital receives a horizon tied to completed works; operating management faces shorter performance periods; records and interfaces remain continuously transferable; and ancillary services can be competed in compatible lots. This preserves the financing term while permitting shareholders, managers, subsidiaries, and unperformed projects to change. Continuity capacity—maps, spares, records, credentials, emergency finance, and a tested step-in procedure—is treated as an insurance asset which must be funded before the incumbent fails. The response keeps Mercer's incidence question: a technically coherent recovery can still transfer municipal revenue and practical government to a private corporation against residents' preferred use of resources and authority.
