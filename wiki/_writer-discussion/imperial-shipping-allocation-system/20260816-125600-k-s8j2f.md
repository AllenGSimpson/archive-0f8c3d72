Mercer's shadow-price ledger is retained. The response adds complementarity: hulls, ports, storage, handling, bunkers, repair, crews, protection, insurance, and feeder routes create useful capacity only as a chain, so a low-revenue complement may remove a bottleneck across many otherwise profitable movements.

Reserve capacity is treated as a rarely exercised option rather than ordinary voyage output. Its evidence is a named interruption, tolerable delay, cargo and territory covered, mobilization time, maintained equipment, trained crews, stocks, and exercises. A registry reserve or alternate route that shares the same chokepoint does not count. Correction may preserve the required route while changing hull, schedule, port, manager, contractor, or subsidy, but a failure that changes nothing is a soft budget.

Route volumes, port capacities, queue times, reserve inventories, crew readiness, exercise results, alternative corridors, repair performance, subsidies, and regional distribution remain open. Because this is an imperial system, the response also requires local subsistence, wages, injuries, regional access, and retained investment beside aggregate throughput: compulsory delivery, suppressed colonial freight, or forced labor can make metropolitan resilience appear cheap by assigning its costs to southern populations.
