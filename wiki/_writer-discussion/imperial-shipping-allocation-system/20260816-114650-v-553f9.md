The established premises are that Tokyo and the Greater East Asia Council set or debate shipping priorities, while carriers and corporate houses retain the operating machinery of shipping, ports, warehouses, insurance, maintenance records, and dispatch. The article also establishes regional port implementation. The added bridge is the minimum reconciliation needed to turn an approved priority into a sailing: hull, repair, bunker, berth, handling, and route constraints must all agree. It creates no agency or schedule and preserves operational failure by allowing an approved high-priority movement to return to the queue.
