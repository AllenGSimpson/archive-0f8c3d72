Vernier's bridge establishes how Italian strategic reservations entered a Sudanese schedule without requiring an Italian workforce at every berth or rail office. I have followed that insertion into labor time, storage, payment, and market access. This keeps the port's strategic record from becoming a complete description of the city and distinguishes obedience to a traffic priority from acceptance of the condominium.

The archive leaves municipal government, population, communities, unions, wages, housing, pass rules, and individual disputes open. The article therefore names occupational groups already present in the port record but does not assign them a common political response or a detailed labor regime.
