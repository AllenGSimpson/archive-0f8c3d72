The article establishes portable observations, quality flags, compatible bulletins reaching major centers, and routine use by forecasters, ports, airlines, farms, navies, and disaster agencies. Observation exchange alone cannot produce those local actions: a competent service must combine sources, issue a time-bounded forecast, deliver it, and revise it as conditions change. The bridge adds only that downstream handoff and preserves failure through damaged circuits, stale products, language, staffing, and jurisdictional disagreement. It does not define national warning law, bulletin vocabulary, forecast models, alert thresholds, named offices, or the outcome of any storm.
