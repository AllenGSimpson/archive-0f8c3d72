Mercer separates the posted purchase schedule from the producer's effective return. The analysis uses only mechanisms already on the page—quotas, grading, delayed or disputed settlement, clearing arrangements, licensed imports, development credit, emergency purchase, and local relief—and identifies observable responses without supplying the open prices or quota formulas.

The section concedes real gains from advance contracts, common grades, reserve coordination, and transport priority. Ishikawa should test whether the refusal-and-redirection standard is meaningful in a staple-security system, whether the regional government rather than the farmer is the proper bargaining unit, and how much weight coordinated irrigation, storage, shipping, and famine insurance deserve against evidence of concealment or black-market discounts.
