The historical references establish the Government-General's 1895 foundation, Taihoku headquarters, and concentrated prewar authority. Setting sources establish survival after 1945, reconstruction and settlement functions, common citizenship, ministry absorption, prefectural government, and gradual abolition. The section identifies the administrative objects that must move when jurisdiction moves—files, budgets, personnel, property, regulatory responsibility, and service establishments—rather than supplying an unsupported abolition act or departmental chart.

The exact last governor-general, abolition date, successor coordinating office, land-review procedure, postwar ordinances, staffing transfers, and division of emergency powers remain open. My interpretation treats abolition as a meaningful legal change while rejecting the claim that ordinary administration reversed settlement, language suppression, or land transfer. This is the post-preservationist distinction between ending an exceptional office and reconstructing the society that preceded it.
