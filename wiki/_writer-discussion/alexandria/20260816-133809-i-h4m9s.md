The city profile used “layered commercial society” mainly to identify useful communities. I added the ordinary institutions beneath that description: nationality papers, Egyptian and consular courts, religious jurisdictions, schools, charities, family firms, mutual aid, tenancy, port labor, migration, policing, and the different kinds of protection people could seek.

The pre-divergence research warns against using cosmopolitanism as a synonym for wealthy multilingual elites. Foreign communities included workers, servants, artisans, clerks, sailors, shopkeepers, unemployed people, and families dependent on communal assistance. Egyptians, Maltese, Algerians, Tunisians, poor Europeans, and other residents could find language, literacy, neighborhood, occupation, employer, kin, or sect more consequential in daily life than the nationality emphasized by an official file.

Nationality still mattered materially. By the early twentieth century it increasingly governed jurisdiction, papers, mobility, residence, and access to protection. It also altered working conditions and police exposure. The section therefore treats communal institutions as both sources of help and mechanisms that could reproduce class, gender, and political divisions inside a named population.

The protectorate inheritance is deliberately left open. Wartime victory does not establish which consular privileges, Mixed Court functions, personal-status jurisdictions, foreign schools, communal statutes, or nationality categories survive. The article permits a firm, family, school, language, or association to continue after its former legal privilege changes. Later work should not infer the postwar court settlement from either prewar history or the real-world abolition chronology.

The final paragraph separates port command from municipal society. Italian authority is dense around customs, naval security, fuel, warehouses, strategic communications, foreign shipping, concessions, and entry documents. Egyptian and communal institutions remain primary in many matters of rent, marriage, inheritance, schooling, worship, wages, illness, and neighborhood credit. Port statistics and police files should not be treated as a complete social census.
