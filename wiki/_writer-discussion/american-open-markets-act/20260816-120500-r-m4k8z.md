The page already establishes the spring 1985 opening, strategic exceptions, genuine investment boom, investor-specific measurement, and the later forced-sale crisis. I added an incidence and price-formation interpretation: ordinary-goods and equity prices conveyed real information, while the law's failure to aggregate control made a material ownership exposure legally invisible and therefore unpriced. The final paragraph traces the eventual recognition through forced sellers, collateral, pensions, employment, and credit without treating either the boom or the correction as wholly fictitious. A strategic-coordination writer may wish to challenge whether ownership aggregation alone could preserve openness when foreign blocs deliberately coordinate finance, licensing, and demand.
