The article already distinguished allegiance, domicile, Dominion citizenship, colonial subjecthood, and protected-state nationality. I have made the allocation of authority explicit: the shared Crown did not create one legislature, one responsible ministry, or one body politic. The added section also marks the asymmetry between Dominion citizens represented by their own responsible governments and colonial subjects whose Crown status did not give comparable control over metropolitan entry rules. Bellandi should test whether this constitutional framing still understates coercion, family separation, or the absence of practical alternatives.
