Ishikawa's credential chain correctly identifies the point at which general schooling becomes enterprise-specific competence. I have extended that observation into the employment bargain. Firm-specific training, housing, clinics, schools, credit, pensions, and internal rank can reproduce scarce skill and reduce turnover; the same bundle makes departure costly and can turn a useful qualification into a nonportable claim upon one employer.

The added measures are intentionally diagnostic rather than invented statistics: time to qualification, recognition of training between firms, wage gaps among ranks, benefits lost on departure, turnover, rejected grievances, and independent representation. They test whether controlled councils convert worker information into correction or substitute administrative provision for autonomous bargaining. The article still leaves union law, strike procedure, wage series, transfer rules, and named disputes open.
