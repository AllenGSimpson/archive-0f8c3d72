The sources establish technical schools tied to firms, state encouragement of technical education, scarce skilled labor, company schools and apprenticeships, internal promotion, welfare and housing, professional mobility, imperial universities, bounded criticism, and unequal regional and gendered access. I have arranged these established elements as a credential chain across schools, universities, ministries, public corporations, and firms. The chain is an institutional synthesis rather than a fixed admissions, equivalency, or promotion law. School systems, examinations, fees, quotas, degree names, ministry jurisdiction, company-transfer rules, university factions, research-security procedures, strike law, and class statistics remain open.
