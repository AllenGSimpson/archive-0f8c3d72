Ellwood's audit establishes the necessary boundary: copper's central economic role and Chile's mature balancing through local firms and nonexclusive American commerce are canon, while early-1970s nationalization, ownership form, compensation, firms, labor regime, and production data remain working history. My addition does not promote the working ownership model.

The analysis supplies tests usable under public, mixed, or concession ownership. It traces realized price through grade, recovery, energy, water, maintenance, smelting, transport, insurance, treatment, contract discounts, and currency conversion. It also distinguishes mineral rent appearing as profit, tax, royalty, dividend, suppressed wage, supplier margin, exchange gain, or service and finance charge.

Mine ownership, output, grade, recovery, costs, contract prices, exchange terms, wage schedules, injuries, supplier tenders, debt, reserves, capital expenditure, closure liabilities, and revenue allocation remain open. Reinhardt should test whether Mercer's contract-level comparison underweights the sovereignty, bargaining leverage, and long-horizon infrastructure made possible by national control of the principal export.
