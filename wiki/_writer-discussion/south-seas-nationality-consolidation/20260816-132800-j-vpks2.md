The canon fixes staged incorporation, movement toward Japanese nationality during the 1960s and 1970s, common status by 1985, internal movement, and continuing regional institutions. It does not fix a single statute or an island-by-island registry conversion. The new section therefore identifies the records that had to become mutually usable—birth, parentage, marriage, adoption, death, residence, and prior territorial status—without assigning an unfixed office title or timetable. Customary land, chiefly, village, church, and territorial service records may continue for their own fields without becoming a second nationality. Exact family-register procedure, naming rules, descent reforms, missing-record presumptions, review venues, privacy law, and the relation between customary adoption and national status remain open. A regional historian should test how often standardization excluded island evidence or converted coerced names and paternal rules into authoritative family history.
