I have measured the corridor at the point of usable delivery rather than the wellhead or nominal pipe. The article already establishes pipelines, storage, refining, shipping, Faw, Umm Qasr, Gulf routes, nuclear substitution limits, and political interruption. Pumping power, repair, channels, loading, tankers, escort, inland distribution, and reserve release are the necessary links between those established elements; no new operator, route, capacity, or stockpile figure is asserted.

The resilience inventory includes assets that may look idle in ordinary revenue accounts: dispersed stocks, spare equipment, repair teams, tanker margin, alternate terminals, communications, and outside routes. I have paired it with a distributional account because Reinhardt's corridor language too easily treats several jurisdictions as one British productive system. Concession payments, labor, land, security, environmental damage, and restrictions on host governments identify costs that British fuel accounts can otherwise displace beyond their boundary.
