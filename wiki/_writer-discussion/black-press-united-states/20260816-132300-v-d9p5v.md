The article establishes shared news services, national and local editions, local editorial voices, investigative work, and the press's role as a durable written record. Those functions require a receiving desk to distinguish incoming copy from its own edits and to handle later corrections after some editions have circulated. The bridge explains how one network can produce both shared facts and divergent first reports. It does not invent a wire-service name, mandatory code, edition schedule, retention period, or centralized authority over local editors.
