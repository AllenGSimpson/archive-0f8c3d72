Falklands–Malvinas War

The Falklands–Malvinas War is a prolonged naval-air siege between Britain and a sovereign German-equipped Argentina. It begins as a sovereignty seizure, becomes a test of Commonwealth endurance, and ends in a restrained British victory whose political boundaries are drawn by America.

The failed diplomatic road

Lisbon talks in 1981–82 and the 1983 Brazilian–Vatican Rio formula fail. Islanders reject any automatic road to Argentine rule; Buenos Aires rejects indefinite postponement. Argentina sets 31 March 1984 as the deadline for a sovereignty-transfer framework.

This is not an improvised diversion by a collapsing junta. Argentina has spent fifteen years integrating German doctrine, equipment, training, maintenance, submarines, missiles, armor, and domestic vehicle production into a national military project.

Operación Rosario

Argentina attacks in April 1984 without a traditional declaration of war. A reinforced British garrison of roughly 800–1,000 regulars and 200–300 local volunteers fights for about three days. Argentina then moves 12,000–16,000 personnel, artillery, mobile air defense, anti-ship missiles, radar, electronic warfare, and runway engineers onto East Falkland.

Britain retakes South Georgia and pushes the Argentine surface fleet toward mainland cover, but aircraft, missiles, and quiet U-boats inflict severe escort and transport losses.

Second Torch

Margaret Thatcher initially applies lessons from Kuwait and authorizes a July 1984 East Falkland landing. Integrated Argentine air, missile, mine, artillery, submarine, electronic, and ground defenses defeat it. The disaster becomes “Second Torch.”

Thatcher accepts responsibility and adopts the No Third Torch doctrine: no major operation merely because political calendars make delay uncomfortable. Britain secures West Falkland, develops infrastructure, blockades East Falkland, and waits for a sustainable lodgment.

Armed mediation and the long siege

Patrick Buchanan imposes armed mediation: no German troops or serving crews, no British attacks on the Argentine mainland or regime-change war, public arms restrictions, American monitoring, and an eventual settlement based on local self-government. Germany's covert Operation Nachtbrücke and American surveillance make the limits an active contest.

The 1985 campaign

Winter Quay and Argentine Tridente contest the West Falkland base; Lantern degrades the East Falkland warning network; and Crown Gate establishes the San Carlos bridgehead. Blackthorn takes Goose Green, but Cóndor del Sur nearly splits the lodgment at Sussex Ridge. Sceptre then captures Mount Pleasant without taking Stanley.

The corrected armored campaign pits British Chieftain Mk 10s and supporting vehicles against Argentine TAM-74A2s, Panzer XI Ausf. D(ar)s, VCTP-74s, and VBE-78 Ñandús. Challenger, Warrior, and Panzer XII do not serve in the campaign.

The capture of Stanley

Repaired runways, land-based air cover, blockade, attrition, repair capacity, and methodical ground operations allow Britain to take Stanley in 1986. The victory preserves the Commonwealth and validates strategic endurance without erasing the casualties, shipping failures, and political haste behind Second Torch.

Punta del Este Protocols

The American-, Brazilian-, Uruguayan-, and Vatican-mediated protocols restore British administration and require islander consent for any later sovereignty change. Argentina withdraws, renounces force for a fixed period, ends war-related German arrangements, and accepts limits on German southern bases.

Britain accepts no mainland occupation, regime change, or punitive unlimited exclusion zone. A multinational commission verifies the settlement. Britain wins the islands; the United States defines how far victory may extend.

The Falklands Commission then condemns discounted warnings, Kuwait analogies, the haste behind Second Torch, and inadequate reserve shipping and lift while validating later siege planning and Factory Reownership capacity. Thatcher accepts the findings and retires voluntarily in late 1987; the resulting leadership contest and Peace Election place reconstruction under David Ashcombe.
