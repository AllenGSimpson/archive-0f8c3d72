Reichskommissariat Mittelafrika

!

Viewer discretion. Mittelafrika is a racial colonial state whose stability rests on coercive registration, labor obligations, selective terror, and unequal reproductive and political law.

Reichskommissariat Mittelafrika is Germany's vast equatorial and central African colonial system. Created amid administrative collapse in 1947, it evolves from coercive census rule into the data-driven Mosaic Order: materially predictable for communities it recognizes, merciless toward people who remain outside its registry or obstruct strategic corridors.

Formation and the Counting Wars

Mittelafrika is established in 1947 from former Belgian, French, and British possessions. Buea remains the ceremonial capital, while the practical headquarters moves during the 1950s onto high ground above the former Léopoldville–Brazzaville complex as Kongostadt.

German is permanently the sole official language, but French, African clerks, missionaries, and former colonial staff remain indispensable during the 1947–57 bilingual emergency. Record destruction, European evacuation, loyalty purges, and German administrative overreach turn the 1948–52 General Registration into the Counting Wars: coercive census campaigns, village massacres, refugee flight, and the first attempt to make the colony legible.

The Mosaic Order

Pragmatic reform creates the African Language and Mediation Service, Tribal Cadastre, and recognized local jurisdictions. Registered communities receive predictable chiefly government, limited customary autonomy, wages, services, and communal benefits in exchange for taxes, labor, production, migration control, records, and political obedience.

The colony's claim is that every community occupies a visible place in a mosaic rather than disappearing into one undifferentiated labor mass. In practice, registration distinguishes people the state will bargain with from migrants, resisters, refugees, and corridor populations it may remove or destroy.

An empire of rails, rivers, and records

Paper and punch-card systems become regional computer centers and the linked MOSAIK network by the late 1960s. Rail and river corridors connect mines, Katanga, the Congo, Cameroon, Kribi, the Sahel, and Atlantic ports. The colony combines labor-intensive extraction with advanced logistics, aerospace facilities, and industrial processing.

It is not one uniform labor camp. Stable registered districts can contain wages, clinics, schools, markets, technical promotion, and communal dividends. Those benefits remain conditional upon an imperial system that sets production obligations, controls movement, and reserves sovereignty for Germans.

The German-speaking African technical estate

Katanga becomes the industrial core and center of a privileged German-speaking African technical estate. Engineers, clerks, managers, medical staff, and skilled workers can hold authority and enjoy living standards above poorer white settlers. Their existence contradicts the regime's public racial simplifications without giving them equal political membership.

Berlin therefore restricts attention as much for ideological embarrassment as for security. The technical estate is useful evidence that the system can promote competence, but dangerous evidence against the claim that German ancestry alone explains administrative capacity.

Kongo-Gesamtwerk and Kongosee

The Kongo-Gesamtwerk joins the Inga-Werke, Malebo-Sperrwerk, power, ports, transport, industry, resettlement, and registration into one continental project. The Sperrwerk below Pool Malebo creates Kongosee, a vast shallow and irregular drowned river world whose staged filling ends in 1976.

The lake displaces millions, creates a permanent navigation and service economy, and makes licensed ports, ferries, fuel, channels, and manifests central to colonial control. The total number of dead cannot be reconstructed because no reliable pre-project population denominator exists.

War of the Chiefs

Labor and registry refusals begin in 1969–71, develop into armed chiefly feud in 1971–72, and cascade across jurisdictions in 1972–74. The Kongosee- und Stromschutz, German regulars, local forces, and joint theater command suppress the principal coalitions in 1974–76; hard peace follows through 1978.

The Second Mosaic Settlement of 1977–79 retains chiefs and gives them fixed stipends, performance shares, protected succession, household privilege, and community dividends tied to registration, order, labor, infrastructure, and worker welfare. Workers remain directly paid. The bargain creates a wealthy Mosaic aristocracy and new forms of labor brokerage.

Mature Mosaic Order, 1980–2000

The Second Mosaic Settlement remains the administrative foundation through the end of the century. Recognized jurisdictions submit household, movement, labor, health, production, and succession records through district offices and the linked MOSAIK system. Chiefs receive stipends, performance shares, household privileges, and communal dividends; workers continue to receive direct wages.

Katanga's German-speaking African technical estate, the Kongo river-industrial system, strategic minerals, and colonial aerospace facilities remain central to production. German sovereignty and racial inequality continue. Limited liaison with Portuguese Angola, South Africa, and Rhodesia covers border cases, transit, refugees, disease, livestock, and mineral traffic without creating an alliance.

The unadvertised empire

Germany restricts access because of strategic minerals, Kribi aerospace, disease, counterinsurgency, and the contradictions of its own racial system. Foreign opinion therefore freezes Mittelafrika into the real images of the Counting Wars, forced labor, refugees, and later atrocities while missing stable districts, technical migration, and administrative complexity.

The outside picture is incomplete but not false. Counterinsurgency in the 1970s, unequal reproductive law, small-family incentives, manipulative medical messaging, and punitive sterilization exposed in the 1978–79 demographic scandal ensure that foreign condemnation remains rooted in genuine imperial discrimination.

Dienstflagge

Mittelafrika's service flag uses a Brunswick-green field charged with a white elephant passant. The flag of the Greater German Reich occupies the canton, marking Mittelafrika as a subordinate colonial administration rather than an independent African state.
