Tehran Concord

The Tehran Concord is the 1985 German–Japanese restraint agreement that freezes their Eurasian rivalry without partitioning every state between them. It recognizes limits, sovereignty, trade, and selected technical cooperation—but creates neither alliance nor joint command.

The Tehran conference

Wolfgang Friedrich Hitler and Prime Minister Yasuhiro Nakasone attend personally in December 1985. Emperor Akihito supplies court sponsorship without acting as negotiator. Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi hosts the meeting, and independent Iran co-signs the sovereignty and oil provisions.

The earlier provisional name Hakone Declaration is superseded by Tehran Concord. The agreement becomes fully effective in 1986.

The recognition lines

Germany's direct eastern empire ends at the Siberian slopes of the Urals; its recognition line follows those slopes south to the northern Caspian and terminates at Iran. Japan's line runs from northern Lake Baikal to the Arctic, follows Baikal's western shore, includes Mongolia but not Tannu Tuva, then encompasses Sinkiang, Afghanistan, and Pakistani successor states to the Indian Ocean. India lies in Japan's broader great-power sphere.

These are rules governing Berlin and Tokyo's restraint toward one another. They are not new international borders and do not automatically turn every government inside them into a client.

The states between

Central Asian successor states between the lines belong to neither sphere. They remain sovereign, fragmented, and open to trade, concessions, aid, and diplomacy. The same principle applies across the Siberian Frontier.

Iran is explicitly independent. Iraq remains German-aligned. Hyderabad enters Japanese association when the agreement takes effect but remains sovereign and separate from Azad Hind. The Concord's core rule is that recognition of outside interests does not erase local legal personality.

Oil, orbit, and controlled exchange

Japan receives preferred purchase rights to part of the oil produced by German-affiliated firms in Iraq and Iran. Reciprocal trade, incident notification, intelligence hotlines, and consultation reduce accidental escalation.

Tehran also regularizes Japanese modules already planned for the Orbitaler Raumhafen: a science and robotics module arrives in 1985, followed by logistics and life support in 1986. Cooperation covers docking, life support, robotics, materials, compact electronics, navigation, cargo handling, reactor materials, industrial heat, fuel safety, and space power.

Détente without alliance

The Concord lowers the chance that German and Japanese frontier clients will drag their patrons into direct war. It gives both empires access to useful trade and space technology while preserving strategic primacy inside their own institutions.

There is no automatic war obligation, integrated command, free movement of forces, or permanent settlement of Russian statehood. Tehran manages the rivalry; it does not end it.
