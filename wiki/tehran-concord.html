Tehran Concord

The Tehran Concord is the December 1985 German–Japanese restraint agreement that defines their Eurasian recognition lines while preserving the sovereignty of states and administrations inside them. It enters full effect through 1986 implementation measures and provides for trade, oil purchase preferences, incident consultation, and selected technical cooperation. It creates no alliance, joint command, or automatic war obligation.[a]

The Tehran conference

Wolfgang Friedrich Hitler and Prime Minister Yasuhiro Nakasone attend personally in December 1985. Emperor Akihito supplies court sponsorship without acting as negotiator. Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi hosts the meeting, and independent Iran co-signs the sovereignty and oil provisions.[a]

The earlier provisional Japanese-side name Hakone Declaration is superseded by Tehran Concord. The agreement becomes fully effective in 1986.[a]

The recognition lines

Germany's direct eastern empire ends at the Siberian slopes of the Urals; its recognition line follows those slopes south to the northern tip of the Caspian Sea and terminates at independent Iran. Japan's line runs from the northern tip of Lake Baikal to the Arctic coast, follows Baikal's westernmost shore, includes Mongolia but not Tannu Tuva, then encompasses sovereign Xinjiang, independent Afghanistan, and the Pakistani successor states along Iran's eastern frontier to the Indian Ocean. India lies in Japan's broader great-power sphere.[a]

These lines govern German and Japanese restraint toward one another. They are not new international borders and do not automatically turn every government inside them into a client.[a]

The states between

Central Asian successor states between the lines belong to neither sphere. They remain sovereign, fragmented, and open to trade, concessions, aid, and diplomacy. The same principle applies across the Siberian Frontier. The settlement supports neither-sphere neutrality but does not create a Central Asian alliance.[a]

Iran is explicitly independent, and Iraq remains German-aligned. Germany ends its special support for Hyderabad under Tehran; when implementation takes effect in 1986, Hyderabad enters formal Japanese association while remaining sovereign and separate from Azad Hind. Recognition of outside interests does not erase local legal personality.[a]

Oil, orbit, and controlled exchange

Japan receives preferred purchase rights to part of the oil produced by German-affiliated firms in Iraq and Iran. Reciprocal trade, incident notification, intelligence hotlines, force consultation in the Siberian Frontier, and restraint toward one another's clients reduce accidental escalation.[a]

Tehran regularizes Japanese modules already authorized for the Orbitaler Raumhafen. A science and robotics module arrives in 1985; a logistics and life-support module follows in 1986, carrying life-science, hydroponics, closed-cycle-environment, and long-duration-medicine functions. Cooperation covers docking, life support, robotics, materials, compact electronics, navigation, cargo handling, reactor materials, high-temperature systems, fuel handling, industrial heat, safety, and space power.[a]

Détente without alliance

The Concord lowers the chance that German and Japanese frontier clients will draw their patrons into direct war. It gives both empires access to trade and selected space technology while preserving separate strategic institutions.[a]

There is no automatic war obligation, integrated command, free movement of forces, or permanent settlement of Russian statehood. The agreement manages the rivalry without ending it.[a]
