German space-program founding generation

The program's public architect, Wernher von Braun, depended on a deliberately differentiated technical bench. Eberhard Rees converted designs into institutions and became the daily successor in 1977; the seven specialists below carried launch operations, science, guidance, production, stage integration, long-range strategy, and founder memory.

Selected historical figures

Historical people remain central where the divergence supplies a credible altered career: Adolf Hitler, Hermann Göring, Joseph Goebbels, Helmut Goebbels, Heinrich Himmler, Reinhard Heydrich, Walter Schellenberg, Erwin Rommel, Manfred Rommel, Ferdinand von Senger und Etterlin, Karl Dönitz, Günter Hessler, Albert Speer, Albert Speer Jr., Karl Schiller, Wernher von Braun, Eberhard Rees, Luigi Broglio, Michael Andreevich Romanov (Tsar Mikhail), Mikhail Kalashnikov, Joseph Stalin, Georgy Malenkov, Vyacheslav Molotov, Lavrentiy Beria, Georgy Zhukov, Nikita Khrushchev, Alan Turing, John F. Kennedy, Nelson Rockefeller, George Lincoln Rockwell, Pat Buchanan, Ronald Reagan, Charles Lindbergh, Margaret Thatcher, Harold Wilson, Benito Mussolini, Umberto II, Vittorio Emanuele, Francisco Franco, Juan Carlos I, António de Oliveira Salazar, Yasuhiro Nakasone, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, and the Catholic leaders indexed above.

The Character Dossier deliberately excludes historical people from its original-cast register. Individual biography pages maintain that distinction by grounding pre-divergence life in the historical record and citing the setting documents that control the altered career.

The founder-generation transition also includes Martin Bormann, the Party Chancellery broker whose machinery was divided after 1973; Werner Naumann, Goebbels's ministerial successor; and Erich Topp, operational head of the Kriegsmarine from 1970.

Portugal's post-Salazar succession is represented by Marcello Caetano, civilian head of government, and Duarte Nuno, Duke of Braganza, the rehabilitated royal claimant.
