Military Technology Portal

Military modernity develops without nuclear weapons becoming the organizing fact of strategy. Armor, submarines, guided conventional weapons, sensors, aircraft, logistics, computing, and orbital systems remain usable instruments of great-power competition.

A conventional technology clock

Jet engines, radar, guided weapons, night vision, digital fire control, nuclear propulsion, satellites, data links, and automation mature unevenly across blocs. No wartime atomic bomb creates a nuclear deterrence template, so conventional force remains politically thinkable and technologically prestigious.

Exact designation, weight, date, inventory, and status claims must follow the armament references. The setting distinguishes historical inheritance, setting extrapolation, working design, prototype, production, operational service, reserve, and retirement.

Land warfare

Germany leads modern armor, assault-rifle doctrine, night fighting, guided antitank weapons, and integrated mechanized formations. Japan adapts high technology to ships, rail limits, weak bridges, mountains, and jungle roads, emphasizing compact vehicles, hydropneumatic suspension, and manpower-saving automation.

Britain preserves heavy armor, reconnaissance families, local Commonwealth arsenals, and expeditionary repair. America divides battle taxis from reconnaissance and fields the M11A3 as its main 1985 tank while M13 enters service. Occitania pioneers division-level integration; Argentina combines German imports with indigenous TAM and VCTP designs.

Naval warfare

Germany builds the world's leading submarine system: quiet conventional boats, nuclear attack submarines, and submerged vertical-launch missile boats for conventional strikes on ports, airfields, rail, fuel, radar, and fleets. Japan and America possess the largest carrier forces.

Britain is the premier Commonwealth surface and anti-submarine navy. Italy and Spain operate peer Latin fleets; Portugal maintains a real Atlantic–Indian Ocean escort navy; Occitania supplies technical experiments; Argentina fields a complete regional navy rather than a token force.

Associated-state fleet building is equally important to the political order. The Philippine Independence Fleet—one cruiser, three destroyers, and four escorts transferred outright in 1960—shows how older Japanese hulls become national training, patrol, relief, and sovereignty institutions without approaching parity with the Imperial Japanese Navy.

Air and orbital warfare

Germany keeps distinct interceptor, air-superiority, strike, close-support, and maritime families. Japan combines naval aviation, data links, compact aircraft, and long-range anti-ship weapons. Britain preserves TSR-2, Harrier, and the Canadian Arrow because it cannot rely on American supply.

America retains three services: Army Air Corps for battlefield aviation, Naval Air Corps for carrier and maritime aviation, and the independent United States Aerospace Force for strategic interception, bombing, reconnaissance, missiles, spaceplanes, and orbital warfare. The Maracaibo Shock drives a move from atmospheric speed toward Zenith inspection and conventional orbital strike.
