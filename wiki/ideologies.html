Political Ideologies and Parties

The fractured peace is not a contest between one ideology and its opposite. Every major system claims order, modernity, participation, and national purpose; they differ over who belongs, where authority resides, and whether hierarchy can ever be challenged.

German National Socialism

The Reich evolves from revolutionary dictatorship into a technologically advanced imperial police state. Its National Socialist worldview makes race and the Volksgemeinschaft prior to the individual, the Führerprinzip the source of authoritative interpretation, the struggle principle a purported law of life, and eastern Lebensraum a historical imperative. Party, SS, firms, ministries, professional institutions, and the Führer share power without creating plural sovereignty. The Showroom Reich offers real prosperity to favored Germans while the eastern and African orders remain coercive caste systems.

The state maintains several layers of belief rather than one national theology. Public civil religion joins founder memory, Volk, work, family, and victory; Party orthodoxy supplies racial history and obedience; the SS racial order turns doctrine into ancestry review, policing, settlement, and hereditary status; and the restricted SS Order Faith develops a denser theology of ancestry, oath, household, settlement, and hereditary vocation. Wolfgang's second generation replaces permanent mobilization with bounded rivalry, standards, systems, and curated openness. It modernizes the method of rule without repudiating race or conquest.

Imperial developmentalism

Japan administers its sphere through categories: direct territory, chartered industrial state, guardian republic, associated sovereign, and resource administration. Its strongest doctrine says Japan first manufactures for Asia, then teaches Asia to manufacture, then designs the shared standards and infrastructure.

Military guardians, technocrats, Co-Prosperity idealists, and corporate imperialists disagree over how much partnership is real. The system's central question is whether service can justify an office permanently reserved to Japan.

Latin royal corporatism

Italy's shareholder corporate state, Spanish national Catholicism, Portuguese conservative corporatism, and Occitan Catholic regionalism cooperate without merging. Crown, Church, armed forces, public holdings, professional corporations, and private firms structure politics.

The Latin bloc is socially conservative and authoritarian but not ideologically uniform. Portugal resists Spanish or Italian command; Occitania supplies technical modernity; and the Vatican treats the bloc as a field of friendship rather than its temporal government. The council's religious immunity and Christian social order distinguish public Catholicism from coerced profession or party-state capture.

Commonwealth constitutional currents

Britain reconstructs through Crown currents, Commonwealth Labour, Ordered Particularism, National Liberal modernization, and Crown-and-Commonwealth conservatism. Each accepts some form of parliamentary rule, strategic public capacity, and continuing Commonwealth obligation.

The contradiction is imperial consent. The dominions possess real sovereignty, while colonies and African associations experience delayed, conditional, or violent self-government. Constitutional language can discipline power without automatically dissolving hierarchy.

The American party order

National Renewal presents corporatism, national service, family policy, industrial credit, racial order, and space achievement through American constitutional symbols. Its internal institutionalists prevent a full one-party break during Buchanan's presidency.

Democratic-Republicans fuse old rivals and eventually develop Reagan's constitutional free enterprise: competitive contracting, independent economic power, strategic capacity, social floor, and reciprocal rather than global markets. Both parties accept defense, science, immigration restriction, nuclear power, and hemispheric primacy; they disagree over how much independent life the national mission may leave outside politics.
