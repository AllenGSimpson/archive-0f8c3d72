The World Order

i

Canon status: the five-system framework and broad strategic relationships are settled.[a] Several borders, legal categories, client-state names, and exact power rankings remain working canon.[e]

The Second World War ends in 1947 without a single global victor. The Axis empires survive, Britain is defeated but intact, and a neutral United States emerges lacking the industrial or ideological confidence of historical wartime mobilization. The result is a multipolar cold war among five unequal systems.[a]

Balance of power

No system possesses enough strength to impose a universal order. Germany dominates continental Europe and European Russia, but it depends upon a populated forced-labor colony and must police an enormous land frontier. Japan commands the western Pacific and a rich resource empire, but its industrial core is narrow and its labor shortages are severe. Italy controls the Mediterranean crossings while remaining overextended. Britain retains a global network without its old imperial artery. America has continental scale but lacks the assumptions of automatic world leadership.[a]

Nuclear weapons do not define this contest. No state possesses one even in 2000. Atomic science develops around electricity, industrial heat, propulsion, and prestige. Deterrence instead rests upon geography, industrial depth, conventional arsenals and missiles, submarines, air and space power, occupation costs, and crisis management.[a]

Modernity belongs to no single ideology. Each bloc can build, educate, calculate, and inspire—and each purchases those achievements with its own forms of coercion.[e]

Einheitspakt

Einheitspakt is the generally recognized name for the German-centered continental system. Its imperial administration extends through Reichsprotektorate in Belgium, Norway, and Denmark, protectorate-grade economies in Hungary and Romania, sovereign associates such as Sweden, Finland, and Argentina, dependent western states, and a direct eastern colonial empire reaching roughly to the Urals. The Northern Shield joins the Scandinavian, Baltic, Arctic, and North Atlantic approaches. German settlement, coerced labor, deportation, administrative sorting, and security corridors restructure the conquered east without emptying it.[a]

The core Reich presents itself as orderly, cultured, prosperous, and selectively open. Its Showroom Reich permits foreign visitors to see consumer life, resorts, technology, model settlements, and civic ritual while screening the empire's foundations. Advanced land weapons, submarines, rocketry, computing, and automation give Berlin formidable prestige and export power.[a]

GEACPS / Dai Tōa Kyōeiken

The Japanese system is called the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere or GEACPS in English-language diplomatic writing and Dai Tōa Kyōeiken in Japanese, ideological, and intra-sphere contexts. It is administered through integral territories, principal associates, protected and dependent members, and external strategic associates rather than as one uniform colony.[a]

Japanese legitimacy rests on a progression—manufacturing for Asia, teaching Asia to manufacture, then setting the standards within which Asian production operates. The claim that hierarchy is an office justified by service is both the sphere's binding ideal and its central contradiction.[a]

Latin Bloc

The Latin Bloc is a de-facto designation for Italy, Spain, Occitania, a cautious Portugal, and Rome's subordinate Mediterranean states. Unlike the Einheitspakt or GEACPS, it begins without one comprehensive pact and develops clearing, defense, procurement, and Catholic-diplomatic institutions gradually after Mussolini's decline and the German–Italian split.[a]

Spain's purchase of Gibraltar is its greatest prestige victory and an enduring British grievance. Portugal uses Atlantic islands, African colonies, and its old British connection to resist absorption. Occitania contributes an industrial and technical base centered on Toulouse and the Rhône–Mediterranean system.[a]

British Commonwealth

Britain loses the war without invasion, occupation, or dissolution. It keeps the Crown, Parliament, Royal Navy, dominions, most Atlantic and sub-Saharan possessions, Gulf system, Aden, Ceylon, and—initially—Burma. It loses the Mediterranean chain and can no longer treat Suez as a secure imperial artery.[a]

The Cape route makes West Africa, South Africa, Rhodesia, Aden, the Gulf, and surviving Indian Ocean stations more important. A Gulf and eastern Mediterranean hierarchy identifies which positions London considers indispensable, endangered, or already lost. Defeat hardens rather than dissolves British imperial determination; decolonization becomes delayed, conditional, or violent. Reconstruction eventually yields a compact high-value Sterling Commonwealth and a limited return to great-power action.[a]

American Hemisphere

The United States remains formally neutral during the general war and receives none of the historical victory culture, mass veteran generation, or wartime industrial transformation. Its foreign policy begins inward and hemispheric. Later administrations convert the Monroe tradition into increasingly coercive intervention against German, Japanese, and Latin penetration.[a]

By 2000 the hemisphere has three overlapping maps. Security alignments still reflect American interventions and external footholds, cultural affinities cross those boundaries, and corporate systems spread farther than the states that sponsor them. Venezuela, Cuba, Mexico, Argentina, Peru–Ecuador, Chile, and the Commonwealth Caribbean therefore cannot be reduced to one-color bloc labels.[a]

The same distinction now operates globally. Corporate networks in India, Africa, Eurasia, and space cross alliance boundaries through finance, standards, maintenance, transport, data, and technical service. Their reach does not by itself change strategic alignment while the host state retains command, law, physical access, and a credible substitute during crisis.[a]

American politics realign around intervention, race, religion, corporate power, and the meaning of national renewal. The 1985 alignment chart shows that Washington's named hemisphere still contains Commonwealth states, Einheitspakt-associated Argentina, GEACPS-aligned Pacific partners, Latin influence, and explicit neutral balancers.[a]

The American opening and its limits

From 1985 the World Market Boom briefly makes New York the common capital market among the rival systems. The international response is neither uniform imitation nor simple rejection: Britain opens behind three firebreaks, Japan and Germany invest abroad while admitting only bounded claims at home, and the Latin states construct four different national bargains.[a]

The 1990 cross-bloc shock ends the experiment in broad foreign ownership while commerce survives. By the later 1990s the major systems converge on interoperability without financial integration—trade, standards, licenses, branches, joint production, and selected new investment across borders, with inherited ownership kept under national political control.[a]

Frontier zones

Neither empire fully owns the vast space between the Urals and the Japanese client belt. Siberian successor administrations, Central Asian states, refugee governments, military districts, railway polities, and local strongmen survive in shifting combinations. Iran remains explicitly independent. India, Afghanistan, and the Pakistani successors sit inside overlapping expectations rather than clean borders.[a]

The 1985 Tehran Concord recognizes German and Japanese restraint lines without erasing the sovereignty of states between them. It reduces the risk of direct war while leaving the frontier open to concessions, trade, intelligence, and competition.[a]
