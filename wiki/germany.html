Greater German Reich

!

Viewer discretion. This article discusses National Socialism, racial persecution, deportation, forced labor, and colonial mass violence in an alternate-history context.

The Greater German Reich is the principal land power of the postwar world and the center of a continental empire stretching from western Europe to the Urals. Its cultivated image of prosperity, technical achievement, and social security rests upon racial hierarchy, coerced labor, colonial extraction, and relentless political control.

Social-history companion: Everyday Life in Greater Germany, 1950–1985.

History

Victory and reconstruction

German victory follows the German–Soviet War and the cumulative defeat of Britain. The collapse of the Soviet state, Ural settlement, and evacuation of Soviet industry and population give Germany continental sovereignty to approximately the Urals while leaving a fragmented armed successor space beyond the frontier. The Lisbon settlement of 1947 recognizes the larger victory while leaving Britain sovereign.

Returning soldiers are divided among industrial release, reserves, reconstruction, settlement, and continued eastern service. Railways, housing, power, agriculture, and fuel receive priority as wartime plants reconvert under a permanent military floor. The 1948 Reichswährungsbereinigung clears monetary overhang. Goebbels converts victory into a story of homecoming and consumption, while Speer, Bormann, Himmler, Heydrich, Rommel, and Dönitz become the functional pillars beneath Hitler.

The Showroom Reich

During the 1950s the German core becomes the empire's curated front parlor. KdF tourism, the long-promised KdF car, standardized Meisterware goods, resorts, the Leipzig Trade Fair, and model settlements make political loyalty tangible. Through curated openness, foreign visitors see genuine German streets and technical modernity within a controlled geography of access, while eastern repression and imperial extraction remain screened, euphemized, or distant.

Government

The state is National Socialist, bureaucratic, technically ambitious, racially hierarchical, and increasingly pseudo-dynastic. Hitler remains final authority, but by the late 1950s ordinary government runs through the “Second Draft”: party offices, state secretaries, technical ministries, security services, and military staffs translate contradictory impulses into executable orders.

After 1962 the Reich Leadership Council and Reichsführungsordnung formalize collective administration. Wolfgang retains this machinery after his accession and creates a Personal Staff for Situation Review to receive dissenting memoranda, raw figures, and unharmonized estimates. The system modernizes its information flow without liberalizing its purpose.

The eastern empire

Germany directly controls European Russia approximately to the Urals. It does not empty the territory. Selected settlement and security corridors are cleared while millions remain as miners, railway workers, agricultural laborers, factory specialists, servants, auxiliaries, and coerced industrial workers. Others are killed, imprisoned, deported, or driven into a thinned and closely policed countryside.

The destructive first occupation produces the quota crisis of 1949–1952. The mature Eastern Reichskommissariats answer that failure through Germanization of sovereignty: German institutions monopolize law, command, strategic property, public literacy, and infrastructure while the subject population remains indispensable and politically subordinate. An estate order, occupational schooling, and automated logistical spine make the colonial system durable enough to reproduce itself.

German settlement concentrates in annexed Poland, the Baltic–Leningrad corridor, the Ukrainian grain belt, Crimea, the Don and Volga approaches, Caucasian oil districts, and strategic rail zones. Stalingrad is stripped of its Soviet name and rebuilt as Adolfstadt an der Wolga, almost universally shortened to Adolfstadt, a militarized transport and industrial center on the lower Volga. Poland ceases to exist as a sovereign state; its General Government becomes an overcrowded colonial labor reservoir. The wider imperial administration keeps such direct territories legally distinct from protectorates and associated states.

Political economy

The Reich is not fully autarkic. Its directed imperial market operates a managed commodity, currency, and clearing hierarchy. Mandatory deliveries coexist with protected local retention and surplus incentives; the Reich Office for Raw-Material Economy sets policy while Reich Goods Equalization moves and accounts for goods. Territorial currencies, the Einheitsmark, and the Verrechnungsmark let Berlin govern differentiated dependencies without making the empire administratively uniform.

Visible security and consumer gains for favored Germans coexist with coerced labor and colonial extraction. Chronic manpower demands encourage mechanization, standardization, automation, and computing. By the late 1960s Germany is the world's largest arms exporter.

When foreign buyers increasingly reject German clearing balances in the 1990s, Berlin introduces a separate external Goldmark. Domestic Reich and Einheitspakt money remains fiat; the hard instrument allows German banks to enter international bullion clearing without surrendering command over wages, taxes, and internal credit.

Society and everyday life

Ordinary life cannot be reduced to either a flawless fascist consumer paradise or a uniformly gray prison. The transcript's working model is a modern industrial police state wearing the costume of folkish national renewal: steady employment, family programs, radios, organized holidays, youth camps, sports fields, technical pride, surveillance, racial privilege, and a permanent imperial underclass exist together.

A veteran may have job security, ceremony, and a place on a regime-approved workers' council but no right to strike or organize independently. A skilled worker may finally receive the promised KdF car, though fuel and materials prevent American-style mass motoring. A mother gains housing and status through children while labor shortages continue pulling women into part-time work. The party describes all three roles—worker, parent, and consumer—as service.

Children encounter ideology as ordinary formation. Maps become moral diagrams; biology, physical education, and history teach hierarchy and destiny. Camps, uniforms, hikes, skills, and real companionship help make the system durable. Churches survive, especially in Catholic and Lutheran regions, but families learn separate public and private vocabularies because schools, charities, youth work, and the press remain political battlegrounds.

Daily fear is often administrative rather than spectacular. The block warden carries donation lists, household files, and a memory for missing flags. Foreign labor barracks, separate facilities, and regulated movement make racial hierarchy part of the street scene. German material security is therefore inseparable from the coerced labor and colonial extraction kept outside the favored citizen's political field of vision.

Layers of belief

The state does not require every citizen to share one esoteric doctrine. Public civil religion emphasizes Volk, ancestors, descendants, work, family, service, founder memory, and victory. Party orthodoxy supplies racial history and political obedience. The restricted SS Order Faith develops theology, household order, marriage, settlement, oath, and hereditary vocation into a denser system. Its 1979 religious constitution gives the inner SS estate church-like institutions without making it Germany's established national religion. Technical ministries translate related racial objectives into databases, health indices, labor allocation, school selection, and resource plans.

Private attitudes range from conviction to inherited privilege, selective Christian accommodation, technical loyalty, careerism, cynicism, quiet nonbelief, and underground dissent. The regime endures because it has enough believers to lead, beneficiaries to defend it, conformists to operate it, and coercion to contain the rest—not because every household thinks alike.

The ruling reproductive order is rigidly pronatalist for Germans while applying different standards to subject peoples. The formally voluntary colonial contraceptive program operates inside deeply coercive power relationships. The 1978–79 scandal produces narrower safeguards without dismantling the racial empire.

Historical memory and strategic play

Public memory of the Second World War treats victory as proof of organized national capacity while allowing criticism of incompetence, corruption, and excess inside the legitimacy of the victorious system. Democracy is represented more often as divided and short-sighted than as inherently evil.

The same institutional culture helps make Germany the principal home of the computerized strategic staff game. Commercial simulations translate logistics, railways, command delay, state capacity, and imperfect information into entertainment, while fictional settings allow the mechanics to cross rival censorship systems.

Military and technology

The Wehrmacht leads in land weapons, rocketry, advanced submarines, guided weapons, night fighting, satellites, and calculation-heavy space infrastructure. Its fleet emphasizes nuclear and conventional attack submarines, guided-missile submarines, and small missile combatants. Carriers and large surface ships serve prestige and presence more than the core war plan.

The first German lunar landing occurs on 20 December 1970. The Orbitaler Raumhafen opens publicly in 1975, combining docks, workshops, fuel systems, reactor power, and partial-gravity habitation.

The founding space team distributes authority across Wernher von Braun, Eberhard Rees, Kurt Debus, Ernst Stuhlinger, Helmut Gröttrup, Arthur Rudolph, Konrad Dannenberg, Krafft Ehricke, and Hermann Oberth. Their biographies distinguish the historical pre-divergence record from the careers preserved by German victory.

German computing also originates the Weltnetz common transport and the Seitenplan directory system. Their global adoption gives German institutions influence over standards and network discovery without placing all foreign traffic under German control.

The second generation

Hitler and Eva Braun marry discreetly before Wolfgang Friedrich Hitler is born in late 1947. Hitler dies on 12 February 1962. Göring succeeds peacefully, designates Wolfgang as eventual successor, and voluntarily transfers authority when Wolfgang turns eighteen in late 1965.

Wolfgang's state remains designated rather than automatically hereditary in law, but it is dynastic in every practical and emotional sense. He marries physician Katharina Elisabeth Hartmann in 1970; their seven children become both a genuine family and an invaluable instrument of regime legitimacy. The succession to Wolfgang still requires designation and institutional confirmation: no child receives an automatic right to rule.

The same anti-monopoly principle governs the retirement of the old guard. Otto Seidel receives cadre appointments without Bormann's control of law or access; Werner Lenz receives technical-investment coordination without Speer's whole ministry; and Matthias Kern receives domestic audit without Heydrich's foreign intelligence or household security. The result is a more legible but still authoritarian second-generation leadership system.
