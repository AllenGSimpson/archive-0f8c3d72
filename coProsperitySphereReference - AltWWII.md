# Greater East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere Reference

## Scope and authority

This document is the controlling geographic, constitutional, economic, and political reference for Japan's Greater East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere and its adjoining Asian client system. It synthesizes the authoritative **Co-Prosperity Sphere Analysis** branch preserved in [transcript.md](./transcript.md), including the user's corrections made later in that branch.

It governs the Russian Far East, Mongolia, China, Korea, Indochina, Manchukuo, the Japanese chartered-corporate system, the East Indian successor states, Malaya, Thailand, Burma, Azad Hind and the Indian successor wars, the Sphere's monetary architecture, Japan's Pacific possessions, and Taiwan. Dedicated land, naval, aerospace, computing, Catholic, and material-culture references continue to control exact equipment, technical, ecclesiastical, and product details. Direct authorial corrections remain superior to any working synthesis here.

## The Sphere in one sentence

The Co-Prosperity Sphere is not a single empire with one citizenship or one constitution. It is a Japanese-presided association of integral imperial territories, protected monarchies, sovereign associates, treaty dependencies, corporate concession systems, and strategic partners whose legal differences are deliberate instruments of rule.

Japan provides the military ceiling, financial clearing system, strategic transport, advanced machinery, shipping, and final arbitration. Member states retain flags, rulers, national laws, currencies, local armies, and unequal amounts of real autonomy. The Emperor remains Emperor of Japan; he is not a supranational emperor, and State Shinto is not the Sphere's common religion.

## Constitutional order

### Status hierarchy

The mature order has four practical levels:

1. **Japan and its integral territories.** Korea, Taiwan, Karafuto, the older Micronesian possessions, and incorporated Pacific territories belong legally to Japan. Their inhabitants possess Japanese nationality even where ethnic, linguistic, and regional distinctions remain.
2. **Principal associates.** Manchukuo, the National Government of China, Thailand, and the Philippines possess the greatest diplomatic weight among associated states. They are not equal to Japan in military authority, but Tokyo normally governs them through treaty, finance, conferences, and negotiated implementation rather than direct orders.
3. **Protected and dependent members.** Mongolia, the Russian Far Eastern State, Indochinese kingdoms, East Indian states, Burma, Papua New Guinea, and other members retain sovereignty in form and varying degrees of local substance while conceding defense, alignment, concessions, or strategic transport to Japan.
4. **External strategic associates.** Azad Hind is Japan's indispensable eastern-Indian partner but remains outside the Sphere's ordinary institutional obligations. Hyderabad enters Japanese association after the Tehran Concord without becoming subordinate to Azad Hind.

The Sphere's conferences seek consensus and issue common programs that national governments implement through their own laws. Japan chairs the system and possesses a reserve supremacy which it tries not to invoke openly. Tokyo's success depends upon making Japanese primacy appear normal, useful, and procedurally mediated.

### Nationality and residence

There is no Co-Prosperity citizenship. Nationality is inherited, usually through the father under the prevailing civil codes. Birth or long residence in another member state does not automatically naturalize a person, and dual nationality is normally rejected. A Japanese settler in Manchukuo remains Japanese; a Manchukuoan resident in Osaka remains Manchukuoan unless formally naturalized under exceptional law.

Treaty residents may work, hold restricted property, use courts, attend designated schools, and maintain permanent domicile without voting or becoming citizens. Two major exceptions develop:

- **Vladivostok/Urajio** operates selective civic naturalization, normally after five years for skilled, economically useful, or politically trusted residents.
- **Singapore** uses a broader commercial naturalization system because its port economy cannot function through rigid inherited communities alone.

The early-1980s **National Home and Return Mandate** guarantees that inherited citizens may return to their state of nationality, resettle, and convert educational or professional credentials. It solves the problem of stranded nationals without creating a common citizenship.

### Integral Japan and cultural nationality

Koreans, Taiwanese, Karafuto residents, and incorporated islanders are Japanese citizens in public law. Their Korean, Taiwanese, Ryukyuan, Micronesian, or other identities are ethnic and cultural identities within Japan, not foreign citizenships. This is compatible with unequal assimilation, regional discrimination, preservation movements, and distinct household languages. It is not compatible with describing Korea as a sovereign protectorate by the 1970s.

## Japan's operating machinery

### State and corporation

The zaibatsu and great trading companies are not sovereign states, but they are the operating machinery through which Japanese primacy becomes material. Mitsui, Mitsubishi, Sumitomo, Yasuda, Mantetsu, shipping firms, banks, utilities, retailers, hotels, insurers, and specialist industrial combines move together along treaty corridors. They finance ports, power plants, mines, mills, railways, communications, housing, and commercial districts while Japanese ministries define the strategic envelope.

Japanese firms abroad retain Japanese ownership and strategic control. They do not gradually become locally owned merely because their labor force and middle management become local. Host governments can tax, regulate labor, demand public access, insist on local managers, police company districts, and compel infrastructure continuity; they cannot simply seize the controlling shares without turning a commercial dispute into a state-to-state crisis.

### The corporate-host bargain

By the 1970s associated governments test the limits of corporate autonomy. Their preferred demands concern taxation, wages, policing, environmental burdens, local promotion, housing, and access to roads or utilities. Corporations answer through investment delay, supplier pressure, credit conditions, transfer pricing, personnel withdrawal, and selective maintenance.

Tokyo intervenes only when a dispute threatens strategic transport, defense production, Japanese controlling ownership, sensitive technology, or the stability of the Sphere. It becomes less willing to defend monopoly profits, private municipal power, or the exclusion of all local managers. Below that threshold, corporations often win. This teaches host politicians that escalation to Tokyo is the only reliable counterweight.

From the late 1980s, corporate sponsorship of politicians and civic organizations becomes overt. By 2000, patronage networks can capture ministries or parties without legally replacing the state. Tokyo interprets this as a mature coordination system whose abuses require discipline, not as evidence that the system itself should be dismantled. Japanese cyberpunk accordingly criticizes the suffocation of the person inside a technically sound order.

### Mantetsu

The correct short name is **Mantetsu**, from the South Manchuria Railway Company. Victory prevents its liquidation. It is rechartered as the great railway and continental-development corporation of the northern Sphere.

Its network uses **1,435 mm standard gauge** across Korea, Manchukuo, north China, Mongolia, Vladivostok, and most of the Russian Far Eastern State. A controlled break of gauge marks the frontier with Irkutsk and the hostile Siberian middle. Mantetsu operates through subsidiaries and national concessions: the tracks may be locally flagged and legally situated inside a sovereign state, but Japanese finance, signaling, procurement, engineering standards, and timetable planning remain decisive.

Mantetsu corridors are not merely railways. They bring banks, power companies, trading houses, hotels, depots, hospitals, retail districts, police arrangements, and Japanese schools. They are the northern Sphere's administrative skeleton.

## The northern continental system

### The Russian Far Eastern State

Japan does not divide Kamchatka and Chukotka into separate sovereign republics. The **Russian Far Eastern State** extends from the Lake Baikal frontier to the Arctic and North Pacific, including Yakutia, Kolyma, Kamchatka, and Chukotka. These distant regions possess territorial and autonomous institutions because their populations, climate, and transport needs differ sharply, but they belong to one state.

Control is uneven. Chita, Khabarovsk, the southern railway, river corridors, ports, mines, and airfields are governable. Much of the northern interior is administered through aviation, river traffic, seasonal posts, concessions, local intermediaries, and maps more confident than the state itself.

#### Monarchy and capitals

A **Zemsky Sobor** invites Michael Andreevich Romanov to the throne in 1955–56. He rules publicly as **Tsar Mikhail**, giving the refugee state a legitimacy that is Russian, Orthodox, anti-Soviet, and distinct from both Manchukuo and a Japanese military government.

- **Chita** is the constitutional and imperial capital.
- **Khabarovsk** is the practical administrative and economic center.
- **Vladivostok** is deliberately excluded from the monarchy and remains a separate Japanese protectorate.

The state is a federal, Orthodox, semi-authoritarian monarchy. Russian ministries and provincial institutions administer ordinary law, education, resettlement, and internal politics. Japan controls defense alignment, strategic transport, concessions, and access to the Pacific. Tokyo prefers this arrangement to annexation by Manchukuo because a single Manchurian-Far Eastern continental complex would make the Kwantung establishment dangerously powerful.

#### Refugees and economy

The Far Eastern State becomes the largest and most populous Russian successor because refugees continue arriving from the German east, the Siberian wars, the Kazakh catastrophe, and failed interior regimes. It uses them to populate the southern corridors rather than attempting to settle the whole Arctic.

Rail is the economic trunk. Rivers and coastal shipping carry bulk freight. Aviation becomes ordinary long-distance human transport rather than a luxury. Mikhail personally patronizes the air network. Japanese engines and avionics, Manchukuoan components, and local assembly produce rugged transports and utility aircraft. Tracked snow and swamp vehicles dominate the last mile; ordinary wheeled vehicles dominate towns and prepared roads.

### Vladivostok/Urajio

The city and its immediate maritime district form a separate Japanese protectorate. Japanese administration uses **Urajio**, while Russian speakers, foreign maps, and international diplomacy continue to use **Vladivostok**. Neither name wholly displaces the other.

The protectorate is a mixed Russian, Japanese, Korean, and Manchurian port society. Japanese authority is clearest here because the harbor, naval facilities, customs system, and connection to Korea and Japan are too important to entrust to the Russian monarchy or Manchukuo. Selective naturalization and commercial residence produce a more civic legal culture than elsewhere in the northern Sphere.

### Manchukuo, the Far East, and the Amur settlement

Manchukuo retains a historical grievance along the Amur and receives transit, settlement, customs, and development privileges inside the Far Eastern system. It does not receive sovereignty over the Russian state. A serious **Amur Crisis** in the late 1950s or early 1960s brings the rivals close to war before Japan imposes an institutional settlement.

Thereafter rivalry moves into tariffs, railway priority, settlement rights, maps, statistics, language, contracts, court precedence, and prestige. The border is frozen; the argument is bureaucratized. Vladivostok remains the third party whose separate status prevents either continental client from controlling the whole Pacific outlet.

## The Siberian middle

The territory between the Urals and Lake Baikal is not a cooperative neutral belt. It is the long, violent afterlife of Soviet collapse. Military districts, evacuated industries, party remnants, refugee governments, railway administrations, and Russian national regimes fight through the late 1940s, 1950s, 1960s, and into the 1970s.

Most battles involve hundreds or a few thousand combatants rather than world-war armies. Cities, rail junctions, workshops, depots, and river crossings matter more than empty land.

### Principal regimes

- **Irkutsk** remains the most explicitly Soviet-restorationist power and the principal ideological threat to the Japanese Far East.
- **Omsk and Novosibirsk** form a dual state only after failing to conquer each other. Novosibirsk's weapons complex preserves Kalashnikov's design tradition, so the AK-47 remains associated with Novosibirsk rather than a unified Soviet Union.
- **Sverdlovsk, Chelyabinsk, Magnitogorsk, Kurgan, and Tobol** survive as industrial and military states around the Urals. They resist German absorption while remaining too divided to restore Russia.
- **Tannu Tuva** remains outside the Japanese-recognized Mongolian sphere and belongs to the unsettled interior system.

German Gebirgsjäger hold the Ural passes and the recognized German frontier follows the crest and watershed. Japan's recognized northern frontier begins at the western shore of Lake Baikal and runs to the Arctic. The states between those lines belong to neither great-power sphere.

### The Kazakh–Russian War

The working chronology for the **Kazakh–Russian War** is 1954–63. It is the bloodiest of the post-Soviet secession wars and the exception to the small-war pattern. Russian refugees and Kazakh authorities fight over the northern cities, railways, farms, and political ownership of the steppe. Massacre, expulsion, and retaliatory population movement produce a north–south partition. The Russian-held north becomes the **Russian Steppe State**. The war feeds refugees into the Russian Far East and makes the neutral Central Asian belt a frontier rather than a buffer of contented republics.

## The United Mongolian State

Japanese-supported Mengjiang defeats the Mongolian People's Republic and reunifies most Mongol lands under Demchugdongrub. The mature formal name is the **United Mongolian State**; *Mengjiang* survives as the familiar wartime foreign name.

The state is a Buddhist, aristocratic, pastoral frontier monarchy organized through banners, princely houses, a Great Khural, and a Chinggisid ruling house. Khüree is the political and sacred capital; Hohhot is the southern administrative and commercial center. Japan controls defense, foreign affairs, strategic finance, railways, and major concessions while allowing Mongol institutions to provide legitimacy.

The economy is not an attempted grain colony. It combines:

- organized mobile pastoralism supported by wells, veterinary services, weather reporting, winter reserves, depots, radios, slaughter facilities, and railway markets;
- coal, iron, copper, fluorspar, gold, tungsten, and later rare-earth development along concession corridors;
- airfields, listening posts, and strategic depth protecting Manchukuo and north China;
- a political demonstration that the Sphere contains a non-Chinese, non-Japanese nation with a restored historical order.

Demchugdongrub dies in 1966. The state survives him because the dynasty, banners, Khural, Japanese mission, and Mantetsu network have been institutionalized. By 1985 Mongolia is stable but secondary: neither an industrial peer of Manchukuo nor an empty map-colored possession.

## Manchukuo

Manchukuo becomes a permanent Manchurian industrial monarchy. It does not retain a practical program to restore the Qing over China. Its legitimacy rests upon the Manchu dynasty ruling a distinct multiethnic Manchurian state under Japanese protection.

Puyi is succeeded by Pujie in 1967 through house law, designation, and adoption arrangements sufficient to maintain dynastic continuity. The court preserves Manchu ceremonial identity. Northeastern Mandarin is the ordinary common language, and Japanese dominates advanced technical, military, and corporate spheres.

Manchukuoan citizenship is political and multiethnic. Manchus are the titular dynastic nation, but Han Chinese, Russians, Mongols, and other residents can be citizens. Japanese and Korean settlers remain Japanese nationals.

### Settlement and identity

Japanese agricultural settlement continues through the late 1960s. A 1967–69 moratorium ends new mass agricultural colonies while preserving existing titles, schools, chartered municipalities, and corporate rights. Settlers develop the formula **Japanese by nation, Manchurian by homeland**. The return mandate permits repatriation but does not empty the settler society.

Manchu cultural recovery proceeds through schools, court patronage, heritage institutions, and the return of Manchus from elsewhere. It does not require removing existing Han citizens.

### Industrial role

Manchukuo is the northern Sphere's heavy-industrial base: steel, chemicals, rail equipment, vehicles, engines, arms, mining machinery, and intermediate goods. Chartered corporate cities are central to the state. This creates a permanent contradiction: Manchukuo is more industrially powerful than most associated states but less sovereign over the commands governing that industry.

## National China

The Nanjing-based **National Government of the Republic of China** survives as a classical republic under Japanese guardianship. It claims civilizational continuity and unity without possessing a modern unitary state's power.

The center controls diplomacy, national standards, the examination system, supreme law, and the symbols of Chinese civilization. Provinces retain taxation, police, education, local administration, and substantial armed forces. The result is a confederal state whose weakness is presented as a return to ordered provincial government rather than national defeat.

The **Examination Yuan** combines classical and administrative examinations, practical civil-service tests, and technical or professional qualifications. Schools use traditional characters, Confucian ethics, and a Han-centered historical narrative. The Qing is treated as a foreign Manchu dynasty, reinforcing the ideological separation between China and Manchukuo.

Japan prevents China from acquiring an independent heavy-industrial and strategic system capable of challenging the Sphere. China may develop agriculture, mining, textiles, consumer goods, repair, local assembly, and technical education, but Japanese and Manchukuoan systems retain the military-industrial ceiling.

### Xinjiang and Tibet

Nanjing does **not** claim or administer Xinjiang or Tibet in the mature settlement. Both are sovereign members of the Japanese strategic outer wall.

- **Xinjiang** is Uyghur-dominated in language, religion, and local administration. Large Japanese treaty bases remain into the 1970s because the territory faces Soviet and Central Asian successor states.
- **Tibet** remains independent under its religious-aristocratic order. Japan maintains a smaller base and advisory mission and keeps the territory insulated from the Indian wars.

Japan allows Nanjing to imagine itself as the cultural center of the Chinese world while Tokyo bears the military, industrial, and frontier burden.

## Korea

Korea is an integral part of Japan, not a sovereign Sphere member. Koreans possess Japanese nationality and participate in Japanese schooling, conscription, employment, media, and public administration.

By 1985 southern and urban Korea is extremely Japanized. Japanese is the ordinary language of advancement and much public life. Korean remains a living language rather than a museum relic, especially in households, northern and rural districts, local religion, and the late preservation movement. The preservationists defend a regional culture within Japan; they are not the majority experience and do not restore Korean sovereignty.

Korea is also a major industrial appendage connecting the home islands to Manchukuo and China. Its ports, shipyards, machinery, chemicals, electronics, and railways make it more than an agricultural colony.

## The three Indochinese kingdoms

French Indochina never becomes one successor state. Japan recognizes three separate kingdoms and prevents Vietnamese demographic and administrative weight from turning them into a federation.

### Vietnam

Vietnam is a Nguyễn monarchy with political centers at Huế, Hanoi, and Saigon. It is the region's demographic, commercial, and cultural heavyweight. Ordinary literacy and administration use **quốc ngữ**. A standardized Hán-Nôm curriculum returns as heritage, ceremonial culture, and basic learned literacy rather than replacing quốc ngữ.

Vietnam develops the first modest local software and hobbyist-computing scene in Indochina because it has the largest educated market and industrial base. Japanese architecture and standards still structure the system.

### Cambodia

Cambodia is Sihanouk's monarchy and retains a permanent grievance because Thailand holds Battambang and other western Cambodian territories recovered during the war. Khmer script, court culture, and Buddhist institutions are preserved and digitized. Cambodia's later computing contribution is strongest in cultural preservation and publishing.

### Laos

Laos is a weak dual-capital monarchy centered on Luang Prabang and Vientiane. Lao script and the monarchy survive, but the country depends heavily on Japanese and regional systems for transport, finance, and technical localization.

### Council of Three Indochinese States

Vietnam, Cambodia, and Laos cooperate through a **Council of Three Indochinese States** handling river management, transport, health, customs, standards, and common petitions to Japan. It is deliberately not an Indochinese federation.

French clergy possess no special political role. Catholic worship retains ecclesiastical Latin while pastoral and catechetical life uses national vernaculars.

## The East Indian successor states

The former Dutch East Indies do not unify as Indonesia. Japan recognizes a set of viable regional states large enough to govern but particular enough to prevent one archipelagic rival.

The working list is:

1. Sunda
2. Mataram
3. East Java and Madura
4. Aceh
5. Tapanuli
6. Minangkabau
7. Sumatran Malay Federation
8. Kalimantan Federation
9. South Sulawesi
10. North Sulawesi
11. Bali
12. Lombok–Sumbawa
13. Eastern Lesser Sunda Federation
14. Moluccan Federation
15. Bornean Malaya, which later fails and therefore does not survive as a mature member

Smaller peoples receive autonomy inside federations rather than a separate microscopic state for every island or ethnicity.

Japanese corporations seize or inherit European assets before the local states are fully constituted. The new states are born legally sovereign after mines, plantations, refineries, shipping, banks, and port infrastructure have already entered Japanese ownership. The archipelago is therefore the earliest and clearest laboratory of corporate primacy.

## Bornean Malaya and protected Malaya

Bornean Malaya fails rather than maturing peacefully.

- **1973–74:** a charter crisis exposes the incompatibility of peninsular Malay institutions, Bornean interests, corporate concessions, and Thai security claims.
- **1974–76:** the Malay–Bornean War breaks the combined state.
- **1976–77:** Japan treats the fighting as a strategic crisis. Zaibatsu security forces may defend assets and transport corridors, creating a precedent Tokyo later restricts.
- **1977–79:** a triangular settlement creates an independent Bornean state, preserves Brunei's special position, and restores the **Federated Kingdom of Malaya** on the former peninsula under Thai protection.

Thailand retains its prewar southern provinces. Singapore remains outside protected Malaya as a separate commercial city-state. Malaya gains additional practical autonomy later because Thailand's Burmese burden consumes attention and resources.

## Thailand

The official name is the **Kingdom of Thailand**. *Siam* remains a historic, royal, and pluralist term. The state is functionally an empire: a Thai-headed crown rules Thai, Lao, Khmer, Malay, and other populations.

Thailand is the Sphere's most autonomous principal associate. It retains its army, baht, banks, monarchy, domestic corporate houses, and territorial empire.

Its political sequence is:

- **1941–57:** Phibun's victory state.
- **1957–63:** the Sarit–Bhumibol royal-military settlement.
- **1963–73:** Thanom's high imperial order.
- **After the early-1970s crisis:** a Prem-like royal-technocratic settlement.

The Malay–Bornean War changes Thai doctrine. Bangkok abandons direct annexation of the whole peninsula in favor of a protected Malayan kingdom. In Burma, Thailand later accepts an even heavier protectorate burden.

Japanese firms enter Thailand through licenses, joint ventures, banks, and alliances with Thai royal and military companies. By 2000 they exert enormous influence, but Thai banks and state-connected firms prevent the country from becoming a pure corporate dependency.

## Burma

Japan does not retain wartime Burma continuously. British forces recover Burma late in the war, and the Lisbon settlement initially leaves it under British protection. Burma becomes a British rear area and a route for evacuating Bengal during the collapse of the Raj.

### Wars and settlements

- **1948–53 — Burmese Companion War:** the strains of the Indian collapse, ethnic armies, refugee flows, and British overextension fracture the protectorate.
- **1952–53 — Rangoon Settlement:** British protection ends. One recognized Union survives under asymmetric federal arrangements and ceasefires; Japan gains predominance through negotiation and selective support rather than a simple conquest.
- **1954–56 — Federal Crisis:** the new Union nearly fails over distribution of power, armies, and revenue.
- **1960s — Second Burmese War:** renewed fighting leads to a temporary **Sovereign States Experiment** in which Bamar, Shan, Kachin, Chin, Karen, Karenni, Mon, and Arakan polities operate separately or under a skeletal common authority.
- **War of the Burmese States:** exhaustion forces them back into a loose common framework.
- **Mid-1990s — final protectorate crisis:** Japan authorizes a Thai protectorate rather than paying indefinitely for direct stabilization.

Under the mature settlement Burma remains formally sovereign and its constituent nationalities retain their institutions. Thailand controls common security, heavy weapons, foreign alignment, strategic transport, and final arbitration. Japan is the ultimate guarantor.

Burma consumes resources originally intended for Azad Hind. Azad Hind consequently treats Burma as the reason it never won India and refuses any Sphere obligation to defend it.

## Azad Hind and the Indian successor system

### Azad Hind

The Provisional Government of Free India, Indian National Army, Indian Independence League, Azad Hind Dal, Azad Hind Bank, and Rani of Jhansi Regiment already constitute a government when they enter India. Calcutta becomes the permanent administrative capital; Delhi remains the promised lawful capital. The state deliberately retains the title **Provisional Government** because the liberation of all India is unfinished.

Azad Hind is pan-Indian, secular in national law, culturally federal, militarized, centralized, and economically planned. It remains Japan's eastern blocker rather than the expected future government of a unified India.

Its early armored forces use Japanese Chi-Nu and Chi-To tanks, with limited Chi-Ri vehicles. Later Japanese systems enter selectively. Exact designations remain controlled by the Land Armaments Reference.

### Rival claimants

- **Congress India** is Nehru's secular state in the middle Ganges system.
- **Hindustan** is a Delhi-centered Hindu-majoritarian claimant.
- **Telangana Praja Samakhya (TPS)** is the communist successor to the Telangana Raithanga Sayudha Poratam.
- **Hyderabad** is the largest princely state but possesses no suzerainty over all princes.
- **Rajputana** operates through a concert of princes.
- Khalistan, Kashmir, Sindh, Kalat, Pakistan successors, and other western states retain separate trajectories.

No princely federation unifies the subcontinent. Dispersed sovereignty is the balance-of-power mechanism preventing any all-India claimant from winning.

### Main war sequence

- **1956:** Burma's breakdown helps stop Azad Hind's last plausible final offensive.
- **Colombo Armistice:** broad war is frozen but not settled; India remains permanently fortified.
- **1960–63 — Preparatory War:** mobilization, border fighting, and alliance-building precede a larger conflict.
- **1963–65 — Northern Indian War:** Azad Hind's limited Type 16 Shu-Ha force confronts Congress India's Model 62 Vijaya system. The war is inconclusive.
- **1970 — Bhola cyclone:** disaster strikes eastern Bengali territories. Congress attacks toward Bihar and Patna; Japanese intervention stops a decisive breakthrough, but the final line moves slightly in Congress's favor.
- **1991 — Chittagong cyclone:** Azad Hind withholds relief formations because leaders fear a repetition of 1970. Congress does not launch the expected invasion, and the decision magnifies civilian catastrophe.

Patna changes hands repeatedly and may be divided or neutralized as a conference city.

### Soviet technical diaspora

The fall of the USSR scatters design bureaus into successor states:

- Congress India receives the Kartsev/Nizhny Tagil medium-tank tradition, Chelyabinsk and Kurgan mechanized design, and Ilyushin aviation. The Vijaya is a T-54/55 lineage made politically Indian.
- Hindustan receives Morozov/Kharkiv advanced armor, Mytishchi air defense, and MiG aviation.
- Hyderabad receives Kirov/VNII-100 heavy armor, Uralmash self-propelled artillery, and Yakovlev aviation. Limited IS-7, T-10M, Object 277, and Object 120 derivatives or prototypes fit this ecosystem.
- Rajputana uses a Chelyabinsk heavy lineage analogous to Object 770.
- Khalistan and Kashmir draw upon Astrov light-vehicle expertise.
- Pakistani successor states develop GAZ/Arzamas-derived wheeled vehicles.
- Tupolev and major naval design institutions migrate principally to Iran; Karachi hosts Rubin, Severnoye, and Nevskoye subsidiaries.

Object 279 remains a prototype. If its concept influences Indian warfare, the design problem is artillery and chemical contamination rather than atomic battlefields. Chemical warfare in the 1960s drives overpressure, sealing, cooling, decontamination, and enclosed infantry vehicles.

## Western Indian frontier

### 1947–69

The early west contains an Iranian-backed Baloch Confederated State with Gwadar, a separate Kalat, changing Afghan control around Chagai and Quetta–Pishin, an emergency Pakistan, independent Sindh, and other border regimes. Mounted infantry and cavalry remain relevant through the 1950s and 1960s.

By the end of the 1960s:

- the Baloch confederation holds Makran, Kharan, Chagai, and Gwadar but loses Las Bela to Kalat;
- Pakistan consolidates Quetta–Pishin, Sibi, Loralai, and Zhob;
- Afghanistan abandons most direct claims around Quetta and Chagai but dominates Peshawar and Waziristan;
- Sindh remains independent;
- Khalistan holds Lahore and western Punjab.

### 1970–90

Consolidation does not produce peace. The **Third Balochistan War** of 1981–84 destroys the Baloch Confederated State. Kalat absorbs it, while Iran withdraws strategically in return for guarantees and access.

The Tehran Concord gives Japan an opening rather than instant rule. Japan maintains Azad Hind as the eastern blocker and chooses Hyderabad as its princely gateway. Japanese investment and security guarantees begin after 1983; Hyderabad formally joins Japanese association in **1986**, while remaining sovereign and separate from Azad Hind.

From 1986 to 1990, Japanese detachments, Hyderabad and princely forces, and zaibatsu security organizations conduct anti-TPS and princely stabilization. Chartered restoration territories protect corridors and useful assets. TPS survives smaller and harder. Britain remains in southern India.

Japan's goal is not to make Azad Hind conquer India. Azad Hind blocks the east, Hyderabad organizes cooperating princes, corporations secure useful corridors, and Japan prevents any hostile all-India consolidation.

## Monetary architecture

The Sphere uses layers rather than one currency.

### Domestic and clearing money

Japan's ordinary domestic yen is fiat. The **Greater East Asia Clearing Yen** is a noncash bloc settlement unit redeemable through approved Japanese machinery, ships, training, arms, and development claims. It is not freely convertible into gold.

Associated states retain national currencies whose exchange, credit, and reserve rules differ by treaty. Thailand's baht is unusually autonomous.

### Gold settlement currencies

Indian fragmentation makes trusted settlement money essential. Japanese firms create the **Indian Gold Settlement Yen**, ordinarily called the **Gold Yen**. It represents a fixed fine-gold claim redeemable through vaults, certified claims, or Japanese goods. It is not freely interchangeable with the domestic yen.

Japan later broadens the Gold Yen as an external currency. The working parity is **100 Gold Yen per fine troy ounce**.

The wider international working scale is:

- United States dollar: legally maintained on gold; **$35 per fine troy ounce**.
- Latin scudo: common circulating gold-backed bloc money; **50 scudi per ounce**.
- Restored gold sterling in the 1980s: **£25 per ounce**.
- Gold Yen: **100 per ounce**.
- German external Goldmark in the 1990s: **100 per ounce**, while the domestic imperial currency remains fiat.

Thus the working cross-rates are £1 = $1.40 = 2 scudi = 4 Gold Yen. These precise parities remain working canon until the monetary reference is formally settled.

The conceptual rule is more important than the arithmetic: America, Britain, and the Latin bloc place ordinary circulating money on gold; Japan and Germany place outsider or settlement instruments on gold while keeping domestic command currencies fiat.

## Pacific and Oceania

### Associated Papua New Guinea

The whole island of New Guinea becomes a unified associated **Papua New Guinea** rather than remaining split among colonial successors. It is federal or highly regionalized. Japan controls defense, bases, and major resource concessions while local institutions manage ordinary government.

### Direct Japanese possessions

Japan directly holds the Bismarcks, Bougainville, the Solomons, Santa Cruz, Fiji, New Hebrides, New Caledonia, Wallis and Futuna, the Gilbert and Ellice groups, Nauru, Tahiti, and French Polynesia.

The older Micronesian possessions are the most Japanized and integrated. Later South Pacific acquisitions move toward Japanese nationality during the 1970s and 1980s while retaining regional cultures and administrations. Japan applies the doctrine of viable statehood selectively: some large territories receive association, while strategically scattered islands remain imperial.

The main surviving external enclaves are:

- Western Samoa under New Zealand;
- American Samoa under the United States;
- Tonga as a British-aligned independent kingdom;
- Guam and the American Phoenix and Line island positions;
- Portuguese Timor;
- British Christmas and Cocos islands.

## Taiwan

The official Japanese name is **Taiwan** (臺灣/台湾), pronounced *Taiwan*. **Formosa** remains the English exonym. The capital is **Taihoku**.

Taiwan is integral Japan and has no separate citizenship. Between 1947 and the late 1960s, mass Japanese and Ryukyuan settlement adds roughly four to five million net settlers. By about 1970 the Japanese population approaches half the island and becomes a plurality in important districts. Japanese dominates public administration, schooling, mass media, and advancement.

Hokkien, Hakka, and Indigenous languages decline sharply. The preservation movement rises in the 1970s, exercises major influence or government power from roughly 1977 to 1985, and declines politically afterward while leaving durable schools, archives, media, and heritage institutions. These languages become endangered, revived, or heritage languages and mixed ethnolects; they do not automatically form a single pidgin or creole.

### Industrial role

Taiwan is an important Japanese-owned chemical, refining, component, packaging, and selected-fabrication center. It is not the principal heart of the Japanese semiconductor system.

- Tokyo–Yokohama controls design, research, and corporate command.
- Kansai leads equipment, materials, and specialist electronics.
- Kyushu carries much high-volume fabrication.
- Taiwan supplies selected Japanese-owned fabrication, packaging, chemicals, and components.

## Technology and culture across the Sphere

Japanese technical systems are localized rather than replaced by a single Japanese consumer culture. Traditional Chinese characters remain standard; Khmer, Lao, Hán-Nôm, Tibetan, Uyghur scripts, and other national writing systems are digitized under Japanese-sponsored character standards. Korea remains linguistically alive but deeply Japanized. Taiwan's local languages often become heritage or revival languages.

Corporate and factory computing spreads from Japan and Manchukuo through standardized procurement. Local software develops first where education, market size, and national institutions support it—especially Vietnam—and later in publishing, cultural preservation, administration, and business applications elsewhere.

Japan's own semiconductor geography remains home-island centered. The Sphere supplies materials, assembly, selected fabrication, markets, and labor, but Tokyo does not willingly reproduce the entire strategic technological command system in one associated state.
