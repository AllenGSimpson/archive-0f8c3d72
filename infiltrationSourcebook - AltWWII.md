# *Infiltration* Sourcebook and AltWWII Reconstruction

Status: source reconstruction v1; complete for the requested scope  
Prepared: 24 July 2026  
Companion to: `infiltrationReferenceCorpus - AltWWII.md`  
Scope: source material and an original reconstruction of the narrative arc used
by Taylor R. Marshall, principally from the nineteenth century through the
Second Vatican Council, with a short Sankt Gallen–Benedict XVI–Francis
epilogue

## Editorial rule

This is not a substitute text of Taylor R. Marshall's *Infiltration* and does
not attempt to reproduce his prose. It reconstructs the relevant argument in
new language from the documents and testimony on which that argument depends.

The distinction matters because Marshall's narrative regularly places four
different kinds of material in one chronological sequence:

1. public documents whose existence and wording can be checked;
2. later recollections about earlier events;
3. disputed allegations for which the underlying exhibit is unavailable; and
4. Marshall's own inference that separate events belong to one coordinated
   operation.

This sourcebook keeps those layers separate. It then adds a fifth layer,
**AltWWII authorial truth**, wherever the setting deliberately resolves a
historical uncertainty in favor of the more dramatic version.

Evidence labels follow the companion dossier:

- **P — Primary:** official document, contemporary record, sworn testimony, or
  participant account.
- **S — Secondary:** later historical or biographical treatment with an
  identifiable source base.
- **A — Anecdotal:** oral recollection, hearsay, or a story transmitted without
  a surviving contemporary exhibit.
- **C — Contested:** authenticity or interpretation is materially disputed.
- **M — Marshall bridge:** an inferential connection characteristic of
  Marshall's synthesis.
- **AT — Authorial truth:** a proposition established as true in AltWWII even
  where the real-world evidence does not establish it.

The resulting setting narrative is not that one immortal committee controlled
every event. It is that several hostile programs, opportunists, theological
schools, and institutional factions learned similar methods and sometimes
cooperated. That gives the setting an intelligible covert history without
canonizing the excluded claim of one continuous Alta Vendita organization.

## Part I — The recoverable narrative

## 1. The Alta Vendita and the strategy of cultural capture, 1815–1846

### Documentary core

The Carbonari and related secret societies were real participants in the
politics of Restoration Italy. Their activities combined nationalism,
constitutionalism, anticlericalism, and resistance to the political order
restored after Napoleon. Papal governments treated them as revolutionary
enemies.

The document usually called the *Permanent Instruction of the Alta Vendita*
entered Catholic polemical literature through Jacques Crétineau-Joly's
*L'Église romaine en face de la Révolution* (1859). Monsignor George F. Dillon
translated and popularized the same documentary tradition in *The War of
Antichrist with the Church and Christian Civilization* (1885). Dillon's book
is public domain and is available in [page scan and OCR
form](https://archive.org/details/warantichristwi00dillgoog).

The program attributed to the Alta Vendita is more patient than a coup. Its
central ideas are:

- direct attacks on the papacy are less useful than changing the social world
  from which future clergy and popes will emerge;
- youth, schools, universities, seminaries, reputations, and patronage are the
  decisive fields;
- an operative need not make a pope into a formal member of a secret society;
  a generation can instead be formed so that a future pope advances compatible
  ideas sincerely;
- respectable Catholic identities are valuable cover;
- the desired result is a transformation in ecclesiastical dress—Dillon's
  famous “revolution in tiara and cope.”

Dillon's English text has the instruction tell its readers to seek “a Pope
according to our wants,” to go “to the youth,” and to spread nets in
“sacristies, seminaries and convents.” These are short, representative phrases,
not evidence that every later resemblance was directed by the original body.

### What the source establishes

The source establishes the existence, in nineteenth-century Catholic
anti-secret-society literature, of a program for indirect, generational capture
of ecclesiastical culture. It is therefore a legitimate ideological ancestor
for later setting conspirators.

It does **not** by itself establish:

- that the same Alta Vendita organization survived intact into the twentieth
  century;
- that later Modernists, Communists, liturgists, or Sankt Gallen participants
  received orders from it;
- that an ideological resemblance proves an operational chain of command.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall uses the Instruction as the book's master template. The later story is
read as a sequence in which the adversary stops trying to destroy the Church
from outside and instead cultivates clergy who will alter her institutions from
within. Similarities of method—formation of youth, quiet promotion, use of
respectability, and capture of appointments—are treated as signs that the
nineteenth-century design reached maturity in the twentieth.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** The Instruction is known to several later anticlerical and revolutionary
networks and becomes a frequently copied strategic text. It inspires emulation;
it does not create one continuous organization. Setting investigators may
correctly identify a “Vendita method” while being wrong if they assume a single
Vendita command structure.

This distinction allows characters to find genuine patterns without making
every apparent pattern proof of the largest conspiracy.

### Key sources

- Jacques Crétineau-Joly, *L'Église romaine en face de la Révolution*, 2 vols.
  (Paris, 1859), especially the documents attributed to the Alta Vendita.
- George F. Dillon, [*The War of Antichrist with the Church and Christian
  Civilization*](https://archive.org/details/warantichristwi00dillgoog) (1885),
  especially chapters XV–XVIII.
- Pius IX, [*Quanta cura* and the *Syllabus of
  Errors*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-ix/en/documents/encyclica-quanta-cura-8-decembris-1864.html)
  (1864).
- Leo XIII, [*Humanum
  genus*](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_18840420_humanum-genus.html)
  (1884).

## 2. La Salette and the warning that Rome may be afflicted from within, 1846–1879

### Documentary layers

Mélanie Calvat and Maximin Giraud reported an apparition at La Salette in 1846.
The diocesan judgment recognizing the apparition and devotion did not make
every later text attributed to either seer equally authoritative.

Three textual stages must normally be distinguished:

1. the public message associated with the recognized apparition;
2. sealed statements written by Mélanie and Maximin in 1851 and transmitted to
   Pius IX; and
3. Mélanie's much longer 1879 publication,
   *L'Apparition de la Très Sainte Vierge sur la montagne de La Salette*.

The 1851 manuscripts were rediscovered in Roman archival holdings in 1999 and
published in modern scholarship. The 1879 text is accessible in [French
transcription](https://www.melaniecalvat.org/notre-dame-de-la-salette/recit-complet-de-lapparition/)
and [English
translation](https://melanie-de-la-salette.fr/en/apparition/). It bears the
imprimatur of Bishop Salvatore Luigi Zola of Lecce. An imprimatur is permission
to publish a text free of identified doctrinal or moral error; it is not a
guarantee that every prediction is supernatural or that the 1879 wording is
identical to the sealed 1851 manuscript.

Roman disciplinary history is still less favorable to treating the long text
as approved prophecy. In 1880 the Holy Office expressed displeasure at its
publication and sought withdrawal of circulating copies. On 21 December 1915
the Holy Office formally prohibited publication or discussion of the various
forms and contemporary applications of the “Secret of La Salette,” while
explicitly preserving devotion to Our Lady under the title Reconciler of La
Salette. The Latin decree appears at p. 594 of the official [*Acta Apostolicae
Sedis*, vol. 7](https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-07-1915-ocr.pdf).
This disciplinary record makes the AltWWII treatment a genuine setting
divergence, not merely a favorable interpretation of an imprimatur.

The long text supplies the phrases that matter to the infiltration narrative:
loss of faith at Rome, clerical corruption, religious houses becoming sites of
decay, chastisement, and a crisis extending through hierarchy and society.

### Real-world evidentiary conclusion

Historically, the 1851 and 1879 texts are not the same document. The longer
publication contains material absent from the surviving 1851 manuscript. It
can be studied as Mélanie's later testimony and interpretation, but its wording
cannot simply be backdated.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

In the infiltration narrative, La Salette converts the political program of the
Alta Vendita into a prophetic one. The conspirators hope to alter Rome; the
apparition warns that Rome itself may lose the faith. The force of the chapter
comes from treating the 1879 language as a supernatural forecast of internal
clerical collapse.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** Mélanie's full sealed 1851 submission contains the same revelatory text
and substantially the same wording printed in 1879. The shorter 1851
manuscript recovered by researchers is an accompanying abstract, not the only
packet sent to Rome. A Vatican copy of the complete packet exists before 1879,
although access to it is tightly restricted. Thus the 1879 publication does
not enlarge the prophecy in setting history; it makes the earlier long text
public.

Characters inside the setting may still dispute interpretation:

- “Rome will lose the faith” can mean the city's population, a faction in the
  Curia, a period of institutional eclipse, or a defect by particular
  officeholders.
- It does not automatically mean that the indefectibility of the Church has
  failed.
- A prophecy can be genuine without every partisan timetable derived from it
  being correct.

### Key sources

- Mélanie Calvat, [1879 French
  text](https://www.melaniecalvat.org/notre-dame-de-la-salette/recit-complet-de-lapparition/).
- Mélanie Calvat, [English
  translation](https://melanie-de-la-salette.fr/en/apparition/).
- René Laurentin and Michel Corteville, *Découverte du secret de La Salette*
  (Fayard, 2002), for the recovered 1851 manuscripts.
- Leo XIII, [*Quod
  multum*](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_22081886_quod-multum.html)
  (1886), a useful comparison for Leo's treatment of revolution and
  Freemasonry.

## 3. The fall of the Papal States and Leo XIII's spiritual diagnosis, 1870–1903

### Historical sequence

Italian forces entered Rome in September 1870. Pius IX rejected the unilateral
annexation and regarded himself as a prisoner in the Vatican. This was a
political and territorial defeat for papal sovereignty, not a demonstrated
capture of the papal office.

Leo XIII's public encyclicals give a documented intellectual response to the
world that followed. In *Humanum genus* he identifies Freemasonry with a
naturalist program that excludes supernatural revelation from public order,
education, family law, and political life. The text is valuable because it
allows the setting to portray the conflict without relying only on secret
documents. Leo himself publicly believed that organized philosophical and
political forces were working to dechristianize society.

### The St. Michael account: what can be dated

The familiar story about Leo hearing a dialogue between God and Satan is not
documented in a contemporary statement by Leo. The evidence arrives in layers:

- prayers after Low Mass began under Pius IX in 1859 for the Papal States;
- Leo altered and extended the prayers in 1884 and added the short St. Michael
  prayer in 1886;
- Cardinal Giovanni Battista Nasalli Rocca's 1946 Lenten pastoral letter says
  that Leo's secretary, Rinaldo Angeli, repeatedly told him Leo had seen
  infernal spirits gathering over Rome;
- Domenico Pechenino wrote in *La Settimana del Clero* on 30 March 1947 that he
  had seen Leo become transfixed after Mass and then compose a text;
- versions containing a detailed divine–satanic dialogue and a defined period
  of extraordinary power appear in later devotional transmission, including
  the German-language tradition associated with Maximilian Vogl.

The late Nasalli Rocca and Pechenino accounts support a remembered vision and a
connection with Rome and the prayer. They do not independently supply every
sentence of the popular dialogue.

Kevin J. Symonds's *Pope Leo XIII and the Prayer to St. Michael* (2018) is the
most useful dedicated source criticism. The relevant earlier testimonies and
their dates can also be compared in this [summary of the Italian
materials](https://www.scrutatio.it/archivio/articolo/inferno/6742/esorcismi-rituale-romanum).

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

The narrative places the vision immediately after the political loss of Rome.
Satan is understood to request a period in which he may test the Church, and
Leo answers by ordering the St. Michael prayer. The twentieth-century crisis is
then read as the allotted trial foretold in the encounter.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** Leo experiences both the vision of spirits over Rome and the literal
dialogue preserved in the standard Vogl-form devotional account: the adversary
claims he can destroy the Church if granted greater power and time, and the
request is permitted as a trial. Leo records the experience privately. The
record is not promulgated as revelation and is dispersed among trusted
secretarial papers; this explains why the public evidence remains late and
fragmentary.

For setting consistency, do not turn the dialogue into a mechanical expiration
date. The request gives the adversary an opportunity, not a guarantee of
victory, and the response of human actors remains free.

### Key sources

- Leo XIII, [*Humanum
  genus*](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_18840420_humanum-genus.html),
  especially §§10–22 and 37–38.
- Giovanni Battista Nasalli Rocca, Lenten pastoral letter (Bologna, 1946).
- Domenico Pechenino, “La preghiera a San Michele,” *La Settimana del Clero*,
  30 March 1947.
- Kevin J. Symonds, *Pope Leo XIII and the Prayer to St. Michael* (Preserving
  Christian Publications, 2018).

## 4. The Modernist crisis as an internal conflict, 1907–1914

### Primary texts

Pius X's campaign against Modernism supplies the strongest official evidence
for a perceived danger operating within Catholic institutions.

[*Lamentabili sane
exitu*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-x/it/decrees/documents/hf_p-x_decree_19070703_lamentabili-sane-exitu.html)
(1907) condemns propositions involving biblical criticism, dogmatic
development, the historical Jesus, sacramental origins, and ecclesial
authority.

[*Pascendi dominici
gregis*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-x/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-x_enc_19070908_pascendi-dominici-gregis.html)
(1907) is still more important to the narrative. It locates the danger among
Catholic laymen and clergy, accuses Modernists of combining multiple roles,
warns that they seek positions of influence, and describes indirect tactics
such as controlling reputation, publication, teaching, and silence.

[*Sacrorum
antistitum*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-x/la/motu_proprio/documents/hf_p-x_motu-proprio_19100901_sacrorum-antistitum.html)
(1910) imposes the Anti-Modernist Oath and refers to organized or clandestine
cooperation. The oath rejects the proposition that dogma may change from one
meaning into another as consciousness develops.

### What can responsibly be inferred

These documents prove that Pius X and the Holy Office perceived an internal,
networked intellectual crisis and used disciplinary mechanisms against it.
They do not prove that every censured theologian was a Freemason, Communist, or
conscious agent of a common conspiracy.

Modernism can function in the setting at three levels:

1. a family of theological positions;
2. informal professional networks defending adherents and placing students;
3. a smaller covert faction that knowingly treats ambiguity and gradualism as
   instruments of capture.

The third group may exploit the first two without every member of the broader
movement knowing it exists.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall treats Pius X as discovering the ecclesiastical form of the Alta
Vendita method. The program is no longer merely outside the Church: it has
teachers, clergy, journals, and patrons. The suppression drives it underground
rather than eliminating it. When vigilance weakens, its pupils return as the
experts and administrators of a later age.

### AltWWII use

The existence of a self-protecting Modernist network is setting fact. Its
relationship to revolutionary politics is mixed:

- some are philosophical Modernists with no secret-society tie;
- some believe they are rescuing Catholic thought from intellectual
  irrelevance;
- some accept outside money or political help;
- a few consciously transmit the Vendita method.

This creates conflicts inside the reform camp and avoids a world in which every
theological disagreement is merely a cover identity.

## 5. Fatima, Russia, and the problem of the withheld text, 1917–1960

### The checkable documentary sequence

The children at Fátima reported apparitions in 1917. Lúcia's principal memoir
texts were written much later, under ecclesiastical instruction. The first two
parts of the “secret” used in the Holy See's publication come principally from
her Third Memoir of 31 August 1941, supplemented by the Fourth Memoir of 8
December 1941.

Those parts join a vision of hell to requests for devotion to the Immaculate
Heart, the consecration of Russia, and reparation. They warn that Russia will
spread its errors if the requests are not heeded. For a setting whose alternate
war and political order depart from real history, this is the most consequential
Fatima material.

Lúcia wrote the third part in January 1944. It was transmitted in a sealed
envelope and eventually brought to Rome in 1957. The instruction on the outer
envelope stated that it could be opened after 1960 by the Patriarch of Lisbon
or the Bishop of Leiria. The Holy See did not publish it in 1960.

In 2000 the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith published photographs,
a translation, a report of an interview with Lúcia, and a theological
commentary by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger. The official [*Message of
Fátima*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000626_message-fatima_en.html)
is the indispensable primary dossier. The published third part is a symbolic
vision: an angel calls for penance; a “bishop dressed in white,” clergy,
religious, and lay people pass through a ruined city and are killed; angels
gather the martyrs' blood.

### Why a “second text” theory arose

The theory of an omitted explanatory document draws on several observations:

- the phrase “In Portugal, the dogma of the faith will always be preserved,
  etc.” appears at the end of the Fourth Memoir's second part and sounds like a
  transition to additional words;
- earlier readers and commentators described the secret in ways that some
  interpreters find difficult to reconcile with a purely visual text;
- reports differ about sheets, envelopes, and the location in which the text
  was stored;
- Lúcia distinguished what she saw from the meaning she was given to
  understand.

Frère Michel de la Sainte Trinité collected the most extensive pre-2000
chronology in volume III of *The Whole Truth About Fatima*. The publisher has
made that [volume available for
download](https://loretopubs.org/fatima/WTAF-3.pdf). It is a partisan
interpretation, not an official archival inventory, but its documentation is
useful when checked against the Holy See dossier.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

The infiltration narrative reads the missing “etc.” and the descriptions by
earlier readers as evidence that a textual explanation concerning apostasy was
separated from the published vision. The 1960 non-publication becomes a
turning point: the hierarchy declines a prophetic warning just before the
Council.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** Lúcia produces two related third-part documents:

1. the four-page vision later released publicly; and
2. a shorter record of words explaining that the ruined city and martyred
   hierarchy follow a crisis of faith and authority within the Church.

The second document is withheld. Its existence is known to a very small chain
of custodians, not to the Council fathers generally.

The setting should not make the withheld text an all-purpose answer. Its
language is prophetic, compact, and capable of misapplication. It warns of an
internal crisis; it does not name every culprit, validate every apparition
industry claim, or provide a date-by-date schedule.

### Source cautions

- The third-secret envelope reached Rome in 1957, not 1955.
- “In Portugal…” is the end of the second part in Lúcia's Fourth Memoir, not
  the first line of the Vatican's published third-part manuscript.
- A private revelation does not add to the deposit of faith. Cardinal
  Ratzinger's commentary explicitly makes this theological distinction.

## 6. Communist method and Catholic institutions, 1920s–1950s

### Official Catholic diagnosis

Pius XI's [*Divini
Redemptoris*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xi_enc_19370319_divini-redemptoris.html)
(1937) describes Communist propaganda as centrally directed, adaptive, heavily
resourced, and active through publications, entertainment, schools, and
universities (§17). Section 57 warns Catholic organizations against accepting
Communist cooperation under humanitarian or charitable appearances. This
document is better evidence for contemporary Catholic fear of infiltration
than later unsourced numerical claims.

### Bella Dodd's sworn testimony

The complete government transcript of Dodd's 17 June 1953 testimony is
available in the House Committee on Un-American Activities,
[*Investigation of Communist Activities in the Columbus, Ohio,
Area*](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-Y1_2-ea93c690868b5f44c8726270f6af4cb2/pdf/GOVPUB-Y1_2-ea93c690868b5f44c8726270f6af4cb2.pdf),
pp. 1741–1777.

The transcript establishes under oath that Dodd:

- worked with the Communist Party from 1932, formally joined in 1943, served
  on the New York secretariat and national committee, and was expelled in
  1949;
- testified that important professionals could be concealed, issued no card,
  or recorded under pseudonyms;
- described small disciplined fractions entering much larger non-Communist
  organizations and arriving with decisions made in advance;
- stated that members were expected to carry the party line into professional,
  educational, and civic associations;
- emphasized teachers and youth as strategic fields;
- described the removal of religion and ethics from education as part of
  “despiritualizing” American culture.

The transcript does **not** contain testimony that Dodd placed 1,100 men in
Catholic seminaries, controlled four future cardinals, or named Communist
cardinals in the Vatican.

### The four-cardinal story and seminary claim

The key surviving source is Alice von Hildebrand's recollection. In a 2001
interview, she said Dodd told Dietrich and Alice von Hildebrand that she had
dealt with four cardinals working for Communism in the Vatican. Later
statements added an Orange County lecture and a claim that Archbishop Fulton
Sheen had told Dodd not to reveal the names.

Kevin Symonds has assembled the transmission history, including the von
Hildebrand interview, a 2002 affidavit by Johnine and Paul Leininger about a
Dodd lecture, and later retellings, in [“Communist
Infiltration?”](https://kevinsymonds.com/2021/05/26/dodd-infiltration-massofages/).
His conclusion is appropriately narrow: the recollection cannot presently be
verified from Dodd's papers or a contemporary recording, but it should not be
confused with the content of her congressional testimony.

A separate 1961 Dodd lecture, “Communism in Education,” survives in the
[Arthur W. Kramer Sound Recording
Collection](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/b7650657-7a45-403e-aae3-18b7fd166711).
It is not the alleged 1953 Fordham recording and does not cure that evidentiary
gap.

### Other congressional witnesses

Manning Johnson and other former Communists testified about the use of clergy,
religious fronts, and indirect rather than frontal attacks. Their testimony
concerned religion broadly and should not be silently rewritten as an
exclusively Catholic program.

The public-domain OCR for the 1953 New York-area hearings is
[searchable here](https://archive.org/download/investigationofc0506unit/investigationofc0506unit_djvu.txt).
Testimony by Nicholas Dozenberg, Louis Budenz, Nicholas Kornfeder, Benjamin
Gitlow, and others describes:

- use of religious language and social causes as a tactical bridge;
- attempts to influence religious organizations from within;
- the “Living Church” tactic in Soviet Russia;
- disciplined minorities working through larger organizations whose ordinary
  members were not Communists.

These witnesses establish a documented repertoire of methods. They do not
identify the alleged four cardinals.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall combines the public anti-religious strategy, Dodd's genuine senior
Party career, her documented account of covert professional cells, the later
von Hildebrand recollection, and clerical decline into one causal narrative:
Communist operatives entered seminaries, rose undetected, and later acquired
governing power.

The sources support the feasibility and the general method more strongly than
they support the scale, number, identity, or eventual rank of the infiltrators.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** Dodd participates in a compartmented program to steer selected
operatives and morally compromised recruits toward Catholic seminaries. She
knows, through Party channels, of four men who eventually receive the
cardinalate. She does not personally recruit every operative and is not given
complete biographical files.

For narrative restraint:

- the “1,100” figure should be treated as a later exaggeration unless a project
  file deliberately establishes it;
- only a minority of troubled, unorthodox, or corrupt clergy are Communist
  agents;
- some recruits abandon the Party, some lose contact, and some were never
  ideological Communists but were selected because they could be coerced;
- Dodd's inability or refusal to provide names prevents the claim from solving
  the setting's personnel mysteries automatically.

This permits the four-cardinal claim while preserving suspense and the
fallibility of investigators.

## 7. Pius XII: reform, theological discipline, and retrospective blame, 1947–1958

### Liturgical reform before Vatican II

Pius XII's [*Mediator
Dei*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_20111947_mediator-dei.html)
(1947) both encourages the liturgical movement and sets limits. It supports
active participation, scholarship, and legitimate development, while rejecting
the idea that ancient practice is necessarily better merely because it is
ancient. It also reserves authoritative regulation to the hierarchy.

Pius XII established the Commission for Liturgical Reform in 1948. The reform
sequence included the experimental Easter Vigil in 1951 and the revised Holy
Week in 1955.

Annibale Bugnini served as the commission's secretary. Later public reputation
often turns this administrative fact into a claim that he personally designed
and drove every reform. The surviving commission record gives other figures,
especially Ferdinando Antonelli and Josef Löw, substantial roles. Bugnini was
important as secretary and later became much more powerful, but the archival
case does not support making him sole author of the Pian reforms.

### Theological controversy

Pius XII's [*Humani
generis*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html)
(1950) warns against false irenicism, doctrinal relativization, disdain for
scholastic theology, and interpretations of dogma that dissolve stable meaning.
It does not name a single monolithic “new theology” organization.

Réginald Garrigou-Lagrange's 1946 article “La nouvelle théologie où va-t-elle?”
gave the controversy its famous label and argued that a theology which changes
with philosophy risks changing the faith itself.

The theologians later important at Vatican II had quite different histories:

- Henri de Lubac was restricted by Jesuit superiors and removed from teaching
  amid the controversy surrounding *Surnaturel*, then rehabilitated and called
  to the Council;
- Yves Congar experienced publication and teaching restrictions before being
  invited as a conciliar expert;
- Karl Rahner was subject to Roman scrutiny but worked within a different
  philosophical and theological project;
- Joseph Ratzinger belonged to a younger generation, served as Joseph Frings's
  expert, and cannot accurately be reduced to a prewar clandestine cell.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall's narrative treats the last Pius XII years as the point at which
disciplined ideas and personnel survive under protection, while liturgical
experimentation furnishes techniques later used more aggressively. Pius's
declining health is made part of the explanation for appointments and reforms
that the narrative views as dangerous.

### Better setting reconstruction

Pius XII knowingly authorizes limited reform; he is not merely tricked into
every change. A covert faction exploits real scholarly movements and lawful
commissions by:

- controlling which memoranda reach decision-makers;
- presenting maximal proposals as technical necessities;
- using pilot reforms to establish administrative precedent;
- merging sincere pastoral arguments with ideological goals;
- protecting promising personnel without disclosing the full program.

This makes institutional capture possible without making Pius XII passive or
incompetent.

## 8. John XXIII, the Third Secret, and a pastoral council, 1958–1962

### Documented sequence

John XXIII announced an ecumenical council on 25 January 1959. The third part
of the Fátima secret was available in Rome during his pontificate, but it was
not publicly released in 1960.

In his [opening address of 11 October
1962](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-xxiii/la/speeches/1962/documents/hf_j-xxiii_spe_19621011_opening-council.html),
John reaffirmed the enduring magisterium and distinguished the deposit of faith
from the way in which it is presented. He criticized prophets of doom and said
the Church now preferred the medicine of mercy to severity.

The address does not announce a rejection of doctrine. It changes tone,
method, expectation, and the desired relationship with the modern world. Those
changes are historically consequential even if the council remains an act of
the Church's magisterium.

### The preparatory schemas

Roman commissions prepared draft texts before the Council. Once the Council
opened, a majority of fathers resisted simply accepting preselected commission
members and several prepared schemas were rejected, remade, or replaced.

The dramatic importance is procedural:

- the Curia did not control the Council merely by controlling preparation;
- organized episcopal coalitions and their experts could alter agendas;
- procedural knowledge, translations, private conferences, and draft
  alternatives became instruments of influence;
- bishops who shared broad reform goals did not need a single secret master to
  act collectively.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

In Marshall's arc, the refusal to publish the secret and the rejection of
prepared schemas are paired: warning is set aside just as the anti-Modernist
guardrails are bypassed. The formerly restricted theologians return as
*periti*, and the generation imagined by the Alta Vendita enters positions from
which it can shape language.

### AltWWII use

The setting retains the documented procedural struggle. The covert faction
possesses advance intelligence, translation networks, and a small coordinating
secretariat, but it does not control all reform-minded bishops. Its achievement
is to make itself useful to a much larger coalition.

The withheld Fatima explanatory text is known to John XXIII and a handful of
officials. John's decision not to publish is prudential and deliberate. He does
not understand himself to be suppressing doctrine; he believes an apocalyptic
text would distort the Council and intensify Cold War panic. This makes the
choice tragic without making him a conscious infiltrator.

## 9. Vatican II: text, coalition, and implementation, 1962–1965

### What the Council actually enacted

The Council promulgated four constitutions, nine decrees, and three
declarations, all approved and promulgated by Paul VI. Its acts cannot
responsibly be treated as though they were merely an unofficial conference.

The claim that Vatican II was “pastoral” is relevant to genre, purpose, and the
absence of newly defined dogmas. It does not mean that the Council produced no
authoritative teaching or that Catholics may dismiss every statement at will.

The core documents for this sourcebook are:

- [*Sacrosanctum
  Concilium*](https://press.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html)
  (1963);
- [*Lumen
  gentium*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html)
  (1964);
- [*Dei
  Verbum*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html)
  (1965);
- [*Gaudium et
  spes*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html)
  (1965);
- [*Dignitatis
  humanae*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651207_dignitatis-humanae_en.html)
  (1965);
- [*Nostra
  aetate*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html)
  (1965).

### The liturgical constitution and later reform

*Sacrosanctum Concilium* calls for conscious and active participation and
authorizes revision. It also says:

- the use of Latin is to be preserved in the Latin rites, subject to particular
  law (§36);
- vernacular use may be extended, especially in readings, directives, and
  certain prayers and chants (§§36, 54);
- Gregorian chant should have pride of place, other things being equal (§116);
- no person, even a priest, may add, remove, or change the liturgy on personal
  authority (§22).

Therefore, the complete displacement of Latin and chant cannot simply be
quoted as the Council's explicit command. The broad postconciliar reform was a
later exercise of papal and curial authority carried out under conciliar
mandate.

### Coalition rather than unanimity

The setting should distinguish:

- the official texts;
- the intentions of different voting blocs;
- the intentions of individual drafters;
- postconciliar implementation;
- popular claims about the “spirit of the Council.”

The same sentence could receive votes from bishops who expected different
future applications. Carefully negotiated ambiguity can be a compromise,
ordinary legislative drafting, or a deliberate opening. Which it is must be
established text by text.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall's account emphasizes the rapid reversal of personnel, vocabulary, and
discipline. Formerly suspect theologians become authoritative experts; older
schemas disappear; the new texts supply phrases that later implementers expand.
The book reads this as the institutional success of the earlier infiltration
program.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** A small drafting network deliberately preserves several expandable
formulations and coordinates them with administrators expected to oversee
implementation. This is not true of every disputed passage and does not make
the Council as a whole a counterfeit.

The official texts remain acts of the Council and Paul VI. The covert success
lies in:

- controlling subsequent commentary;
- defining maximal implementations as the only faithful ones;
- marginalizing bishops who read the same language conservatively;
- converting temporary experiments into expectations before objections can
  organize.

This is an institutional, not sacramental, victory.

## 10. Paul VI, Bugnini, and the postconciliar liturgical settlement, 1964–1976

### Documented institutional history

Paul VI established the *Consilium ad exsequendam Constitutionem de Sacra
Liturgia* in 1964 to implement the liturgical constitution. Bugnini served as
its secretary and now exercised the influence that is sometimes
retrospectively projected onto the entire Pius XII period.

The reform proceeded through instructions, transitional rites, experiments,
and the new Order of Mass promulgated in 1969. The first Sunday of Advent 1969,
not an unspecified point in 1970, is the normal implementation landmark,
although complete altar-book publication and local adoption varied.

The “Ottaviani Intervention”—the *Short Critical Study of the New Order of
Mass* sent with a letter from Cardinals Alfredo Ottaviani and Antonio Bacci in
September 1969—argued that the new rite and its initial General Instruction
departed seriously from the theology expressed by the received Roman rite. An
English [text is hosted by Fordham's Modern History
Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/mod/1969ottoviani.asp). It is
evidence of high-level opposition, not proof by itself that the reformers were
Communists or Masons.

### The Bugnini removal

In 1975 Paul VI reorganized the relevant curial offices, leaving Bugnini
without his liturgical post. Bugnini was appointed pro-nuncio to Iran in
January 1976. His abrupt displacement after years of access and influence is a
documented event.

The Freemasonry allegation has several evidentiary stages:

1. lists circulated that assigned Bugnini a Masonic name and a 1963 initiation
   date;
2. Bugnini denied being a Mason;
3. a story circulated that incriminating material was found in a misplaced
   briefcase;
4. Brian Harrison published a version in 1989;
5. later inquiry identified Eric de Saventhem as the intermediary who relayed
   Michael Davies's account and Cardinal Dino Staffa as the prelate said to
   have taken the material to Italian police and Paul VI.

Kevin Symonds's investigation, summarized in [“Bugnini's
Briefcase”](https://lms.org.uk/massofagesarticle/bugninis-briefcase), improves
the story from anonymous rumor to named, retrospective testimony. It still
lacks the briefcase, police report, membership record, or a statement from Paul
VI. That is serious historical allegation, not public documentary proof.

The alleged 1963 initiation date also prevents the allegation from explaining
Bugnini's supposed Masonic direction of Pius XII's reforms years earlier.

### Reconstructed Marshall-type bridge

Marshall makes Bugnini the operational link between preconciliar experiment
and postconciliar revolution. The alleged Masonic membership explains motive;
the briefcase explains his sudden fall; and the new Mass becomes the intended
product of the generational infiltration strategy.

### AltWWII use

**AT:** The misplaced briefcase contains genuine records connecting Bugnini to
an anticlerical lodge and to intermediaries who sought liturgical
de-sacralization. Staffa obtains an Italian police authentication and confronts
Paul VI. The Pope removes Bugnini quietly to avoid a scandal that would damage
the authority of the reform already promulgated.

Limits required for internal coherence:

- Bugnini's formal lodge connection begins in the 1960s, not before Pius XII's
  commission.
- He is a skilled administrator and advocate, not the sole author of every
  liturgical change.
- Paul VI's approval remains a real exercise of papal governance even if
  subordinates manipulate evidence and options presented to him.
- The validity of the reformed sacraments is not made dependent on Bugnini's
  private motives.

These limits keep the allegation politically explosive without turning it into
an automatic theological answer to every liturgical question.

## 11. Consolidated setting narrative through Vatican II

The following is the recommended prose-level reconstruction for use by setting
writers:

> The nineteenth-century secret societies discovered that direct war against
> the papacy made martyrs and provoked reaction. Their more durable legacy was
> a method: form the environment, cultivate the young, reward the useful, and
> let institutions eventually be governed by men who believe the new order is
> their own. That method survived the organizations that first articulated it.
>
> La Salette and Leo XIII supplied the Church's prophetic counter-memory. In
> AltWWII, both the long La Salette warning and Leo's dialogue are authentic,
> but neither is public in a form capable of compelling universal agreement.
> The warnings are therefore powerful enough to guide the perceptive and
> obscure enough to be ignored, politicized, or misread.
>
> Pius X exposed a real Modernist network, but the network was not a single
> species. It included open scholars, discreet mutual-protection circles, and a
> covert minority prepared to use theological change as an instrument of
> institutional capture. Suppression broke its public organization while
> dispersing its personnel into universities, religious orders, and patronage
> chains.
>
> Communist services later adopted compatible techniques for their own
> reasons. Bella Dodd's public testimony accurately described disciplined
> minorities working through respectable institutions. In the setting, her
> private four-cardinal warning is also true. The Communist operation,
> however, intersects with rather than commands every Modernist or Masonic
> network.
>
> Under Pius XII, legitimate liturgical scholarship and theological renewal
> created opportunities. The Pope authorized reform but attempted to hold it
> within doctrinal and juridical boundaries. Covert actors learned to exploit
> the commission system, pilot programs, and selective briefing. Their chief
> achievement was not a single corrupted decree; it was a cadre experienced in
> turning expert recommendation into irreversible administrative momentum.
>
> John XXIII's Council was neither invented nor wholly controlled by the
> infiltrators. It was a genuine council called for pastoral renewal. The
> faction's success came from preparation, coalition service, and procedure.
> It helped larger reform blocs defeat curial control, inserted a limited
> number of expandable formulations, and positioned allies to dominate later
> interpretation.
>
> Paul VI promulgated the Council's documents and approved the liturgical
> reform as Pope. His acts were not nullified by hidden motives among
> subordinates. Yet he received curated options and faced a manufactured sense
> of inevitability. When the Bugnini evidence reached him, he chose quiet
> removal rather than public repudiation. The Church therefore entered the
> later twentieth century with valid institutions and sacraments, but with
> damaged trust, competing interpretive regimes, and several covert networks
> convinced that history had vindicated their methods.

This version preserves the dramatic causal arc while avoiding three weak
shortcuts:

- resemblance is not automatically organizational continuity;
- lawful authority is not automatically absent because advisers acted badly;
- doctrinal, liturgical, political, and moral corruption are related but not
  interchangeable.

## 12. The Sankt Gallen circle, 1996–2006

### Documented minimum

The Sankt Gallen group was a real informal circle of reform-minded European
prelates, not an invention of Marshall. Bishop Ivo Fürer and Cardinal Carlo
Maria Martini were central to its formation. The first regular meeting took
place in January 1996; gatherings ended in 2006.

Participants over time included Martini, Fürer, Walter Kasper, Karl Lehmann,
Godfried Danneels, Cormac Murphy-O'Connor, Achille Silvestrini, and others.
They discussed collegiality, the role of episcopal conferences, the Roman
Curia, the appointment of bishops, sexual ethics, and desired qualities in a
future pope.

The principal disclosed source is Karim Schelkens and Jürgen Mettepenningen's
authorized *Godfried Danneels: Biografie* (2015), whose [bibliographic record is
here](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/danneels-biografie/).
Danneels jokingly used “mafia” language for the circle in publicity surrounding
the biography. The label should not be made to prove criminal structure.

The Diocese of St. Gallen confirmed that the meetings occurred and emphasized
that they ended in 2006, years before Benedict XVI resigned. This chronology is
fatal to a claim that the regular group met continuously to force the 2013
resignation.

### Relation to the conclaves

Members discussed papal candidates around the 2005 conclave, and surviving
members or associates favored Jorge Mario Bergoglio. Accounts of the 2013
pre-conclave period, especially Austen Ivereigh's *The Great Reformer*, describe
reform-minded cardinals promoting Bergoglio through conversations and dinners.
The details of a coordinated “campaign” were disputed by the named cardinals
and the Holy See press office at the time. Later reporting accepts that
significant informal advocacy occurred while remaining unable to inspect
conclave ballots.

John Paul II's [*Universi Dominici
Gregis*](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_jp-ii_apc_22021996_universi-dominici-gregis.html)
§81 forbids pacts, promises, or commitments that oblige cardinals to give or
withhold a vote. The same section explicitly says it does not forbid an
exchange of views during the vacancy. Evidence that cardinals advocated a
candidate is therefore not enough to establish the forbidden pact, much less
the invalidity of the election.

### AltWWII use

The group is setting fact as an influential private circle and a carrier of a
distinct postconciliar program. It is not an Alta Vendita command cell, did not
exist during McCarrick's youth, and did not operate uninterrupted through 2013.
Personal relationships and shared preferences outlive the formal meetings.

This supports a believable long institutional memory without importing the
excluded claims about Crowley, McCarrick's supposed early Swiss recruitment, or
an invalid conclave.

## 13. Benedict XVI's resignation and Francis's election

Benedict XVI's official [*Declaratio* of 11 February
2013](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/speeches/2013/february/documents/hf_ben-xvi_spe_20130211_declaratio.html)
states that he acted with full freedom because declining bodily and mental
strength left him unable to administer the ministry adequately. It declares
that the See of Rome would become vacant and a conclave should be convoked.

His [last general audience](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2013/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20130227.html)
again says that he sought God's guidance, recognized declining energies, and
made the decision for the good of the Church.

The setting may acknowledge pressures Benedict faced—Vatileaks, curial
conflict, abuse cases, governance burdens, and factions hoping for a different
pontificate. It should not state as historical fact that a Vatican ATM shutdown
forced the resignation. The available evidence does not establish that causal
chain, and that proposition remains excluded from setting canon.

The defensible Sankt Gallen–Francis bridge is:

1. an informal reform circle existed and discussed a different model of
   governance;
2. some members or associates had favored Bergoglio by 2005;
3. the regular group ceased meeting in 2006;
4. informal networks and preferences survived;
5. reform-minded prelates promoted Bergoglio in the open discussion period
   before the 2013 conclave;
6. no public evidence establishes a binding pact that invalidated his election.

This is enough background for later ecclesiastical factions in the setting. The
McCarrick material, Vatican financial speculation, and extended account of the
Francis pontificate are intentionally omitted because they occur too late to
shape the principal AltWWII divergence.

## Final use note

When this material appears in a setting document, label the viewpoint:

- **archival fact** for checkable records;
- **witness allegation** for recollection or testimony;
- **factional interpretation** for a character's causal synthesis;
- **authorial truth** only in out-of-world canon notes.

Characters should almost never possess this sourcebook's complete layered
picture. The difference between what is true, what can be proved, and what a
faction believes is one of the setting's most useful sources of conflict.
