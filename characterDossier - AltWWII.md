# AltWWII Character Dossier

## Scope and authority

This dossier collects the **named original post-divergence characters currently established in the setting**. Historical people are omitted even when their alternate careers are central. Rejected alternatives, unnamed placeholders, and people proposed only as examples are not treated as cast members.

Status follows the Setting Bible:

- **Canon:** directly established or subsequently relied upon.
- **Working canon:** accepted role and characterization with details still requiring confirmation.
- **Open:** intentionally incomplete.

## German ruling household

### Wolfgang Friedrich Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in late 1947 after Adolf Hitler and Eva Braun enter a discreet legal marriage, Wolfgang is presented as the *Siegeskind*. Göring designates him eventual successor in 1962 and voluntarily transfers the Führership to him in late 1965.

**Personality:** Naturally observant, slow to trust, routine-bound, verbally gifted, imaginative, stubborn when engaged, and not instinctively aggressive. Ceremonial training gives him a genuinely warm, curious, articulate public manner. The result is melancholic-sanguine: privately analytical, sensitive, controlled, and perfectionistic; publicly charming and capable of making technical interest feel personal.

**Goals:**

- preserve German primacy without fighting every rival simultaneously;
- convert conquest into durable infrastructure, rules, standards, and client systems;
- make the Reich technologically modern without surrendering hierarchy;
- obtain information not filtered through factional “Second Drafts”;
- internationalize German achievements, especially the Raumhafen and Weltnetz;
- prove that German leadership can be materially beneficial while never repudiating the imperial order that gives him power.

**Relationships:**

- **Adolf Hitler:** father, founder, and emotional absence. Wolfgang does not reproduce him genetically or politically, but never repudiates him.
- **Eva Braun:** emotional center of his childhood and guardian of the household.
- **Hermann Göring:** affectionate political guardian who teaches ceremony and transfers power voluntarily.
- **Erwin Rommel:** model of duty, professional limits, and the responsibility of ordering others into danger.
- **Joseph Goebbels:** tutor in words and public memory; valued personally, distrusted intellectually.
- **Reinhard Heydrich:** uncanny but valued instructor in motives, files, and competing bureaucratic interests.
- **Martin Bormann:** source of knowledge about appointments and access, but also the example that makes Wolfgang divide power.
- **Wernher von Braun:** technical refuge and model of the systems architect who can tell a ruler the difference between possibility, production, funding, and operational sustainability.
- **Katharina:** spouse and only near-age intimate who is neither subordinate, relative, nor inherited mentor.
- **Alexander:** eldest son and presumptive heir, whose public ease, learned aerospace interest, and attraction to state ceremony resemble Wolfgang while making him more receptive to SS Order culture.
- **Konrad:** decisive second son whose racing and athletic interests draw military attention without making him a manufactured “soldier prince.”
- **Johanna:** quiet archival daughter who shares Eva's private family memory and later collaborates naturally with Felix.
- **Sophie:** socially gifted daughter whose warmth is politically useful but whom Katharina protects from becoming a permanent propaganda performance.
- **Felix:** technically patient younger son whose emerging interest in programming and networks gives Wolfgang a connection to the next technological generation.
- **Helena and Anna:** the two youngest daughters, whose film and musical interests remain developmental rather than fixed careers at the 1985 endpoint.
- **Akihito:** genuine personal respect that makes the German–Japanese détente possible but cannot substitute for interest.
- **Umberto II:** respected rival whose trust permits the Verona non-aggression settlement.
- **Pat Buchanan:** initially hoped-for nationalist interlocutor who proves unwilling to concede German power in the Americas.

**Key events:**

- 1962: father's death; Göring regency and formal designation.
- 1963–65: apprenticeship in budgets, security, foreign affairs, and competing briefs.
- Late 1965: accession at eighteen.
- 1966: Göring's death; Personal Staff for Situation Review created.
- 1967–68: Iceland crisis teaches bounded coercion and negotiated exits.
- 1970–75: Moon landing and Raumhafen make infrastructure his signature.
- 1978–79: demographic scandal teaches that “encouragement” becomes coercion where the state controls livelihoods.
- 1981–82: accepts orbital incident rules and permits civilian computer-language experimentation.
- 1983–85: internationalizes the Raumhafen, supports Weltnetz, misjudges the Falklands war, then concludes the Tehran and Verona settlements.

**Religion and succession:** Maintains a Lutheran household, accepts ordinary Protestant rites, and attends some SS ancestral ceremonies as observer and patron while rejecting most Himmlerite esotericism. The eldest child has dynastic prestige, but institutional designation and confirmation—not primogeniture—control succession.

**Open:** Final successor designation and fate beyond 1985.

### Katharina Elisabeth Hartmann Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1948 into a professionally respected but politically uninteresting technical-medical family. Her father, Dr.-Ing. Matthias Hartmann, works in aviation systems and civil certification; her mother, Elisabeth Hartmann née Vollmer, is a physician or senior child-health administrator. Katharina studies rehabilitation medicine and meets Wolfgang at a rehabilitation or aerospace-medical institution in 1967. They marry in 1970. Her rapid first pregnancy redirects her principal career toward pediatrics and maternal health.

**Personality:** Intelligent, grounded, physically active, socially capable, and secure enough to admit what she does not understand. She treats patients normally in Wolfgang's presence and does not reorganize her personality around power.

**Goals:**

- preserve an honest private life inside a political household;
- improve child health, rehabilitation, maternal medicine, hospital standards, and support for hazardous-service families;
- protect Wolfgang's capacity for uncertainty without becoming an unofficial minister;
- keep household intimacy from becoming another factional office.

**Relationships:** Accepted by Eva for caring about Wolfgang rather than the founder cult; approved by Heydrich because of her clean security profile; tolerated by Bormann because she brings no machine; understood by technical circles; initially underwhelming to Himmler's dynastic planners. She is Wolfgang's principal private confidante and quietly influences his response to the demographic scandal. She is the children's actual daily mother: household staff support nursing, security, travel, and education without replacing her control of ordinary illness, discipline, schooling, friends, meals, and bedtime.

**Key events:** Meeting in 1967; correspondence during Iceland in 1968; engagement in 1969; civil marriage and private Lutheran blessing in 1970; first child in 1971; six additional births in 1973, 1975, 1977, 1979, 1982, and 1984; pediatric, maternal, rehabilitation, and family public work through the 1970s; private role during the 1978–79 policy review.

**Household and education:** Katharina prepares the daughters principally for marriage, motherhood, household responsibility, cultural patronage, and public representation rather than compulsory professional careers, while allowing serious personal interests. She rejects both courtly parental distance and the fiction that a ruling family needs no staff. She also limits the propaganda ministry's access to the children: affection may be photographed, but friendships, exhaustion, illness, and distress may not be manufactured into spectacles.

**Open:** Precise public title beyond *Frau Hitler* and the children's adult lives.

## Children of Wolfgang and Katharina

The family comprises three sons and four daughters. The names honor German and maternal-family traditions without binding a child to a living factional patron. None is named Adolf: Wolfgang does not repudiate his father, but refuses to make the founder's first name a second office imposed upon the eldest boy.

The children are raised comfortably but with deliberate household frugality. They reuse clothes and toys, share rooms when young, perform age-appropriate chores, and receive books, instruments, tools, and practical equipment more readily than courtly luxuries. Nurses, tutors, physicians, security personnel, and household staff are unavoidable, but support rather than replace the parents. Wolfgang keeps regular family meals, rotating individual time, letters or recorded messages while traveling, and attendance at important personal events whenever state obligations permit.

### Alexander Friedrich Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1971; eldest son. Foreign observers call him a crown prince, and German institutions regard him as the presumptive first candidate for succession, but Wolfgang has neither designated him nor altered the legal requirement for institutional confirmation.

**Personality:** Sanguine-melancholic—publicly fluent, socially comfortable, attentive to faces and ceremony, but privately perfectionistic, sensitive to criticism, and eager to satisfy inherited expectations. He shares Wolfgang's two dominant temperamental elements in reverse order.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Aerospace becomes a genuine learned interest. Alexander initially asks about missions because technical questions draw Wolfgang into unusually open conversation; repeated positive attention produces real fascination. He focuses less on engineering dependencies than on crews, launch narratives, public memory, ceremony, and the national meaning of exploration. His emerging goal is to make state achievement emotionally intelligible to ordinary Germans.

**Relationships:** Close to Wolfgang through aerospace but disagrees with him over pomp. Alexander believes ceremony gives people a language for shared meaning; Wolfgang warns that spectacle must never become evidence that a policy was correct. Alexander and Konrad are genuinely affectionate complementary brothers even as institutions begin comparing them.

**SS relationship:** More emotionally receptive than Wolfgang to memorial ritual, genealogy, Wewelsburg, oath, inherited obligation, and Hagedorn's codified Order culture. By 1985 he is interested, not captured. Wolfgang allows study, visits, and public ceremonies but forbids a private exclusive oath, a separate SS educational household, or treatment of Alexander as the SS's candidate.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Fourteen in 1985; publicly promising, privately burdened, presumptive rather than designated heir.

### Konrad Matthias Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1973; second son and the most visible alternative within the male succession generation.

**Personality:** Choleric-phlegmatic—decisive, competitive, physically confident, loyal, difficult to embarrass, tolerant of risk, and emotionally controlled enough to choose rather than merely erupt into confrontations.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Motor racing, competitive sport, and physical command. He likes speed, rapid judgment, and controlling a powerful machine more than mechanical maintenance. At twelve this means guarded karting, simulators, skiing, riding, shooting, football, and controlled track sessions rather than unrestricted racing. His personal goal is to possess a life and field of competence that are not defined merely as insurance against Alexander's death or failure.

**Relationships:** Alexander persuades and gives decisions meaning; Konrad decides and gives them momentum. Military officers notice his courage and command presence, but Wolfgang and Katharina resist manufacturing a “soldier prince.” He may eventually serve, but childhood is not an extended recruiting film.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Twelve in 1985; strongly noticed by military and hard administrative circles without having chosen a military vocation.

### Johanna Elisabeth Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1975; first daughter and quiet keeper of the younger household's memory.

**Personality:** Melancholic-phlegmatic—exact, loyal, emotionally private, patient, difficult to provoke, and inclined to reconcile disputes through accurate reconstruction rather than charm.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Archives, education, documentary preservation, letters, photographs, home films, maps, speech drafts, and the difference between original records and official retellings. She wants to know what actually happened before deciding what it ought to mean.

**Relationships:** Especially close to Eva Braun, whose private films, correspondence, and ordinary memories complicate founder mythology. Johanna also becomes Felix's natural collaborator: she supplies archival judgment while he makes records searchable through catalogs, databases, and digitization.

**Potential trajectory:** Family archive, historical foundation, schools, libraries, and controlled release of private papers are plausible adult roles, not fixed offices.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Ten in 1985; already trusted by siblings because she rarely converts confidences into performance.

### Sophie Eva Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1977; second daughter and the family's most naturally social child.

**Personality:** Sanguine-phlegmatic—warm, expressive, observant of relationships, quick to notice who is nervous or excluded, and naturally suited to hospitality. Her childhood weakness is gossip: she perceives more than she yet understands should remain private.

**Keystone interest and goal:** People and relationships themselves—hospitality, visits, charitable patronage, receiving foreign families, and informal representation. Katharina teaches that understanding people is a gift while exposing them is not the same gift.

**Relationships:** Werner Naumann's ministry recognizes her extraordinary future propaganda value. Katharina permits genuine public encounters but prohibits manufactured friendships, scripted affection, repeated appearances by an exhausted child, and exploitation of illness or distress.

**Potential trajectory:** Public representation, family patronage, charitable work, and informal diplomacy are plausible; no formal career is predetermined.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Eight in 1985; socially gifted and photogenic, but still being taught discretion.

### Felix Wolfgang Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1979; third son and representative of the technological generation forming after the Moon race.

**Personality:** Phlegmatic-melancholic—calm, self-contained, technically patient, less directly competitive, and inclined to understand a system before seeking control of it.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Computing, programming languages, games, protocols, networks, search, simulation, and communication between unlike systems. Hardware knowledge is substantial enough to understand processors, memory, communications, and compatibility, but software and networks become his distinctive field as he grows through the later 1980s.

**Relationships:** Aerospace remains a shared family subject rather than his personal identity. Matthias Falkenberg and other computing specialists can teach him without displacing Wolfgang. Johanna's archives give the siblings their first natural joint projects: indexing films, cataloguing books, recording elderly household figures, genealogy databases, and digitized correspondence.

**Potential political significance:** Felix may become the child best able to explain how searchable records and interconnected machines change Wolfgang's state. At the 1985 endpoint this is a trajectory, not an office or succession decision.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Six in 1985; curious around household terminals, telemetry, games, and communications equipment.

### Helena Katharina Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1982; third daughter.

**Personality:** Melancholic-sanguine—inward, aesthetically sensitive, and capable of public expression once convinced that a subject matters.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Film, photography, editing, interviews, documentary narration, medical and scientific film, family history, and the emotional interpretation of recorded events. Where Johanna preserves a record, Helena's likely contribution is shaping it so that someone absent can understand what the event felt like.

**Relationships:** Katharina supplies practical nursing, childcare, and household-medicine formation without requiring Helena to become a physician. Eva's private films, Raumhafen imagery, and official propaganda provide both material and eventual ethical tension.

**Key state at the endpoint:** Three in 1985. Film is an authorially intended developmental direction, not a demonstrated childhood specialization or fixed profession.

### Anna Maria Hitler

**Identity and role:** Born in 1984; youngest daughter.

**Personality:** Phlegmatic-sanguine—affectionate, approachable, comparatively relaxed, and naturally inclined to reduce family tension.

**Keystone interest and goal:** Music, especially piano, singing, hymns, choral life, family performance, and later musical patronage or composition. Music gives her a household role that is neither administrative nor competitive and may make her the sibling most capable of gathering political rivals into an ordinary family evening.

**Generational position:** Anna grows up with the established Wolfgang of Tehran, Verona, the international Raumhafen, and the post-Falklands reassessment rather than the young ruler still proving himself through the Moon program.

**Key state at the endpoint:** One in 1985. Her temperament, music, and mediating role are prospective character architecture rather than completed biography.

### The succession triangle

By the later 1980s the three sons imply different possible futures without producing an automatic contest. Alexander represents public legitimacy, ceremony, aerospace, and historical meaning. Konrad represents decision, physical confidence, and military-administrative action. Felix represents software, networks, and the technical world emerging after his brothers' childhood formation. Alexander begins first in public expectation, but lawful succession still requires Wolfgang's designation and the regime's institutional confirmation. The unresolved question is which child could inherit the office without allowing the office to consume the person.

## German second generation

### Otto Albrecht Seidel

**Identity and role:** Born 1921; lawyer and personnel statistician. He specializes in cadre records, technical appointments, and imperial-service careers. After Bormann's 1 October 1973 retirement, Seidel heads the **Party Main Office for Cadre Order and Appointments** while Party law, legal promulgation, and access to dissenting information remain in other hands.

**Personality:** Courteous, uncharismatic, meticulous, and institutionally dangerous precisely because he does not seek a public following.

**Goal:** Make the Reich's personnel system predictable, searchable, and useful without recreating Bormann's monopoly over every door.

**Relationships:** Works alongside Gerhard Klopfer and Wolfgang's Personal Staff in an intentionally divided succession. Owes authority to Wolfgang's determination that no single official again control organization, careers, law, and access.

**Key event:** Division of the Party Chancellery after Bormann's disabling stroke and retirement on 1 October 1973; Bormann dies on 10 April 1974.

### Friedrich Hagedorn

**Identity and role:** Born 1918; wartime SS officer and postwar administrator in Ukraine and Ostland. By the early 1970s he is Deputy Reichsführer for Order and Settlement. Wolfgang confirms him as **Reichsführer-SS** on 28 May 1976 after Himmler's death.

**Personality:** Severe, doctrinal, methodical, and distrustful of flamboyant cruelty because it harms production and the legitimacy of Order. He is not humane; he is an administrator of mature racial empire.

**Goal:** Transform the SS from revolutionary terror instrument into a permanent oath-bound governing estate with codified settlement, service, and population categories.

**Relationships:** Functional heir to Himmler under tighter institutional rules; acceptable to Wolfgang because he is administratively legible, dangerous because his governing vision entrenches the SS indefinitely.

**Key events:** Himmler's death on 23 May 1976 and Hagedorn's succession five days later under the Reich Leadership Council; promulgation of the final SS *Glaubensordnung* on 21 December 1979. Hagedorn relies upon Rabe for doctrinal synthesis while retaining institutional authority over the faith.

### Professor Dr. Johannes Eberhard Rabe

**Identity and role:** Born in Lüneburg in 1921. Initially trained in Protestant theology, Rabe moves into classics, Old Norse, Sanskrit, Indo-European studies, comparative religion, and philosophy. He enters SS education during the war and serves as translator, funerary administrator, and Wewelsburg editor. By 1980 he is an SS-Oberführer, director of the Office for Doctrine and Interpretation, and **Lehrwalter des Ordensglaubens**—Custodian of Order Doctrine.

**Personality:** Precise, patient, intellectually hierarchical, and wary of charismatic improvisation. He prefers concordances, distinctions, source ranking, and explicit ritual consequences to inspired slogans.

**Goal:** Give the Order Faith a coherent theology that can survive Himmler without either dissolving into private occultism or becoming an imitation of Christianity. His seven-book *Die Ordnung des Lebendigen* systematizes divine order, gods, blood, Volk, personhood, death, recurrence, duty, purification, ritual, and the relationship among Führer, Order, household, and foreign religions.

**Relationships:** Hagedorn gives Rabe bureaucratic protection but remains his superior. Wolfgang accepts him as a stabilizing interpreter while remaining personally Lutheran. Rabe's doctrine that Providence may employ a ruler outside the inner faith is essential to preserving SS loyalty to Wolfgang.

**Key event:** During the war Rabe experiences the **Hall of Names** vision. His father's independently confirmed sacrificial death persuades him that the vision deserves interpretation, but his insistence that names appear only at birth and living pages remain open preserves free will against predestination.

### Ernst Keller

**Identity and role:** An SS-Oberscharführer who serves as a dependable records and instructional clerk. He is the second of the Order Faith's two accepted corroborated visionaries, but never becomes its theologian or a self-appointed prophet.

**Personality:** Conscientious, literal, modest, and institutionally obedient. His credibility derives from reliability and lack of ambition rather than rhetorical force.

**Goal:** Preserve an accurate account of what he experienced without claiming authority beyond the evidence.

**Relationships:** Rabe seals Keller's testimony before investigation and later interprets it under rules designed to separate a true experience from a visionary's potentially mistaken conclusions. Keller's Great Hall account is linked to the records of First World War soldier Josef Hintermayr and the birth of Hintermayr's namesake descendant.

**Key event:** After hearing Himmler speak about Barbarossa and heroic recurrence, Keller dreams of the Great Hall, Green Height, glacier, and wounded Bavarian soldier. Independent record matches make it a *bestätigte Schau*—a corroborated vision—without elevating Keller to permanent prophetic status.

### Dr. Matthias Kern

**Identity and role:** Born 1919; lawyer and statistical administrator experienced in identity systems, territorial audits, and security procedure. He heads a reorganized domestic Security and Administrative Audit Office.

**Personality:** Emotionally cold, procedural, skeptical, and obsessed with cross-checking police, labor, travel, and production records.

**Goal:** Replace theatrical terror and rival unchecked files with a security system that can reconcile the empire's records and detect contradictions.

**Relationships:** Administrative rather than operational heir within Heydrich's divided legacy; colder than Schellenberg but intentionally prevented from controlling foreign intelligence and household security.

**Key event:** Heydrich's retirement on 1 October 1978 formally divides foreign intelligence, domestic security audit, and household protection; Heydrich dies on 4 June 1979.

### Dr. Werner Lenz

**Identity and role:** Born 1920; engineer-economist trained in machine tools, automation, and nuclear-industrial procurement. After Speer's 1 October 1977 retirement he directs the **Reich Fund for Special Development** and chairs the **Technical Investment Board**, linking civilian industry, aerospace, computing, and armaments.

**Personality:** Technical, integrative, investment-minded, and more interested in capacity than monumental architecture.

**Goal:** Direct German capital toward the industrial systems that connect production, energy, aerospace, computing, and defense.

**Relationships:** Works beside Karl Schiller's macroeconomic coordination and Albert Speer Jr.'s architecture rather than replacing Speer alone.

**Key event:** Speer's retirement on 1 October 1977 and death on 1 September 1981.

### Dr. Matthias Falkenberg

**Identity and role:** Born in Königsberg in 1940 and raised in Riga after 1948 in a colonial technical household. A distributed-computing and satellite-communications architect, he becomes Wolfgang's Special Commissioner for Computer Federation and the World Network by 1983.

**Personality:** Visionary, persuasive, technically synthetic, politically adroit, and sincerely internationalist without being liberal. He favors identified, authenticated, logged, hierarchically administered connectivity.

**Goals:**

- make physical distance an engineering problem rather than a limit on German power;
- connect firms, banks, universities, clients, satellites, and software markets through **Weltnetz**;
- preserve heterogeneous civilian language and machine ecosystems so long as they can interoperate;
- make German standards the connective layer among otherwise separate world networks.

**Relationships:** Introduced to Wolfgang by Helmut Gröttrup and Eberhard Rees around 1980–81. Wolfgang sees in him the computing analogue of von Braun. Falkenberg uses orbital imagery to win patronage while knowing most traffic will move by cable, microwave, fiber, and local packet networks.

**Key events:** 1979 “Computing Without Distance” lecture; 1981 formal Weltnetz study; 1982 language-policy intervention and special commission; 1983–85 expansion of civilian Planform, software houses, satellite gateways, and partner networks.

### Ilmar Bielenstein

**Identity and role:** Provisionally born in Riga in 1956 into a Baltic-German technical family. A programmer at an Ostland mathematical-systems bureau, he creates the falling-block puzzle **Mauerwerk**.

**Personality:** Quiet, playful, formally trained, and poorly equipped for the legal and political struggle that follows his recreational program.

**Goal:** Treat the game as an elegant mathematical diversion and see it played, rather than become the proprietor of a global entertainment property.

**Relationships and conflict:** His employer claims work-product ownership; the German state distributor claims export authority; a foreign licensee claims commercial exclusivity; and an American publisher claims Western-market rights. Each possesses enough paperwork to make the dispute durable.

**Status:** Provisional background character. Employer, licensees, and final rights outcome may change when the story is developed.

## Philippine independence generation

### Colonel Mateo de Vera

**Identity and role:** Fictional Philippine Army officer and wartime-trained organizer who coordinates Luzon cells during the independence struggle and later helps turn defectors and guerrillas into a professional national force.

**Personality:** Disciplined, guarded, practical, and suspicious of both American tutelage and uncontrolled revolutionary romanticism.

**Goal:** Create armed institutions loyal to a sovereign Philippine government rather than to provincial patrons or Japanese handlers.

**Relationships:** Accepts Laurel, Recto, and Tañada as necessary civilian legitimizers; relies on Lena Magsalin's civic networks; cooperates uneasily with Isidro Cabahug's maritime organization and Japanese trainers.

### Elena “Lena” Magsalin

**Identity and role:** Fictional lawyer-journalist who builds courier, municipal, labor, legal-defense, and prisoners' family networks during emergency rule.

**Personality:** Socially adept, relentless, skeptical of uniforms, and unusually good at translating elite constitutional language into local obligation.

**Goal:** Ensure independence produces functioning civil government and does not merely exchange American military supervision for Japanese or domestic military rule.

**Relationships:** Bridges elder nationalist civilians and younger clandestine organizers; frequently challenges de Vera over emergency authority and protects Cabahug's port networks through legal and municipal cover.

### Captain Isidro Cabahug

**Identity and role:** Fictional Cebuano sailor and maritime organizer who connects Visayan ports, fishermen, smugglers, defecting sailors, and Japanese liaison officers. He becomes one of the institutional founders of the post-independence Philippine Navy.

**Personality:** Gregarious, opportunistic, locally rooted, and intensely serious about seamanship and Philippine ownership of transferred vessels.

**Goal:** Make the Independence Fleet a genuinely Philippine service rather than a Japanese squadron with Filipino paint and crews.

**Relationships:** Operational partner to de Vera, protected by Magsalin's civic network, and natural advocate for BRP *Lapu-Lapu* and the transferred escort force.

## American computing founders

### Elias Rowan Mercer

**Identity and role:** Born in Pasadena in 1951; founder of Silicon Valley-based **Concord Computer Works**, creator of the Concord architecture, Civic language, Concord One (1978), and graphical Concord Meridian (1983).

**Personality:** Sociable, theatrical, patriotic, moralizing, relentlessly confident, and gifted at explaining technology to nonprogrammers.

**Goal:** Make the personal computer a coherent civic and cultural object whose users need not assemble their own technical republic.

**Relationships:** Open National Renewal voter and cultural sympathizer; supports Buchanan in 1980 and publicly endorses Phillips–Landry in 1984. His closed development tools, serialized systems, controlled formats, and publisher agreements provoke critics who call Concord “the gated republic.” He and Martin Keene dislike one another personally as well as commercially.

**Key events:** Concord One's 1978 mass launch; support for Buchanan; Concord Meridian in 1983; public endorsement of Phillips triggers employee protests and urban boycotts in 1984.

### Martin Avery Keene

**Identity and role:** Born in Seattle in 1953; founder of Seattle-based **Interlink Software Corporation** and architect of the Bridge operating environment. IBM selects Bridge/16 for its 1981 personal-computer family.

**Personality:** Reserved, dry, impatient with ceremony, obsessive about interfaces and compatibility, and indifferent to grand political narratives.

**Goal:** Make software portable across temporary hardware through stable interfaces, translators, device abstraction, and licensed environments.

**Relationships:** Regards Mercer as pompous, controlling, wasteful, and politically dangerous; Mercer regards him as rootless and culturally irresponsible. Keene is not pro-German and refuses to treat general-purpose software as an instrument that should have a national foreign policy.

**Key events:** Meeting and immediate antipathy with Mercer at Toronto in 1977; IBM Bridge/16 selection in 1981; 1982–83 Munich Licensing Affair; German and Argentine use of Interlink-derived tools becomes a political scandal after the Falklands seizure.

## American political generation

### Gabriel E. Landry

**Identity and role:** Born in Louisiana in 1946; fictional Catholic economic nationalist associated with Gulf energy, ports, shipbuilding, technical education, and family policy. He wins the Louisiana governorship young and becomes Howard Phillips's 1984 vice-presidential nominee.

**Personality:** Energetic, personable, regionally accented without caricature, optimistic about industrial development, and less doctrinal than Phillips.

**Goals:**

- retain the Southern, Catholic, working-family, and mission-industrial parts of Buchanan's coalition;
- prove that National Renewal can produce a second generation not formed by Rockwell;
- connect energy and port development to national technical capacity;
- preserve constitutional electoral competition while defending much of Buchanan's economic settlement.

**Relationships:** Phillips values his regional and religious balance but sometimes distrusts his flexibility. Buchanan approves him as evidence of generational continuity. Elias Mercer endorses Phillips–Landry. Old-guard Renewal figures regard Landry as another dilution of founder doctrine.

**Key event:** Selection for the 1984 ticket and lawful defeat by Reagan–Kemp. His role in post-defeat National Renewal remains open.

**Post-1984 development:** Landry responds to defeat by treating constitutional transfer as proof that Renewal can survive opposition. He becomes the clearest spokesman for constitutional Buchananism: strong family policy, domestic ownership, technical capacity, and regional development without compulsory industrial organization. In 1992 he acknowledges Harwood's lawful nomination but refuses to endorse him for president, urging Renewal votes down ballot. After the schism he leads **Constitutional Renewal**.

### Nathaniel Reed Bell

**Identity and role:** Born in Colorado in 1949; engineer-administrator formed by the Front Range aerospace expansion, orbital logistics, and mission procurement. Bell becomes National Renewal's principal mission-technocratic leader, presidential nominee in 1988, and Thomas Harwood's running mate in 1992.

**Personality:** Methodical, courteous, dry, physically self-contained, and more comfortable explaining a schedule or failure tree than performing grievance. He believes technical missions can reconcile classes and regions because a launch either works or does not.

**Formative experiences:** Bell grows up during the Rocket Awakening near families whose livelihoods depend on test ranges, subcontractors, technical schools, and federal procurement. Early work in aerospace administration teaches him both the productive force of public missions and the danger of political managers who conceal failure from engineers.

**Politics and goals:** Mission technocracy; long-horizon infrastructure; aerospace, nuclear, and computing capacity; published performance standards; domestic ownership; and a meritocratic state that uses contracts rather than mass ideological mobilization. He distrusts both Reagan's optimism about ownership controls and Harwood's belief that sector organization can substitute for technical competence.

**Relationships:** Respects Buchanan as the president who made technical administration culturally legible. Kincaid supplies moral and regional breadth to their 1988 ticket. Harwood values Bell's credibility and selects him in 1992, but their alliance deteriorates when presidential discipline becomes compulsory party organization. Landry regards him as honorable but too willing to serve systems he hopes to moderate from within.

**Key events:** Loses the 1988 election with Miriam Kincaid roughly 58–41; accepts the 1992 vice-presidential nomination after the crash; becomes the central constitutional question inside Harwood's administration when Renewal fractures in 1993. His precise choice during that schism remains open.

### Miriam Kincaid

**Identity and role:** Born in Nebraska in 1950; Christian-social agrarian organizer, cooperative-policy advocate, and National Renewal vice-presidential nominee in 1988. She joins Wesley Crane in forming **American Producers** after the 1993 schism.

**Personality:** Warm in small rooms, formidable in hearings, patient with ordinary complaint, and resistant to the theatrical language of national destiny. She listens longer than most Renewal leaders and remembers the practical burdens hidden inside policy abstractions.

**Formative experiences:** Kincaid's politics develop around farm credit, cooperative storage, rural hospitals, church relief, school consolidation, and the vulnerability of towns dependent on one processor or bank. The experience makes her sympathetic to collective provision but suspicious of remote corporate or federal command.

**Politics and goals:** Family income, farm and producer cooperatives, rural credit, local hospitals and schools, anti-monopoly enforcement, conservation, and Christian social obligation. She accepts national planning where small communities cannot bargain alone, but insists that institutions remain answerable to households and counties.

**Relationships:** Bell trusts her moral judgment and chooses her as his 1988 running mate. Crane shares her defense of independent producers, though he is more market-oriented and hostile to subsidy. Landry is a frequent ally on family policy. Harwood's compulsory sector system appears to her as corporate domination wearing the language of solidarity.

**Key events:** 1988 vice-presidential campaign; rural relief advocacy during the Open Door Depression; break with Harwood and co-foundation of American Producers in 1993.

### Wesley Crane

**Identity and role:** Born in Montana in 1952; lawyer, legislator, or small-enterprise organizer associated with producer federalism, ranching and mining communities, and independent firms. He becomes the principal anti-patronage theorist inside National Renewal and co-founds American Producers.

**Personality:** Austere, skeptical, mordantly funny, and impatient with ceremonial unity. He distrusts any institution that promises protection while preventing exit.

**Formative experiences:** Crane is formed by remote communities where freight rates, bank credit, federal land decisions, extractive concessions, and one dominant purchaser can determine whether nominally independent families remain free. He learns to see dependency through contracts rather than formal ownership alone.

**Politics and goals:** Producer federalism; antitrust; portable benefits; local banks; open technical standards; cooperative bargaining; limits on corporate sponsorship; and protection of small firms from both foreign control and domestic patron systems. He supports strategic ownership rules but opposes Harwood's compulsory councils.

**Relationships:** Kincaid humanizes and broadens his producer program. Landry shares his constitutional objections but accepts more national family policy. Cade regards him as economically serious but culturally unreliable; Harwood considers him an obstacle to coordinated recovery.

**Key events:** Builds a western and small-state Renewal network under the Omaha Rules; opposes the 1993 purge; helps establish American Producers; becomes a persistent critic of the 1998 sponsorship settlement.

### Everett Cade

**Identity and role:** Born in Virginia in 1943; veteran movement organizer and custodian of the Founders' Guard, the institutional descendant of Rockwell's old guard. Cade forms the **National Vanguard** after the 1993 Renewal schism.

**Personality:** Formal, disciplined, suspicious, personally brave, and emotionally most comfortable in organizations with ranks, oaths, and inherited memory. He is less flamboyant than early Rockwell activists and more dangerous because he has learned patience.

**Formative experiences:** Cade enters movement politics when National Renewal is still excluded from respectable office. The Stolen Mandate, Rockwell's murder, and Buchanan's prohibition on retaliation teach him that martyr memory can be preserved through lawful organization without surrendering radical ends.

**Politics and goals:** Founders' Guard nationalism, hierarchical civic service, restrictive membership, cultural discipline, veteran and youth formation, racial politics inherited from the early movement, and a strong presidency bounded chiefly by national rather than liberal-constitutional purpose.

**Relationships:** Reveres Rockwell's memory but accepts Buchanan's tactical discipline. He regards Landry as dilution, Bell as technician without doctrine, Kincaid and Crane as provincial interest politicians, and Harwood as a materialist who mistakes industrial organization for national rebirth.

**Key events:** Preserves old-guard institutions through the Buchanan period; loses influence under the Omaha Rules; rejects both Landry and Harwood after 1993 and organizes National Vanguard.

### Thomas Harwood

**Identity and role:** Congressional leader of National Renewal's **Productive Commonwealth** faction, 1992 presidential nominee, and president from 20 January 1993. His precise birthplace and early professional career remain open; an industrial-state congressional base is strongly implied by his coalition.

**Personality:** Severe, prepared, legally exact, and convinced that institutional resistance usually masks private power. He is capable of real analytic foresight and of treating disagreement as evidence that coordination has not yet become compulsory enough.

**Formative experiences:** Harwood rises through congressional work on domestic ownership, sector capacity, family wages, bankruptcy, and industrial charters. The concealed-control scandal vindicates warnings that colleagues had treated as obsession, hardening his belief that procedural opposition serves irresponsible capital.

**Politics and goals:** Chartered domestic industry, sector councils, family wages, closed strategic ownership, public obligations attached to corporate privilege, and a Productive Commonwealth in which labor, management, profession, and state are organized rather than merely consulted.

**Relationships:** Bell gives the 1992 ticket technical legitimacy but resists political compulsion. Landry and Buchanan acknowledge Harwood's lawful nomination without endorsing his program. Crane and Kincaid reject his sector system from a producer perspective. Cade rejects its materialism. Harwood interprets all four objections as remnants of the undisciplined coalition that allowed the crash.

**Key events:** Ownership warnings before 1990; presidential nomination and 51.0–48.5 victory in 1992; attempted caucus binding and committee purge in 1993; resulting five-party schism; charter of Frontier Orbital as a National Orbital Carrier.

### Andrew Prescott Hale

**Classification:** Expanded mentioned fictional character; identifying particulars remain intentionally limited.

**Identity and role:** Democratic-Republican presidential nominee in 1992, paired with Caleb Harlan. Hale represents the constitutional market party after eight years of Reagan and during a depression for which the opening program receives much of the blame.

**Personality:** Courteous, restrained, patient with procedure, emotionally difficult to read, and more reassuring than inspirational. Hale dislikes promises whose legal mechanism cannot be stated and treats emergency speed as a danger in its own right. He can look bloodless beside Harlan or Harwood, but his reserve is deliberate: he wants frightened institutions to behave normally again.

**Formative experiences:** Hale's public identity develops through the constitutional and foreign-policy institutions of the Reagan era rather than through movement politics. He accepts the benefits of commercial opening but is shaped by the discovery that investor-by-investor safeguards did not reveal effective control. His exact profession, offices, state, education, and family remain open so that the final 1992 electoral map can determine the most credible biography.

**Politics and goals:** Emergency relief through ordinary law; competitive enterprise; open commerce with closed strategic ownership; continued defense and space programs; restoration of trustworthy registries and aggregation rules; and rejection of compulsory occupational or sector citizenship. He refuses both to pretend Reagan's opening caused no harm and to let Harwood's diagnosis authorize permanent economic command.

**Relationships:** Caleb Harlan supplies warmth, regional reach, and household language that Hale lacks. Hale respects Reagan's constitutional transfer while campaigning as a corrective rather than an heir. Harwood is a serious opponent whose ownership warning he partly accepts and whose institutional remedy he rejects. Landry's refusal to support Harwood releases constitutional Renewal voters toward Hale without creating a formal coalition.

**Key events:** Democratic-Republican nomination in 1992; defense of the new closed-control rule; defeat by Harwood approximately 48.5–51.0 and 252–286 electoral votes in the last direct two-party election; concession and post-defeat career remain open.

### Caleb Harlan

**Classification:** Expanded mentioned fictional character; identifying particulars remain intentionally limited.

**Identity and role:** Democratic-Republican vice-presidential nominee in 1992. He is selected to broaden Hale's constitutional market coalition during the Open Door Depression.

**Personality:** Warm, quick, policy-fluent, socially adaptable, and much easier than Hale in crowds or unscripted conversation. Harlan can translate ownership law, bankruptcy, education, and regional credit into household consequences without sounding as though he is reading an administrative brief. His flexibility attracts voters and makes ideologues suspect that he believes every room deserves a different answer.

**Formative experiences:** Harlan is formed in state or regional politics where schools, small employers, banks, churches, and family budgets meet federal policy directly. This gives him an instinct for institutional reform presented through ordinary life rather than national systems. His state, prior office, denomination, education, and family remain open so the final ticket balance is not prematurely fixed.

**Politics and goals:** Constitutional markets joined to education, household security, regional development, emergency credit, and rules that distinguish productive enterprise from concealed control. He accepts strategic ownership closure after the crash but argues that recovery must remain legible to families and local institutions rather than belong only to federal authorities and systems corporations.

**Relationships:** Hale provides institutional gravity and foreign-policy reassurance; Harlan supplies the campaign's emotional and regional language. He attacks Harwood's compulsory councils without trivializing the depression that made them attractive. He courts constitutional Renewal defectors while avoiding any claim that Landry has joined the Democratic-Republican ticket.

**Key events:** Selection as Hale's 1992 running mate; principal public campaign role during the final two-party election; defeat by Harwood–Bell; concession, later office, and factional career remain open.

### Konrad Elias Voss

**Identity and role:** American son of a neglected Sänger–Bredt engineer; founder of Frontier Orbital Lines in 1985 and principal builder of commercial orbital transport.

**Personality:** Operational, unsentimental, opportunistic without being reckless, and unusually respectful of maintenance crews. Voss dislikes prestige programs that treat reliable freight as beneath visionary leadership.

**Formative experiences:** His household preserves both technical fascination with reusable aerospace craft and resentment toward institutions that celebrate concepts while forgetting the engineers who failed to receive credit. Work around government contractors teaches him that surplus hardware, certification knowledge, and dependable schedules can be more valuable than a revolutionary vehicle.

**Politics and goals:** Commercial access under strict safety law; reusable transport; multiple paying mission classes; private ownership without private sovereignty in orbit; and gradual vehicle specialization. He accepts emergency requisition and military traffic control as the price of legitimacy.

**Relationships:** Aerospace Force officers initially treat him as a buyer of castoffs, then as an indispensable carrier. Reagan's legislation makes the company possible; the Open Door Depression makes outsourcing necessary; Harwood's 1993 charter gives Frontier privilege while threatening political dependence. Voss's relationship with Columbia crews is built on service reliability rather than ideology.

**Key events:** Founds Frontier with two OV-3A Liberty aircraft and an OV-3B freighter; develops Courier, Caravan, and Wayfarer; survives the 1990 crash; receives the National Orbital Carrier charter in 1993; begins Project Switchback; attaches the Frontier Annex to Columbia in 1994–96.

## British postwar generation

### David Ashcombe

**Identity and role:** Born about 1950; engineer and aerospace/procurement administrator turned politician. Under Thatcher he is associated with industrial reconstitution, Commonwealth supply, aerospace, and wartime production. He succeeds Thatcher in late 1987 and seeks a mandate in early 1988.

**Personality:** The **Quiet Engineer**—calm, precise, detail-fixated, difficult to provoke, reassuring after years of war, and almost incapable of sounding inspirational. His characteristic question is: “What happens when the first assumption fails?”

**Goals:**

- regularize rather than reverse Thatcher's reforms;
- match every independent commitment with logistics;
- preserve irreplaceable capacity while returning operation to commercial hands;
- rebuild reserves, infrastructure, technical education, and contractual Commonwealth coordination;
- reduce isolation through precise cooperation without surrendering strategic ownership.

**Relationships:** Thatcher's quiet preferred successor; compromise acceptable to market liberals, Commonwealth conservatives, and moderates. Principal opponent is Martin Calder. Rooke challenges from the market wing; Halden from the sovereignty wing. Reagan is a partner in the Halifax Understanding, not a leader Ashcombe intends Britain to follow.

**Key events:** Falklands production/logistics management; wins the second-ballot leadership contest in 1987; **Build the Peace** election in 1988; develops Responsible Independence and negotiates Halifax.

### Martin Calder

**Identity and role:** Born about 1946; northern industrial economist or lawyer, former junior industrial minister, and post-Healey Labour leader.

**Personality:** Warm, combative, emotionally fluent, industrially literate, and more capable than Ashcombe of translating administrative change into human consequence.

**Goal:** Accept commercial discipline, mixed ownership, strategic defense, and the Commonwealth while giving workers and regions security during conversion.

**Relationships:** Supports resistance to Argentina but condemns Second Torch. Defeats union-restoration and anti-imperial Labour factions. His principal contrast with Ashcombe is moral emphasis: Ashcombe counts restored jobs; Calder asks who survives the winter before they return.

**Key events:** Rise during Healey's later period; Falklands stance; Labour succession; principal opposition campaign in the 1988 Peace Election.

### Sir Julian Rooke

**Identity and role:** Chancellor or former Chancellor and leader of the National Liberal–Constitutional Union's market-radical wing.

**Personality:** Intelligent, disciplined, respected by business, doctrinaire, and politically colder than Ashcombe.

**Goal:** Complete liberalization through faster asset sales, wider foreign investment, fewer strategic designations, freer finance, reduced Commonwealth preference, and closer economic integration with Reagan's America.

**Relationships:** Leadership rival to Ashcombe and favorite of financial markets. Believes the market should decide which capacities survive; Ashcombe insists the state must first decide which capabilities it cannot safely lose.

**Key event:** Loses the 1987 leadership contest but remains the natural challenger whenever growth slows.

### Matthew Halden

**Identity and role:** Younger Powellite and Crown-and-Commonwealth leader inside the governing party.

**Personality:** Patriotic, suspicious, strategically minded, culturally conservative, and highly sensitive to dependency concealed as cooperation.

**Goal:** Maintain large defense reserves, imperial-route bases, Canadian and Australian ties, strategic ownership, immigration restriction, and an independent British military-space program.

**Relationships:** Distrusts Reagan and fears that American docking, intelligence, and market access will dissolve British sovereignty. His supporters transfer to Ashcombe in the leadership contest, making him indispensable to the governing right but not master of foreign policy.

**Key event:** 1987 leadership contest and subsequent pressure on the Halifax negotiations.
